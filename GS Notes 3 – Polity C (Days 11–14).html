Unit 9 · Political Ideologies – Part II (PDF p. 177–196)

9.1 Anarchism

  • Etymology: Greek anarchos = "without authority". Self-governed societies based on man's innate co-operative instincts; abolish State, property, religion; society of voluntary associations.
  • Thinkers: Pierre Joseph Proudhon (one of the earliest anarchists; book: "famously described profit as theft" – popular form "Property is theft"); Peter Kropotkin (Russian thinker – "Where there is authority, there is no freedom"); Leo Tolstoy (Russian author); Bakunin (if there is a State there is domination of one class by another – all States must vanish); Gerard Casey (21st c.: all states are criminal organisations).
  • State = unnecessary evil (negative liberals: necessary evil). Anarchy ≠ chaos/lawlessness – "society without rulers, not without rules"; principles of self-determination and self-ownership. Also opposes family and religion; some equate property with tyranny.
  • vs Marxism: Marxism – State withers away after socialism; Anarchism – immediate destruction of State.
  • Kinds: philosophical, socialist, revolutionary, libertarian (libertarian anarchism only restricts State to minimum).
  • Criticism: too optimistic about human nature; without State – terrorism, cybercrime, pollution, nuclear weapons with extremists.

9.2 Feminism – movements/ideologies for women's empowerment and equality with men; emerged in last decades of 19th c. "Feminism is the radical notion that women are people" (Cheris Kramarae). Five schools:

Post-Colonial1980s, Asia & Africa. Audre Lorde – essay "The Master's Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master's House". Also Gayatri Spivak, Chandra Talpade Mohanty, Ethel Crowley. Women face double colonisation (colonialism + patriarchy). Most apt for India, Namibia.

Indian State & women empowerment

  • 73rd & 74th Amendments – one-third seats for women in panchayats & urban local bodies.
  • Vishakha Guidelines (Supreme Court) → Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act, 2013 – Internal Complaints Committee & Local Complaints Committee.
  • Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005.
  • Long-standing demand: one-third reservation in Parliament & State Legislatures. (extra) Fulfilled by the 106th Amendment – Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam, 2023 (operative after delimitation following the next census).

9.3 Communitarianism (1980s)

  • Michael Sandel – "Liberalism and the Limits of Justice" (criticised libertarianism & John Rawls). Others: Alasdair MacIntyre, Michael Walzer, Charles Taylor, Amitai Etzioni, Will Kymlicka. Word "communitarian" coined by British socialist Goodwyn Barmy in 1840s.
  • Revolt against libertarianism (too individual) and Marxism (class-based). Man = "situated self" (rooted in community), not "unencumbered self".
  • State = positive instrument for common good; common good has priority over individual rights; positive rights (State-subsidised education, housing, safe environment, universal healthcare); Responsive Communitarianism = synthesis of rights and responsibilities.
  • Justice is particularistic (differs society to society) – rejects Rawls's universalism. Criticism: may lead to collectivism/authoritarianism.
  • Robert D. Putnam – study of Bowling in America: decline of social capital (TV, internet) → weaker democracy.

9.4 Post-Modernism

  • Critique of modernity (rationality, universalism, reductionism). Proponents: Nietzsche, Jean-Francois Lyotard, Michel Foucault, Jacques Derrida.
  • Friedrich Nietzsche = Father of Post-Modernism; "God is dead"; "There are no facts, only interpretations".
  • Truth is plural; particularism (parts more important than whole); linked to identity politics ("we" vs "they").
  • Jacques Derrida (French) – deconstruction ("rules for reading, interpretation and writing").
  • Alan Kirby (British) – post-modernism is dead; era of digi-modernism.

9.5 Environmentalism

  • Shallow Ecology – anthropocentric, utilitarian, instrumental value of nature; technological solutions; 3 Rs: reduce, reuse, recycle; supporter Anthony Weston (American).
  • Deep Ecology – term coined by Norwegian Arne Naess in 1973 (influenced by Rachel Carson & Gandhi); biocentric equality, intrinsic value of all life; limit population; reduce ecological footprint. Eight tenets (e.g. quality of life > standard of living). Quotes: Aldo Leopold "Humans are part of the environment and not conquerors of it"; Gandhi "enough for everyone's need but not for anyone's greed".
  • Andrew Heywood – 3 kinds in political theory: Social, Modernist, Deep ecology.
  • Social Ecology – term by Murray Bookchin (American). Trends: Eco-socialism (Rudolph Bahro – "From Red to Green"; capitalism is enemy), Eco-anarchism (Murray Bookchin; authority is enemy), Eco-feminism (Carolyn Merchant – "The Death of Nature"; patriarchy is enemy).
  • Modernist Ecology = shallow ecology; "enlightened anthropocentrism"; inter-generational justice; sustainable development ("getting richer slower").

Unit 10 · Public Opinion and Party System (PDF p. 197–210)

  • Public opinion (பொதுக் கருத்து) – collective views/preferences of people on government and politics; an organised, considered opinion of section(s) of people on a public issue – not an individual, private or expert opinion. Essential for democracy; "In a democracy public opinion is harnessed through political parties."
  • Hindrances to genuine public opinion: selfish interests ("me above nation"), illiteracy, poverty, racist/caste discrimination, lack of freedom of speech and independent media.
  • Political party – group of people who come together to contest elections and hold power; definite ideology and programme. Functions: contest elections, put forward policies, make laws, form/run government, act as opposition, shape public opinion, give access to welfare schemes; training ground for leaders.
Party systemFeatures / examples
One-partyLess participation, weak accountability. China (CPC), Singapore (People's Action Party), North Korea (Korean Workers Party), Vietnam (CPV), Cuba. 1920s fascists – Hitler (Germany), Mussolini (Italy), General Franco (Spain).
Two-party (bi-party)Greater accountability ("Damocles sword" of opposition); polarisation. USA – Democrats & Republicans; UK – Conservatives & Liberals (no constitutional limit on number of parties). Perpetuated by winner-take-all, "wasted vote" theory.
Multi-partyCoalitions; proportional representation in France & Italy → coalition cabinets. Canada, France, Germany, India, Sweden.

Modern party systems

  • UK: Tories (Conservatives) and Whigs (Liberals) – arose over monarchy; Labour eclipsed Liberals in 20th c.
  • USA: under George Washington – Federalist Party (strong national govt) vs Democratic Republican Party (state autonomy); renamed Democratic Party in 1828; Republican Party 1854 (anti-slavery; Abraham Lincoln).
  • Europe: parties after French Revolution (1789) – Conservatives, Liberals, Christian Democrats; later social-democratic/labour parties. Bolshevik Party – Communist Revolution 1917 → USSR; COMINTERN (Communist International) to spread revolution.
  • South Africa: parties first formed for decolonisation; Constitution – universal adult suffrage at 18, common voters roll, multi-party system.

Party system in India

  • INC 1885 – founded by A.O. Hume; after Non-Cooperation (1921–23) demanded complete freedom; split Moderates/Extremists after Partition of Bengal 1905.
  • Muslim League 1906; Hindu Mahasabha 1916; South Indian Liberal Federation (Justice Party) – Madras Presidency, non-Brahmin interests.
  • Swaraj Party 1922 – Chittaranjan Das; Congress Socialist Party 1934 – Acharya Narendra Dev & Jayaprakash Narayan; Communist Party – 1920s, efforts of M.N. Roy.
  • Till 1977 opposition: Communists, Socialists, Jana Sangh, Swatantra Party. Janata Party (under JP) won power at Centre in 1977; shattered after 1980; Jana Sangh revived as BJP; Kanshi Ram – Bahujan Samaj Party (social justice). CPI(M) strong in Kerala, West Bengal, Tripura.
  • 1990s – coalition era; federalisation of politics; regional parties: Shiromani Akali Dal (Punjab), Samajwadi Party (UP), TDP (AP), RJD (Bihar), Trinamool (WB), TRS (Telangana), Asom Gana Parishad (Assam), Shiv Sena (Maharashtra), National Conference & PDP (J&K), DMK & AIADMK (TN).
  • Tamil Nadu – early Congress dominance; Dravidian movement under Periyar E.V. Ramasamy (anti-North, anti-Hindi, anti-Brahmin); TN = pioneer model of pre-eminence of regional parties; Dravidian parties in power for the past 52 years (as per book).
  • Party symbols (exercise): Congress – Hand; BJP – Lotus; BSP – Elephant.
  • Rightist = conservative, traditional, capitalistic; Leftist = pro-equality, labour interests.
  • Opposition: Leader of Opposition enjoys rank & privileges of a Cabinet Minister; weak opposition → unaccountable govt / tyranny of majority.
  • Pressure / interest groups – voluntary membership, lobby for specific interests, do not contest elections (business, trade unions, farmers, teachers, students, doctors).
  • Psephology – study and statistical analysis of elections and polls. Opinion poll = before voting; exit poll = after voter casts vote (more trustworthy).
  • Glossary: By-election (seat vacant between general elections); Elector; Electoral roll (kept by Registrar of Electors); Coalition; Majority government; Election Commission (independent body for Parliament & Assembly elections).

Unit 11 · Election and Representation (PDF p. 211–231)

11.1 Franchise and representation

  • Franchise = right to vote in public elections; Anglo-French word, from Franc = FREE. Election = process of voting to choose a representative. Representation = speaking/acting on behalf of someone.
  • Part XV (15), Articles 324–329 = "Elections". Art. 324 – single Election Commission. Art. 326 – elections to Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha on basis of adult suffrage.
  • Elections used in Ancient Athens, Rome, selection of Popes & Holy Roman Emperors; modern elections arose with representative govt in Europe & North America from the 17th century.
  • What makes an election democratic: one person one vote, equal value; real choice of parties/candidates; regular intervals; candidate preferred by people wins; free and fair conduct.
  • Psephology = study of results and statistics of elections.

  • General election – all constituencies at the same time after 5-year term; By-election – one constituency, vacancy by death/resignation.
  • Constituencies: Lok Sabha – 543; Tamil Nadu elects 39 Lok Sabha MPs; each parliamentary constituency contains several assembly constituencies; villages/towns divided into wards.
  • Reserved constituencies for SC and ST (later extended to other weaker sections at district/local levels).
  • Delimitation on basis of census + physical features, administrative boundaries, communication, convenience. Delimitation Acts: 1952 (first), 1963, 1973, 2002.

  • Nomination: candidate minimum age 25 (voter 18); party nomination = "ticket"; security deposit; SC-directed declaration of criminal cases, assets & liabilities (self + family), educational qualification.
  • Campaign: about two weeks between final list of candidates and polling. Election law bans bribing/threatening voters, appeal to caste/religion, use of govt resources (election can be set aside by court). Model Code of Conduct: no places of worship for propaganda; no govt vehicles/aircraft/officials; after announcement Ministers shall not lay foundation stones, take big policy decisions or promise public facilities.

11.3 Free and fair elections – challenges: money power, criminal candidates, family domination of parties, little choice between major parties, disadvantage to small parties/independents. Voter's right to know about candidates flows from Art. 19(1)(a) (voting = expression).

First General Election (box): 25 Oct 1951 – 21 Feb 1952 (~4 months); Congress 364 of 489 seats, 45% votes; CPI second with 16 seats; turnout 67.6%; 54 parties; 26 states, 401 constituencies; Nehru first PM.

TN Assembly elections (table): 1st 1952 (Jan, 9 days of polling) … 1967, 1971 (3 days each) … 1977 & 1980 (2 days) … from 1984 (24 Dec) mostly single-day polls; 1991 (16 June), 2001 (10 May), 2006 (8 May), 2011 (13 Apr), 2016 (16 May), 16th Assembly 2021 (6 April, 1 day). (Book misprints the 9th election date as "1969 Jan 21" – it is 1989.)

11.4 Election Commission of India (Art. 324) – superintendence, direction & control of elections to Parliament, State Legislatures, President and Vice-President; permanent, independent body.

  • Structure: multi-member – Chief Election Commissioner + two Election Commissioners; appointed by the President; term 6 years or 65 years of age (whichever earlier); CEC removed like a SC judge (impeachment-like process); other ECs removed on recommendation of CEC.
  • Functions: prepare/revise electoral rolls; allot seats; conduct elections; recognise parties; allot symbols (decides symbol disputes on party split); advise President/Governor on disqualification of members; ceiling on election expenses; collect asset affidavits; disqualify candidate not filing election returns; notify that House is duly constituted.
  • SC landmark verdicts (box): 10 July 2013 – convicted MPs/MLAs/MLCs disqualified immediately; Section 8(4) of RP Act (3-month appeal window) struck down. 11 July 2013 – person in jail/police custody cannot contest (book).
  • Election machinery: Chief Electoral Officer (state/UT; nominated by ECI in consultation with state govt) → District Election Officer → Returning Officer (conducts election in a constituency; + Assistant ROs) → Electoral Registration Officer (prepares rolls) → Presiding Officer with Polling Officers (appointed by DEO; in UTs by RO) → Election Observers (general & expenditure; report directly to ECI).

11.5 Electoral reforms – committees (chart): Tarkunde Committee 1974, Dinesh Goswami Committee 1990, Vohra Committee 1993, Indrajit Gupta Committee 1998, Law Commission Report 1999, Venkatachaliah National Commission 2002, Election Commission of India Report 2004, Veerappa Moily Commission 2007, Tanka Committee 2010.

Reforms introduced

  • Voting age 21 → 18 by 61st Amendment Act, 1988.
  • Roll staff on deputation to ECI; proposers = 10% of electors or 10 electors, whichever less.
  • 1989 – use of EVMs provided; adjournment of poll for booth capturing.
  • Candidate lists in order: recognised parties, registered-unrecognised parties, independents.
  • Liquor sale banned in polling area for 48 hours before close of poll; on death of a candidate the party may name another within 7 days; paid holiday on polling day; prohibition of arms.
  • 1998 – staff of local bodies, nationalised banks, universities, LIC, PSUs can be requisitioned. 1999 – postal ballot for certain classes.
  • 2003 – proxy voting for service voters (armed forces); ECI order on declaring criminal antecedents & assets; Rajya Sabha: domicile requirement removed, open ballot introduced; travel expenses exempted from election expenses.
  • 2009 – restrictions on exit polls; 3-month limit for submitting cases of corrupt practice for disqualification. Increased security deposit; appellate authorities within district.
  • 2010 – voting rights to Indian citizens living abroad. 2011 – election expenditure ceiling raised.

Anti-Defection Law

  • 52nd Amendment Act, 1985 – added Tenth Schedule. 91st Amendment Act, 2003 – disqualification no longer excused for a "split" (only merger exception remains).
  • Disqualified: party member who voluntarily gives up membership or votes/abstains against party whip without prior permission; independent who joins a party; nominated member who joins a party after 6 months of taking seat.
  • Exceptions: merger of parties; Presiding Officer giving up / rejoining party.
  • Deciding authority = Presiding Officer of the House; his rules placed before House for 30 days; wilful breach treated as breach of privilege.
  • Evaluation: prevents defection for office/money, stability, allows realignment by merger, gives constitutional recognition to parties.
  • Why India adopted FPTP: simplicity, clear choice of candidate & party, local accountability, bonus seats for largest party → stable government, encourages social groups to combine.
  • Rajya Sabha: elected by State MLAs by single transferable vote (preferences). Quota = [Total votes ÷ (seats + 1)] + 1. Example: 4 seats, 200 TN MLAs → 200/5 + 1 = 41 votes. Lowest candidate eliminated and votes transferred until seats filled.

11.6 State funding – Indrajit Gupta Committee on State Funding of Elections, 1998: backed state funding; only national & state parties with symbol (not independents); short-term funding in kind only; separate Election Fund – ₹600 crore/year by Centre + matching amount by States; parties must have filed income-tax returns.

11.7 Role of youth – NOTA

  • NOTA (None Of The Above) – Supreme Court upheld right to reject all candidates; directed ECI to put NOTA (last option) on EVMs. (extra) Judgment: PUCL case, Sept 2013.
  • Earlier "negative voting" – Rule 49(O), Conduct of Election Rules 1961; voter entered serial number in Form 17A.
  • NOTA countries: Colombia, Ukraine, Brazil, Bangladesh, Finland, Spain, Sweden, Chile, France, Belgium, Greece; USA in some cases (Texas since 1975).
  • Open ballot = raising hands; Secret ballot = vote cast without anyone's knowledge (ballot box / EVM).
  • Glossary: Plurality (more votes than any other, not absolute majority); Delimitation; Voters' turnout (% of eligible voters who voted); Anti-defection law (1985); Factionalism.

Unit 12 · Local Governments (PDF p. 232–253)

12.1 Meaning – govt managing local services (roads, water, street lights, birth/death, residence/income certificates); lowest unit of administration; council elected every 5 (or 4) years; "grassroots" channel; ICT has made the world a global village.

  • Gandhi vs Ambedkar: Gandhi – Gram Swaraj ("a complete republic, independent of its neighbours for its own vital wants…"); "The greater the power of the people, the better for the people." Ambedkar – village "a sink of localism, a den of ignorance and communalism"; draft Constitution did not mention Panchayati Raj.
  • Gandhians got Article 40 (DPSP, Part IV): State shall organise village panchayats as units of self-government.

12.2 Classification – Rural (agriculture-based) vs Urban (industry/trade). Urban: Municipal Corporations (1 million+), Municipalities (<1 million; Class I, II…), Townships/Notified Area Committees (new industrial areas), Cantonment Boards (military). Rural: District Panchayat, Panchayat Union, Village Panchayat, Ward. Box "Towards Total Sanitation": ILCS scheme (eradicate manual scavenging by 2010), JNNURM, Rajiv Awas Yojana 2011.

12.3 Around the world – developed (European) countries decentralising; developing countries centralising; India – pressure for more devolution.

12.4 Origin and development in India

  • 600 BCE–600 CE rise of republics; Jain & Buddhist literature classify villages; Kautilya's Arthashastra – village administration; Mauryas – village and district units.
  • Cholas – well-developed local self-govt. Uthiramerur inscriptions (Kanchipuram dist.) of Parantaka Chola I (919–922 CE): assemblies – Ur, Mahasabha, Nagaram (mercantile towns), Nadu. Rajaraja Chola I ordered the mahasabha of Viranarayana Chaturvedimangalam to confiscate traitors' property. Mahasabhas and Kudavolai confined to Brahmin settlements. Historians Charles Metcalfe, George Birdwood, Elphinstone praised ancient local govt.
  • Mughals (1500–1777): Subhas (provinces) → Sarkars → Paraganas (union of villages).
  • British: after Plassey 1757 – Diwani rights; Municipal Corporations in Presidency towns Calcutta, Bombay, Madras; District Collector = kingpin.
  • Lord Ripon's Resolution 1882 – rural local boards (sub-division/taluk) & district boards; non-official elected majority; ~500 rural boards with two-thirds non-officials. Ripon = "Father of Local Self-Government in India" (evaluation Q). District boards' work till 1909: police, public works, education, sanitation.
  • 1907 – Royal Commission on decentralisation (relations between GoI, provinces, local bodies). GoI Act 1919 – local govt a transferred subject under dyarchy (1919–35).
  • Tamil Nadu: village bodies 1,417 (1926) → 6,250 (1937); three tiers – District Boards, Taluk Boards, Village Boards; by 1923 non-official chairmen; District Boards dominated by Justice Party (545 members in 1927).
  • Shriman Narayan – Gandhian Constitution for Free India (panchayats as basic units).
  • Community Development Programme 1952; National Extension Service; Second FYP (1956–61).

12.5 73rd Amendment (Panchayat Raj Act)

  • Art. 243B – three-tier system (village, intermediate, district) in all States except those with population not exceeding 20 lakh (no intermediate tier). Village/intermediate areas notified by Governor.
  • Eleventh Schedule (Art. 243G) – 29 subjects (agriculture, land improvement, minor irrigation, animal husbandry, fisheries, social forestry, minor forest produce, small-scale industries, khadi, rural housing, drinking water, fuel & fodder, roads, rural electrification, non-conventional energy, poverty alleviation, education, technical training, adult education, libraries, cultural activities, markets & fairs, health & sanitation, family welfare, women & child development, social welfare, weaker sections/SC/ST, PDS, community assets).
  • All seats by direct election; electorate = Gram Sabha (all on village electoral roll). Chairpersons as per State law; MPs/MLAs represented above village level.
  • Reservation for SC/ST; not less than one-third seats for women; tenure 5 years; qualification = eligible for State legislature; State Finance Commission; State Election Commission.
  • About 2,50,000 village panchayats in India (2011 Census).
  • TN three tiers: District Panchayats (elected + nominated) → Panchayat Union Councils → Village Panchayats; Gram Sabha (all voters, advisory).
  • Gram Sabha meets 6 times a year: 26 Jan (Republic Day), 22 Mar (World Water Day), 1 May (May Day), 15 Aug, 2 Oct, 1 Nov (Local Governance Day – Ullatchi Naal).

12.6 74th Amendment (Nagarpalika) – notified June 1993; States given one year to amend laws (e.g., Punjab Municipal Bill 1998 replaced 1911 Act). Cantonment Boards under Central Cantonments Act, 1924 (glossary: Cantonments Act 2006, Ministry of Defence).

12.7 Tamil Nadu

  • TN Panchayats Act 1994 (replaced 1958 Act), amended 1996 (District Planning Committee, State Election Commission, State Finance Commission). First elections under the 1994 Act: 1,17,000 representatives. Local body elections 1996, 2001, 2006, 2011.
  • TN: 12,620 village panchayats, 385 panchayat unions, 38 district panchayats; urban – 21 Municipal Corporations, 152 Municipalities, 561 Town Panchayats (book figures).
  • TN Municipal Laws (Fourth Amendment) Bill 2018 & TN Panchayats (Second Amendment) Bill 2018 – special officers' tenure extended 6 months.
  • Five systemic challenges of cities (ASICS): weak spatial planning; weak finances; poor HR; powerless mayors & fragmented governance; no citizen participation/transparency.

12.8 Contemporary issues – expanding responsibilities need reclassification of lists; village courts/policing; overlap with State departments; only 18 States ratified (book).

Unit 15 · Tamil Nadu Political Thought (PDF p. 290–309; Key Terms glossary p. 310–319)

15.1 Historical background

  • Strabo (63 BCE – c. 24 CE), Greek geographer – early diplomatic history of the Pandyas. Naval expeditions of Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas, Pallavas.
  • Trade with Mediterranean & SE Asia 200 BCE – 300 CE; ancient ports: Korkai, Poompuhar, Vasavasamudram, Perimula, Arikamedu, Alagankulam, Mamallapuram.
  • Sangam literature (~300 BCE – 300 CE): akam (love) & puram (war, justice, kingdom). Ettuthogai (8 anthologies): Natrinai, Kurunthogai, Aingurunooru, Pathitrupathu, Paripadal, Kalithogai, Agananooru, Purananooru; + Pathupattu (Ten Idylls); early part of Tholkappiyam. Post-Sangam: Pathinen Kilkanakku (18 minor works, incl. Thirukkural), epics, five minor epics. Prabandha forms: kovai (Pandikkovai), kalambakam (Nandikkalambakam), parani (Kalingattupparani).
  • Kharavela of Kalinga (165 BCE) inscription – destroyed a "Tamil federation" that existed 113 years; Agananooru also mentions a Tamil federation → Tamil nationalism has Sangam roots.
  • Tamizhagam: from Tirupati hills to Kanyakumari; five tinai – kurinji (hills), mullai (forest), marudam (fertile plains), neidhal (coast), palai (arid).
  • Capitals: Cholas – Uraiyur; Pandyas – Madurai; Cheras – Kongunadu & Kerala; Pallavas – Kanchipuram. Crowned kings = vendhar; chieftains = vel/velir; Seven patrons (Kapilar, Avvaiyar): Aay, Pari, Ori, Malayan, Elini, Pegan, Nalli.
  • Sabhai / mandram = highest court, presided by the king; every village had a mandram. Hero (memorial) stones; wound in the back = disgrace. Purananooru: Pandyan king warned women, children, sick, elderly and cattle to move to safety before attack. King's legitimacy rested on people's consent.
  • Pattinappaalai – taxes: customs, income tax, toll. Pandya officers: pearl fisheries (kalatika), chief scribe (kanatikan). Emblems: Chola – tiger; Chera – bow & arrow; Pandya – fish; Pallava – lion. Spies essential.
  • Caste (jati) unknown in Sangam society; class by occupation – kudi: tudiyan, panan, kadamban; arasar, vaishyar, velalar. Folk religion – Murugan (Seyon). Inscriptions in Tamil + Sanskrit only after 6th c. CE; Sanskritisation; Pallavas claimed descent from Brahma (divine, hereditary kingship).
  • Avvaiyar = title ("respectable woman") held by 4–6 women. Sangam Avvaiyar (1st–2nd c. CE) – patron Athiyaman Neduman Anchi of Tagadur; diplomat – averted Thondaiman (Kanchi) attack by ironic praise of his unused weapons. Chola-age Avvaiyar (~10th c.) – Aathichoodi, Konrai Vendhan (young children), Moodurai, Nalvazhi (older children).
  • TN political ideologies: Thiruvalluvar (ancient), Bharathiyar (nationalist), Singaravelar (communist), Periyar (Dravidian – social equality).

15.2 Thiruvalluvar – ancient political ideas

  • Arathupal – moralist; Inbathupal – poet; Porutpal – political philosopher.
  • Porutpal = 7 parts, 70 chapters: Politics/royalty (அரசியல்) 25, Ministry (அமைச்சியல்) 10, Defence/fort (அரணியல்) 2, Wealth (கூழியல்) 1, Army (படையியல்) 2, Friendship (நட்பியல்) 17, Citizens (குடியியல்) 13. (Book wrongly writes "70 verses".)
  • Kural 381 – six essentials of a state: "படைகுடி கூழமைச்சு நட்பரண் ஆறும் உடையான் அரசருள் ஏறு" (army, people, wealth, minister, friends, fort).
  • King described in 25 chapters – from Iraimatchi (ch. 39) to Idukkan Azhiyamai (ch. 63); king mentioned 46 times (Arasar, Vendhar, Nilan Aandavar, Mannavar).
  • Welfare state – Kural 388: king who rules justly and protects is deemed God ("முறைசெய்து காப்பாற்றும் மன்னவன் மக்கட்கு இறையென்று வைக்கப் படும்").
  • Features of state – Kural 384 (virtue, removing vice, courage, honour).
  • Ideal rule – Kural 542 ("வானோக்கி வாழும் உலகெல்லாம் மன்னவன் கோல்நோக்கி வாழுங் குடி" – as the world looks to rain, subjects look to the king's sceptre); Kural 544.
  • Against tyranny – chapter Kodungonmai (cruel sceptre); Kural 555: tears of suffering people are a weapon that destroys the king's wealth.
  • Budget – Kural 385: Iyatral (finding sources of income), Eettal (collecting), Kaathal (protecting), Vaguthal (allocating for welfare).
  • Thirukkural = secular work (no religion named); Kural 972 – all are equal by birth ("பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும்…") – unlike Manu, Plato, Aristotle.

15.3 Nationalism – Subramaniya Bharathiyar (1882–1921)

  • Born 11 Dec 1882, Ettayapuram (book: Tirunelveli district); childhood name Subbiah; parents Chinnaswamy & Lakshmi Ammal; title "Bharathi" at age 11 after winning debate on "Education" at Ettayapuram Durbar. Married Chellammal (June 1897). Two years in Benares (aunt Kuppammal, Krishna Sivan); passed Allahabad University Entrance; adopted moustache and Sikh-style turban.
  • Journalist: sub-editor Swadesamitran (Nov 1904); weekly "India" (May 1906) – motto Liberty, Equality, Fraternity; printed on red paper; first Tamil paper with political cartoons; also Vijaya (Tamil daily), Bala Bharatha (English monthly), Suryothayam (Pondicherry weekly).
  • Swadesa Geethangal (1908); Swaraj Day meeting, Madras 1908 (poems Vande Matharam, Enthaiyum Thaayum, Jaya Bharath distributed). Arrest warrant → Pondicherry 1908 (~10 years) with Aurobindo, Lajpat Rai, V.V. Subramaniam; re-entered British India near Cuddalore, Nov 1918 – arrested; met Gandhi 1919 at Rajaji's home.
  • Associates: V.O. Chidambaram, Subramania Siva, Mandayam Thirumalachariar, Srinivasachari; attended INC Benares 1905 and Surat 1907; extremist leaders Tilak, B.C. Pal.
  • Works: Kannan Pattu, Nilavum Vanminum Katrum, Panchali Sabatham (India = Draupadi, British = Kauravas, freedom fighters = Pandavas), Kuyil Pattu.
  • Social reformer: only two castes – men and women; removed sacred thread; temple entry for SCs; against child marriage & dowry; for widow remarriage; meeting with Sister Nivedita → women's rights (Shakthi symbol). Quote: "World will prosper in knowledge and intellect, if both men and women are deemed equal."
  • Died 11 Sept 1921; only 14 people attended his funeral.

15.4 Communism – M. Singaravelar (1860–1946) – "first communist of South India"

  • Organised labour unions in Madras Presidency (with Thiru. Vi. Ka and Varadarajulu); wrote in Swadesamitran, The Hindu, Navasakthi.
  • 1922 Congress Committee – demanded economic freedom of workers & farmers; INC resolution 1922 → six-member committee incl. Singaravelar. Labour-protection law 1926 ((extra) Trade Unions Act 1926). 1918 – resolution on use of vernacular at Madras Presidency Association conference (with Periyar).
  • 1 May 1923 – organised first May Day celebration in India at Chennai and announced the Labour-Kisan (labour-farmer) party – demands: 8-hour work, right to unionise & strike, minimum wages, PF, maternity leave, free water for farming, abolition of zamindari, voting rights for all.
  • 1925 – headed first socialist/communist conference at Kanpur; socialism without violence; socialist bloc inside Congress.
  • Supported Periyar; after Periyar's USSR visit Self-Respect became "Self-Respect Social Justice Party" (book); wrote in Periyar's magazine against superstition. Founded a Buddhist association (1880s); member of Chennai Municipality.

15.5 Dravidian ideology – Periyar E.V. Ramasamy (1879–1973)

  • Born 1879, Erode (affluent business family); chairman of Erode municipality; "Thanthai Periyar"; joined Congress (Gandhi), temple-entry movements; left Congress over Varnashrama practice → Justice Party, Self-Respect Movement. Died 1973.
  • Views: nationalism = exploitation by rich, an "unreal feeling"; spoke against nationalism in Sri Lanka; admired USSR's equality; India = land of diversities – Dravida Nadu, Andhra, Bengal as nations; no language should be imposed.
  • Slogan "Tamil Nadu for Tamils and Dravida Nadu for Dravidians"; later casteless society became top priority; "The caste feeling divided the Dravidian race and Dravidians became slaves for Aryans" (1950s).
  • Box: weddings without rituals, property & divorce rights for women, drop caste suffix, inter-dining with food cooked by SC persons at conferences (1930s).
  • Glossary: Pure Tamil Movement (freeing Tamil from Sanskrit words); Home Rule Movement (1916–18, Annie Besant); Self-Respect Movement; Welfare state.

C7 T1 · Political Parties (PDF p. 181–189)

  • India became democratic in 1950. Political party = voluntary association with common ideology seeking power through elections; components – leader, active members, followers; "backbone of democracy"; intermediaries between citizens and policy-makers. Manifesto = programme announced before election.
  • Book: party recognised if active 5 years and secured at least 6% votes in last general election. To form a party: register with ECI, party constitution, at least 100 members with voter IDs.
  • Party systems: Single – China, North Korea, Cuba; Bi-party – UK (Labour, Conservative), USA (Republican, Democratic); Multi-party – India, France, Sweden, Norway. India has the largest number of parties; party system from late 19th c.; three levels – National, Regional, Registered-unrecognised.
  • Election Commission – autonomous constitutional body, HQ New Delhi. Election Symbols Order 1968: reserved symbols (recognised parties) vs free symbols (unrecognised).   • Glossary: Election manifesto – தேர்தல் அறிக்கை; Opposition party – எதிர்க்கட்சி; Federal system – கூட்டாட்சி அமைப்பு; Election Commission – தேர்தல் ஆணையம்; Electoral symbols – தேர்தல் சின்னங்கள்.

C7 T3 · Market and Consumer Protection (PDF p. 194–201)

  • Market (economics) = arrangement where buyers and sellers meet, not a place. Product vs factor markets. By area – local, regional, national, international; by time – very short, short, long period; by transaction – spot (cash) vs future (credit); by regulation – regulated (stock market) vs unregulated.
  • Competition: perfect; imperfect – monopoly (single seller, no close substitute), monopolistic competition (term by Edward H. Chamberlin, Harvard, 1933, Theory of Monopolistic Competition), oligopoly (Greek oligoi = few; few sellers).
  • Consumer – buys for consideration (paid/promised/partly) for own use or self-employment; not a consumer if free of charge, for commercial purpose, or under contract of service.
  • Unfair trade practices: false representation, second-hand sold as new, misleading price/ads, gifts with no intention to give, hoarding, spurious goods; "goods once sold will not be taken back" has no legal weight. Regulators e.g. TRAI, IRDAI.
  • Eight consumer rights: basic needs, safety, information, choice, representation, redress, consumer education, healthy environment.
  • Consumer Protection Act 1986 (COPRA) – in force 24 Dec 1986 (= (extra) National Consumer Day); "Magna Carta" of consumer protection; consumer protection councils at national, state, district levels; three-tier redressal = three-tier system. 1986 limits: District up to ₹20 lakh; State ₹20 lakh – ₹1 crore; National above ₹1 crore. NCDRC set up 1988, New Delhi, headed by sitting/retired SC judge.
  • Consumer Protection Act 2019 (passed Aug 2019): covers e-commerce, teleshopping, direct selling, MLM; e-filing & video-conferencing; Central Consumer Protection Authority (CCPA) with investigation wing under a Director-General; misleading ad penalty up to ₹10 lakh + 2 years (repeat: ₹50 lakh + 5 years); endorser ban up to 1 year (repeat 3). Book limits: District ≤ ₹1 crore; State ₹1–10 crore; National > ₹10 crore. (extra) Act in force 20 July 2020; limits revised Dec 2021 to District ≤ ₹50 lakh, State ₹50 lakh–₹2 crore, National > ₹2 crore.
  • Acts: Essential Commodities Act 1955; Prevention of Black Marketing & Maintenance of Supplies of Essential Commodities Act 1980; Consumer Protection Act 1986; BIS Act 1986; Legal Metrology Act 2009. Adulteration = mixing inferior material.

C7 T3 · Road Safety (PDF p. 202–211)

  • India = world's largest contributor to road accidents; ~10% of world road-accident deaths.
  • Causes: distracted driving (leading cause – mobile), reckless driving, night driving, tailgating (too close behind), heavy traffic, poor roads, no helmet/seat-belt, impatient pedestrians, drunk driving, jumping signals, over-speeding, unmanned level crossings.
  • Licence at 18; no horn near hospital/school; residential speed ideal 20, limit 30 km/h; seat belts cut death risk by >60%; helmet cuts severe head injury by >70%; pedestrians have right of way at zebra crossing (Rule 11).
  • Law sections (book): speed – S.112; signs – S.119; helmet – S.129; dangerous driving / mobile – S.184; drunk driving – S.185; seat belt – CMV Rules 138(3). Motor Vehicles Act 1988 – in force 1989; Central Motor Vehicle Rules 1989; Rules of Road Regulations 1989.
  • Documents: driving licence, RC, insurance, tax, fitness certificate, permit. Raksha Safe Drive device (crash detection, GPS, panic button); carpooling; bollards at unmanned crossings; two-wheelers max 50 km/h suggestion.
  • Mumbai police video on Sachin Tendulkar's 45th birthday – helmet. Traffic signs = "silent conductors" (mandatory, cautionary, informatory). Road Safety Week – January every year.

Right to Information (RTI)

Textbook (C9 p. 270; C11 p. 67):

  • RTI Act enacted October 2005 – "revolutionary act" for transparency and accountability; any citizen can seek information from any government body (corporations, departments, govt schools, road authorities); copies of records, reports, papers.
  • Information within 30 days; if not, appeal to the Appellate Authority; penalty fee collected from the official. Application signed with name, address, date and sent by registered post; an illiterate person can ask the Public Information Officer to write it.
  • Exempted: personal information; defence & intelligence organisations – BSF, CRPF, Intelligence Bureau.
  • RTI activists: Aruna Roy, Nikhil Dey. Leader of Opposition sits on the panel that selects the Central Vigilance Commission and Information Commission (C9 p. 257).

(extra) Lokpal and Lokayuktas Act, 2013

  • Term "Lokpal" coined by L.M. Singhvi (1963); First ARC (1966, Morarji Desai) recommended Lokpal (Centre) & Lokayukta (States); model = Scandinavian Ombudsman (Sweden 1809). Anna Hazare's movement 2011.
  • Act: President's assent 1 Jan 2014, in force 16 Jan 2014.
  • Composition: Chairperson + up to 8 members; 50% judicial members; at least 50% members from SC/ST/OBC/minorities/women. Chairperson – former CJI / SC judge / eminent person.
  • Selection committee: PM (chair), Lok Sabha Speaker, Leader of Opposition (LS), CJI or SC judge nominated by him, an eminent jurist. Appointed by President. Term 5 years or age 70.
  • Jurisdiction: includes the PM (not on international relations, security, public order, atomic energy, space; inquiry needs 2/3 of full bench, in camera), Ministers, MPs, Group A–D officers. Has Inquiry and Prosecution wings; can superintend CBI in cases it refers.
  • First Lokpal: Justice Pinaki Chandra Ghose (March 2019); present: Justice A.M. Khanwilkar (2024).
  • States to set up Lokayuktas within 365 days. Maharashtra – first Lokayukta (1971). Tamil Nadu Lokayukta Act 2018; TN Lokayukta constituted 2019 (first chairperson Justice P. Devadass).
  • Central Vigilance Commission – set up 1964 on Santhanam Committee recommendation; statutory by CVC Act 2003; CVC + up to 2 Vigilance Commissioners; term 4 years or 65; selected by PM, Home Minister, LoP (LS). Prevention of Corruption Act 1988 amended 2018 (bribe-giving also an offence).

Textbook (C8 p. 197–201; C9 p. 261–271):
