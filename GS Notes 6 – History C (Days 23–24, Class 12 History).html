Unit 1 – Rise of Nationalism in India (PDF p. 5–19)

  • Nationalism (தேசியம்) = loyalty and devotion to a nation; placing one nation above others, promoting its culture and interests.
  • Gandhi returned from South Africa in 1915; led the national movement from 1919 → mass phase. Before him: Dadabhai Naoroji, G.K. Gokhale, Bipin Chandra Pal, Lala Lajpat Rai, B.G. Tilak = early nationalists.

1.1 Socio-economic background

  • New land tenures: British fixed land revenue in cash (earlier = share of actual crop), regardless of crop failure, price fall, drought/flood; sale of land for debt → moneylenders grabbed land. Two results: commodification of land (no private property in land before British) and commercialisation of agriculture; new class of absentee landlords; produce now for the market → peasant discontent.
  • Laissez faire (free trade, தடையில்லா வணிகக் கொள்கை) and de-industrialisation: raw cotton, jute, silk taken to Britain; machine goods flooded India → weavers, cotton dressers, carpenters, blacksmiths, shoemakers jobless. Farmers forced to grow indigo, cotton (cash crops) → food scarcity. Continued till WWI.
  • Indigo revolt 1859–60, Bengal: European planters forced tenants to grow indigo under unfair contracts/advances; peasants petitioned, then refused advances and new contracts → planters forced to withdraw from northern Bengal.
  • Famines: British extended laissez faire to famines (Indian rulers had given tax relief, fixed grain prices, banned exports). 1770–1900: 25 million Indians died in famines. William Digby (editor, Madras Times): 1793–1900 ≈ 5 million died in all wars of the world, but in just 10 years (1891–1900) 19 million died in Indian famines.
  • Orissa famine 1866: 1.5 million starved while British exported 200 million pounds of rice → prompted Naoroji's lifelong study of Indian poverty.
  • Madras famine 1876–78: two monsoons failed; Viceroy Lytton – hands-off approach; ≈ 3.5 million died in Madras Presidency.
  • Emigration / indentured labour (ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்): plantation crops (coffee, tea, sugar) in Ceylon, Mauritius, Fiji, Malaya, Caribbean, South Africa. 1815 – Governor of Ceylon asked Governor of Madras for "coolies"; Thanjavur collector said people attached to soil. Famines of 1833 and 1843 pushed Tamils to Ceylon coffee/tea estates. Abolition of slavery in British India 1843 aided emigration.
  • Tamil labourers in Ceylon: 1837 – 10,000; 1846 – 80,000; 1855 – 1,28,000; 1877 (famine year) – ≈3,80,000.
  • Mauritius: 1843 – 30,218 men + 4,307 women; by 1900 ≈ 5,00,000 Indians.
  • Indenture = penal contract for 5 years, return passage paid; worse than slavery; agents called kanganis tricked/kidnapped labourers. Ship Calcutta→Trinidad 1856–57 deaths: 12.3% men, 18.5% women, 28% boys, 36% girls, 55% infants.

1.2 Western education and its impact

  • Pre-British education: segmented by religion/caste; Brahmins monopolised higher learning in Vidyalayas and Chatuspathis; medium Sanskrit; technical skills hereditary; women and lower castes barred; rote learning.
  • General Committee of Public Instruction 1823 – split into Orientalists (vernacular education) vs Anglicists (Western education in English).
  • T.B. Macaulay – India's first Law Member of Governor-General's Council (1834–38); sided with Anglicists; 'Minute on Indian Education' 1835 – wanted a class "Indian in blood and colour, but English in taste, in opinion, in morals and in intellect".
  • English Education Act 1835 (drafted by Macaulay). Universities of Bombay, Madras, Calcutta – 1857.
  • British sneered at the educated middle class as the "Babu class"; it became the progressive intelligentsia that led the freedom struggle.
  • Educated middle class / "neo-social classes": traders, landlords, moneylenders, English-educated employees, lawyers, doctors. Leaders: Raja Ram Mohan Roy, Vidyasagar, Vivekananda, Aurobindo, Gokhale, Naoroji, Pherozeshah Mehta, Surendranath Banerjea. Influenced by Locke, J.S. Mill, Mazzini, Garibaldi, Rousseau, Thomas Paine, Marx. Three cherished rights: free press, free speech, association.
  • Missionaries: among the first to give modern secular education, esp. to underprivileged; attacked polytheism and caste; few converted, but their challenge triggered reform movements.

1.3 Social and religious reforms

  • Reformist: Brahmo Samaj (Raja Ram Mohan Roy, 1828), Prarthana Samaj (Dr Atmaram Pandurang, 1867), Aligarh Movement (Syed Ahmad Khan).
  • Revivalist: Arya Samaj (1875), Ramakrishna Mission, Deoband Movement.
  • Social movements: Jyotiba Phule (Pune), Narayana Guru and Ayyankali (Kerala), Ramalinga Adigal, Vaikunda (Swamigal), Iyothee Thassar (Tamil Nadu). Followers of Roy: Keshav Chandra Sen, Vidyasagar. Issues: abolition of sati, child marriage, widow remarriage.

1.4 Other decisive factors

  • Memories of 1857 – national movement "dates its birth" from 1857; un-avenged British atrocities. Elphinstone (Governor of Bombay) to John Lawrence on the siege of Delhi: "…we have indeed surpassed Nadirshah."
  • Racial discrimination: ICS age limit cut from 21 to 19 to keep Indians out; simultaneous exam in India refused.
  • Repressive/exploitative measures: Section 124A IPC (1870) – sedition ("exciting disaffection"); Vernacular Press Act 1878; abolition of customs duty on English cotton goods + excise on Indian cotton cloth; Ilbert Bill (Viceroy Ripon) – Indian judges to try Europeans – amended under European pressure.
  • Press: Ram Mohan Roy's Sambad Kaumudi (1821, Bengali) and Mirat-ul-Akbar (1822, Persian). Nationalist papers: Amrit Bazaar Patrika, Bombay Chronicle, The Tribune, Indian Mirror, The Hindu, Swadesamitran.
  • Glorious past: Orientalists William Jones, Charles Wilkins, Max Muller translated Sanskrit/Persian/Arabic texts. Aurobindo: mission of nationalism is "to recover Indian thought… from the Indian standpoint".

1.5 Birth of Indian associations

  • Madras Native Association (MNA) – 26 Feb 1852; inspired by Gajula Lakshminarasu (Madras businessman); landed and business classes. Petition Dec 1852 to Parliament (before Charter Act 1853): ryotwari and zamindari had ruined agriculturists; revive village system; slow judiciary, incompetent judges; objected to state funds for missionary schools (grants-in-aid). Discussed in Parliament March 1853.
  • H.D. Seymour, Chairman of Indian Reform Society, visited Madras Oct 1853 (Guntur, Cuddalore, Tiruchirappalli, Salem, Tirunelveli). After Charter Act 1853, MNA's 2nd petition (14,000 signatures) asked to end Company rule / transfer to Crown. Lakshminarasu died 1866; MNA ceased by 1881. Limited to Madras Presidency and elite viewpoint.
  • Madras Mahajana Sabha (MMS) – organised May 1884; inaugural meeting 16 May 1884: G. Subramaniam, Viraraghavachari, Ananda Charlu, Rangiah, Balaji Rao, Salem Ramaswamy. Merged with INC after its leaders attended INC's 1st session.
  • Agitations 1875–85: cotton import duty (1875), Indianisation of services (1877), against Vernacular Press Act (1878), for Ilbert Bill (1883).
  • INC: idea from meeting of the Theosophical Society, Madras, Dec 1884, presided by A.O. Hume (retired ICS). Founded 28 Dec 1885, Bombay; first president W.C. Bonnerjee; fewer than 100 members but all-India character. One-third of founding members were journalists.
  • INC demands – Constitutional: Indian representation in government; Economic: reduce land revenue, protect peasants from zamindars, heavy tax on imports for swadeshi goods; Administrative: simultaneous ICS exams in England and India; Judicial: separation of Executive and Judiciary.
  • INC sessions in Madras (map p. 13): 1887, 1894, 1898, 1903, 1908, 1914, 1927. No session in 1930, 1935, 1941–45. First session Bombay 1885; Calcutta 1886.

(d) Early nationalists (1885–1915) = "Moderates"

  • Elite (lawyers, teachers, doctors, journalists); method = petitions, prayers, memoranda ("constitutional methods"). Built the idea of "we as a nation" and an indigenous anti-colonial ideology.
  • Newspapers: Naoroji – Voice of India, Rast Goftar; Surendranath Banerjea – Bengalee; Tilak – Kesari, Mahratta.
  • Late 1890s: "Extremists" – Bipin Chandra Pal, Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai. 1897 Tilak: "Swaraj is my birth right and I shall have it". Tilak arrested 27 July 1897 under Sec. 124A.

1.6 Naoroji and Drain Theory

  • Dadabhai Naoroji = "Grand Old Man of Indian Nationalism"; Bombay Municipal Corporation (1870s); elected to British Parliament 1892; founded India Society (1865) and East India Association (1866) in London; INC president thrice.
  • Book: Poverty and Un-British Rule in India (1901) → drain of wealth: taxes from India spent for England. India exported avg £13 million/year (1835–72) with no return. Home Charges (profits to Company shareholders, guaranteed interest on railways, pensions, interest on war loans) = loss of £30 million a year.

Glossary: laissez faire – free trade; indentured labour – ஒப்பந்தத் தொழிலாளர்; Orientalist; Anglicist; proselytizing – மதம் மாற்றும் முயற்சி; polytheism – பல தெய்வ நம்பிக்கை; vernacular – சுதேச மொழி.

Unit 2 – Rise of Extremism and Swadeshi Movement (PDF p. 20–34)

  • Extremists (radicals/militants) attacked the moderates' "mendicant policy" (prayers and petitions). Leaders: Tilak (Maharashtra), Bipin Chandra Pal (Bengal), Lala Lajpat Rai (Punjab) = Lal–Bal–Pal triumvirate (முப்பெரும் தலைவர்கள்); also Aurobindo Ghose. Causes: factionalism in Congress, frustration with moderates, anger at Curzon's partition.
  • Swadeshi movement 1905–1911 = most important phase of pre-Gandhian era; for the first time women, workers, peasants, marginalised exposed to nationalism. Vernacular press grew: Swadesamitran (TN), Kesari (Maharashtra), Yugantar (Bengal).
  • Repressive Acts: Public Meetings Act 1907, Explosive Substances Act 1908, Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act 1908, Indian Press Act 1910. Shorthand first used by police to record political speeches.

2.1 Partition of Bengal

  • Lord Curzon Viceroy from 6 Jan 1899. Measures: cut elected Indians in Calcutta Corporation (1899); Universities Act 1904 (Calcutta Univ. under govt control); Official Secrets Act (1904) amended; created CID 1903 (Unit 3).
  • Bengal unwieldy – partition discussed since 1860s, revived March 1890; European planters in Assam wanted port near Calcutta. Dec 1903 – Curzon's Minutes on Territorial Redistribution of India → published as Risley Papers (reasons: relief of Bengal, improvement of Assam).
  • New province Eastern Bengal and Assam: Chittagong, Dhaka, parts of Rajshahi, hills of Tippera, Assam, Malda. Aim: divide Hindus (west) and Muslims (east) along river Bhagirathi; linguistic division rejected. Curzon's Dhaka speech Feb 1904 wooed Muslims.
  • Figures (1905–11): Bengal – 54 mn people, 9 mn Muslims (16.67%); E. Bengal & Assam – 31 mn, 18 mn Muslims (58.06%).
  • Partition united Bengalis (Bengali identity; Tagore central figure of Bengali literature).

2.2 Anti-Partition movement

  • Announced Dec 1903; moderates (Surendranath Banerjea, K.K. Mitra, Prithwishchandra Ray) – petitions only. Partition declared 19 July 1905; came into effect 16 Oct 1905 = day of mourning; bath in Ganga, marches singing Bande Mataram.
  • Radicals pressing: Bipin Chandra Pal, Aswini Kumar Dutta, Aurobindo. 17 July 1905 Calcutta meeting – Surendranath Banerjea called for boycott of British goods and institutions (moderates' first step beyond petitions). 7 Aug 1905, Calcutta Town Hall – formal proclamation of Swadeshi Movement.
  • Students joined; govt threatened scholarships → boycott of official schools, national schools.

2.3 Boycott and Swadeshi in Bengal (1905–11)

  • G. Subramaniam (Madras): swadeshi = "a revolt against their state of dependence… in all branches of their national life". Gokhale: swadeshi is for "an allround enhancement of our national life".
  • Idea of Swadeshi ("of one's own country"): M.G. Ranade, 1872, Poona lectures – prefer Indian goods even if less satisfactory. Gandhi (1920s): use and serve "immediate surroundings"/neighbours' products.
  • (a) Constructive Swadeshi (self-help): Tagore – atmashakti; education in swadeshi languages; use of melas. Dawn Society – Satish Chandra Mukherjee, 1902; National Council of Education – 5 Nov 1905; Bengal National College and School – Aug 1906. Failed due to poor job prospects.
  • (b) Samitis (volunteer corps): physical/moral training, famine relief, arbitration courts, schools. Weakness: did not reach Muslim peasantry and Depressed Classes; upper-caste Hindu base; coercion (social boycott).
  • (c) Passive resistance (from 1906): boycott of foreign goods, govt schools, courts, titles, services; armed struggle if repression unbearable; religious symbols and glorious past used.

2.4 Militant nationalism

  • Moderates: British rule reformable by petitions; militants: rulers never amenable to reason.
  • Swaraj: Tilak – Indian control of administration, not total break from Britain; Bipin Chandra Pal – complete freedom from foreign rule.
  • Sympathetic to revolutionaries but did not approve political murders. Hindu religious symbolism alienated Muslims. Declined by 1908; Surat split a factor.
  • Surat Split 1907: Minto Viceroy 1906. 1906 Calcutta session – split avoided by electing Dadabhai Naoroji; militants passed 4 resolutions: Swadeshi, Boycott, National Education, Self-Government. 1907 venue shifted from Poona → Surat by moderates. Militants proposed Lala Lajpat Rai (he declined) vs moderate Rash Behari Ghosh; Pherozeshah Mehta group wanted the 4 resolutions dropped → chaos. Post-split Congress = "Mehta Congress"; 1908 session only moderates.

2.5 Revolutionary extremism

  • From ~1908 shift to violence. Bengal akharas (gymnasiums) from 1870s (Vivekananda's "strong muscles and nerves of steel"). Bankim Chandra Chatterjee – Anandmath; its song Bande Mataram = anthem of swadeshi movement.
  • Reasons for individual violence: youth impatient with apolitical constructive work; militants failed to lead long mass movement; "recovery of Indian manhood".
  • Anushilan Samiti, Calcutta (1902) – Jatindranath Banerjee and Barindra Kumar Ghose (Aurobindo's brother). Dhaka Anushilan Samiti 1906 – Pulin Behari Das. Weekly Yugantar. First swadeshi dacoity – Rangpur, Aug 1906. Hemchandra Kanungo – military training in Paris; bomb factory at Maniktala garden house (1908).
  • Alipore Bomb Case: plot to kill Judge Douglas Kingsford; 30 April 1908 Khudiram Bose (18) and Prafulla Chaki (19) bombed a carriage, killing two English women; Chaki killed himself, Khudiram hanged. Aurobindo, Barindra + 35 arrested; C.R. Das defended; Aurobindo acquitted → moved to Pondicherry (died 1950); Barindra and Ullaskar Dutt – death sentence commuted to transportation for life.
  • Limitations: revolutionaries mostly from Brahmin, Kayastha, Vaishya castes.
  • British repression: Morley–Minto Reforms (announced Dec 1908; Act 1909) – moderates welcomed, but institutionalised communal electorates. Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act 1908 (confiscate press); Indian Press Act 1910 (security deposit); Indian Criminal Law Amendment Act (summary trials, ban on associations). Centre of revolutionary activity moved to U.P. and Punjab.

2.6 Swadeshi campaign in Tamil Nadu

  • Strongest in Tirunelveli district; Tamil pride underpinned it; TN Congress divided into moderates/extremists.
  • (a) Vernacular oratory: Tamil speeches; meetings at Marina beach and Moore Market, Madras; police called students seditious (1905–07); students shouted Vande Mataram at Europeans. 1907 – Bipin Chandra Pal's speeches on Madras beach electrified TN.
  • (b) V.O. Chidambaram (V.O.C.) and Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company (SSNC): idea 1906 against British coastal shipping monopoly; registered 1906, capital Rs 10 lakh = 40,000 shares of Rs 25; shares only for Indians, Ceylonese, Asians. Ships S.S. Gallia and S.S. Lawoe. Praised by Tilak (Kesari, Mahratta) and Aurobindo. Major shareholders: Pandithurai and Haji Fakir Mohamed. Europeans showed racial bias against it. (Film: Kappalottiya Tamizhan.)
  • (c) Coral Mill strike, Tuticorin: after Surat 1907 VOC found orator Subramania Siva; Feb–Mar 1907 daily beach meetings at Tuticorin. March 1908 Coral Cotton Mills workers struck – one of the earliest organised labour agitations in India; owners conceded. Aurobindo's Bande Mataram hailed it: "Every victory of Indian labour is a victory for the nation".
  • (d) Subramania Bharati: G. Subramaniam + five others founded The Hindu (English) and Swadesamitran – first Tamil daily; 1906 book condemning British action at Barisal Congress conference. Bharati = sub-editor of Swadesamitran (1904); edited Chakravartini (Tamil monthly for women); edited Tamil weekly India (voice of radicals). Met Sister Nivedita (1905) – called her Gurumani. Admired Tilak after Surat 1907; translated Tilak's Tenets of the New Party into Tamil + booklet on Madras militants' trip to Surat.
  • (e) Arrest of V.O.C. and Siva: B.C. Pal released after 6-month jail on 9 March (book prints 1907; actual 1908 – extra) → to be celebrated as "Swarajya Day" in Tirunelveli; banned; VOC, Siva and Padmanabha Iyengar defied → arrested 12 March 1908 (sedition). Riots: Tirunelveli municipality building and police station burnt; firing killed 4. Judgment 7 July 1908 (King Emperor vs VOC and Siva; judgment excerpt dated 4 Nov 1908): Siva – 10 years transportation; VOC – life (20 yrs) for abetting + another life term for own speech. Punitive tax on people of Tirunelveli and Tuticorin.
  • (f) Ashe murder: June 1911 (17 June – extra), Tirunelveli Collector Robert Ashe shot at Maniyachi railway station by Vanchinathan (forest guard at Punalur, Travancore; member of Bharata Mata Association), who then shot himself. Trained in revolver by V.V. Subramaniam (V.V.S.) in Pondicherry. Trial linked Pondicherry exiles → lull in TN until Home Rule Movement 1916.

Glossary: factionalism – உட்கட்சி பூசல்; annulment – ரத்து; triumvirate – முப்பெரும் தலைவர்கள்; seditious – ஆட்சி எதிர்ப்பு; punitive tax – தண்டனை வரி.

Unit 3 – Impact of World War I on Indian Freedom Movement (PDF p. 35–45)

  • Background: Japan defeated Russia 1905; Young Turks 1908; Chinese revolution 1911. India paid ≈ £367 million for the war (£229 mn cash, rest loans) + war material £250 mn → economic distress.
  • 1916: extremists under Tilak regained control of Congress; Congress reunited; Lucknow Pact 1916.

3.1 All India Home Rule League

  • Annie Besant: Irish-born; Irish home rule, Fabian socialist, birth-control activist; joined Theosophical Society; came to India 1893; founded Central Hindu College, Benaras (upgraded to Benaras Hindu University by Madan Mohan Malaviya, 1916). Succeeded H.S. Olcott (d. 1907) as international president of Theosophical Society (HQ Adyar, Chennai). Followers: Jamnadas Dwarkadas, George Arundale, Shankerlal Banker, Indulal Yagnik, C.P. Ramaswamy, B.P. Wadia.
  • Weekly The Commonweal (1914); book How India Wrought for Freedom (1915); slogan "The moment of England's difficulty is the moment of India's opportunity"; daily New India – 14 July 1915. Declared Home Rule League on 28 Sept 1915 (Irish model).
  • Dec 1915 Bombay Congress altered constitution to re-admit extremists.
  • Twin objectives: Home Rule for India within British Empire; pride in the Motherland.
  • Tilak's League – April 1916, Belgaum (Bombay Provincial Conference); Maharashtra, Karnataka, Central Provinces, Berar; 6 branches; 14,000 members (Apr 1917) → 32,000 (1918). Tilak arrested 23 July 1916 (60th birthday).
  • Besant's League – Madras, Sept 1916; rest of India; branches Kanpur, Allahabad, Benaras, Mathura, Calicut, Ahmednagar. "The price of India's loyalty is India's Freedom". Members: Nehru, Jinnah, B. Chakravarti, Jitendralal Banerji, Satyamurti, Khaliquzzaman.
  • June 1917 Besant, B.P. Wadia, George Arundale interned at Ootacamund; Sir S. Subramaniam renounced knighthood; AICC 28 July 1917 – Tilak urged civil disobedience; Jamnadas Dwarkadas and Shankerlal Banker (on Gandhi's orders) collected 1,000 signatures. Released.
  • Montagu Declaration – 20 Aug 1917: goal = "self-governing institutions and responsible government" → Besant near-loyalist; President of Calcutta Congress 1917.
  • Importance: prepared ground for Gandhian satyagraha; first movement cutting across Congress, League, Theosophists, Labourites.
  • Decline: Besant accepted Montagu–Chelmsford reforms; Tilak went to Britain (Sept 1918) for libel case against Valentine Chirol (author of Indian Unrest). League renamed Commonwealth of India League → India League (1929) by V.K. Krishna Menon.
  • Irish Home Rule: Government of Ireland Act 1920 (6 northern counties); Anglo-Irish Treaty 1921 (26 southern counties).

3.2 Impact of the War; Lucknow Pact

  • Minto–Morley Reforms 1909 – separate electorates for Muslims. 1913 Jinnah joined Muslim League (already in Congress).
  • Ghadar Party: Lala Hardayal, San Francisco, 1913 – Pacific Coast Hindustan Association, president Sohan Singh Bhakna; mostly Sikh immigrants of US/Canada; journal Ghadar from 1 Nov 1913 ('Ghadar' = rebellion, Urdu). Komagata Maru ship turned back from Canada; passengers killed/arrested on return.
  • Oct 1916 memorandum on post-war reforms by Hindu and Muslim elected members of Imperial Legislative Council to Viceroy (unmoved); Congress–League meet Calcutta Nov 1916; confirmed at Lucknow, Dec 1916 (Congress, League and Home Rule League sessions all at Lucknow). Congress president Ambika Charan Mazumdar welcomed extremists back after "ten years of painful separation".
  • Lucknow Pact (Congress–League Pact) provisions: provincial autonomy from central control; 4/5 of central and provincial councils elected, 1/5 nominated, broad franchise; half of executive council members Indians elected by councils; Congress accepted separate electorates for Muslims and weightage in all provinces except Punjab and Bengal; govts bound by council resolutions unless vetoed (if passed again after ≥1 year, to be implemented); Secretary of State's relation with India like that with Dominions; India equal to Dominions in imperial bodies.
  • Jinnah = chief architect; Sarojini (Naidu) Ammaiyar called him "Ambassador of Hindu–Muslim Unity". Paved way for Khilafat–Non-Cooperation unity.

3.4–3.5 Repressive measures; Defence of India Act

  • Revolutionaries = first to demand complete freedom; centres Maharashtra, Bengal, Punjab; briefly Madras Presidency.
  • CID – Curzon, 1903; Newspapers (Incitement to Offences) Act 1908; Explosive Substances Act 1908; Indian Press Act 1910; Prevention of Seditious Meetings Act 1911; Foreigners Ordinance 1914.
  • Defence of India Act 1915 (Defence of India Regulations Act): special tribunals of 3 Commissioners; death/transportation for life/up to 10 yrs; trial in camera, no appeal; used in First Lahore Conspiracy trial; basis of Rowlatt Act.

3.6 Khilafat Movement

  • Sultan of Turkey (= Caliph/Khalifa) sided with Central Powers; Treaty of Sevres – Syria, Lebanon mandated to France; Palestine, Jordan British.
  • Led by Ali brothers – Maulana Muhammad Ali and Maulana Shaukat Ali; joined by Abul Kalam Azad, M.A. Ansari, Sheikh Shaukat Ali Siddiqui, Syed Ataullah Shah Bukhari.
  • Demands (Mohammad Ali, Paris, March 1920): Caliph's position undisturbed; holy places under Sultan; sufficient territory; Jazirat-ul-Arab (Arabia, Syria, Iraq, Palestine) under his sovereignty.
  • Gail Minault: "A pan-Islamic symbol opened the way to pan-Indian Islamic political mobilization." U.P. lower classes read khilaf (against) as revolt; Mappillais of Malabar turned it into anti-landlord revolt.
  • Gandhi returned his medals: Kaisar-i-Hind gold medal, Zulu War silver medal (1906), Boer War silver medal (1899–1900).

3.7 Rise of labour movement

  • Early voices: Sorabjee Shapoorji, N.M. Lokhanday (Bombay), Sasipada Banerjee (Bengal). Causes: war-time expansion then lay-offs, price rise, influenza epidemic, Bolshevik Revolution 1917, ILO (League of Nations).
  • Madras Labour Union, 1918 – B.P. Wadia = first trade union in the modern sense in India; due to ill-treatment at Buckingham and Carnatic Mills, Perambur.
  • Other unions: Indian Seamen's Union, G.I.P. Railway Workers Union, M.S.M. Railwaymen's Union, Jamshedpur Labour Association, Indian Colliery Employees Association (Jharia), etc.
  • AITUC – 30 Oct 1920, Bombay; 64 unions, 1,40,854 members; chairman/president Lala Lajpat Rai; backed by Motilal and Jawaharlal Nehru, C.R. Das, Vallabhbhai Patel, Subhash Chandra Bose.
  • April 1919 strikes in Ahmedabad after Jallianwala Bagh and Gandhi's arrest. Registered unions: 107 (1919–20) → 1,833 (1946–47).

Unit 4 – Advent of Gandhi and Mass Mobilisation (PDF p. 46–64)

  • M.K. Gandhi: born Porbandar, 1869; returned from South Africa 1915; political guru G.K. Gokhale; toured India for a year on Gokhale's advice; set up Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad; did not join Home Rule movement.
  • Satyagraha = Satya (truth) + Ahimsa (non-violence); evolved in South Africa; no hatred of wrongdoer; weapon of the brave.

4.2 Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms (book calls it "Indian Councils Act of 1919"; = Government of India Act 1919)

  • Montagu (Secretary of State) and Chelmsford (Viceroy). Provincial councils with elected majorities; Dyarchy (இரட்டையாட்சி) in provinces: reserved subjects (law and order, finance) under Governor; transferred subjects (health, education, local self-government) under ministers responsible to legislature; Governor's veto ("special powers").
  • Centre bicameral: Central Legislative Assembly 144 members (41 nominated); Council of States 60 (26 nominated). No control over Governor-General; restricted franchise.
  • Congress special session Bombay, Aug 1918: "disappointing and unsatisfactory". Moderates under Surendranath Banerjea left → Indian Liberal Federation.

4.3 Non-Brahmin movement

  • Movements: Namasudra (Bengal/Eastern India), Adidharma (North-Western India), Satyashodhak (Western India), Dravidian (South India).
  • Jyotirao Phule – Gulamgiri (1872); Satyashodhak Samaj. Decennial census caste ranking ("social precedence") caused conflict; caste associations grew; electoral politics from 1880s.
  • Two trends: Sanskritisation of lower castes (north and east), and radical pro-poor peasant-labour movements (west and south → nationalist and Dravidian–Left).
  • South: Brahmins 3.2% of population but 72% of graduates. Non-Brahmin Manifesto – end of 1916: non-Brahmins are "bulk of the tax payers…". Brahmanetara Parishat (Bombay) and Justice Party (Madras) dominant till about 1930. Radical turn: Dalit-Bahujan movement – Dr Ambedkar; Self-Respect Movement – Periyar E.V. Ramasamy. Most nationalists (esp. extremists) hostile – called them British stooges.

4.4 Non-Cooperation Movement

  • British policy "rally the moderates, isolate the extremists" → 1919 Act + Rowlatt Act (March 1919) – imprisonment without trial; passed though every elected member opposed. Gandhi's Satyagraha Sabha pledged to disobey; hartal March–April 1919; khadi = nationalist uniform.
  • Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar – 13 April 1919: >2,000 protesting arrest of Satyapal and Saifuddin Kitchlew; Lt. Governor Michael O'Dwyer, military commander Gen. Reginald Dyer; one narrow entrance; firing till ammunition exhausted; official deaths 379, real >1,000; martial law in Punjab. British enquiries exonerated Dyer and O'Dwyer.
  • Tagore renounced knighthood – letter to Viceroy 31 May 1919.
  • Udham Singh (raised in Khalsa Orphanage) shot Michael O'Dwyer at Caxton Hall, London, 30 March 1940 (13 March 1940 – generally cited date, extra); hanged at Pentonville jail.
  • Two immediate causes of NCM: Khilafat wrong and Punjab wrong.
  • Khilafat Conference decided NCM from 31 Aug 1920; all-party meet at Allahabad – boycott schools and courts. Special Congress session, Calcutta, Sept 1920 accepted Gandhi's proposal; Nagpur session Dec 1920 endorsed + linguistic Provincial Congress Committees; membership fee 4 annas (25 paise).
  • Programme: boycott schools, colleges, courts, offices, legislatures, foreign goods; return titles; national schools, panchayats, swadeshi; later no-tax and civil disobedience. Gandhi promised Swaraj within a year.
  • Prince of Wales' visit 1921 boycotted. Ali brothers jailed for sedition.
  • South India: Andhra peasants withheld taxes; Chirala-Perala town vacated en masse; village Patels and Shanbogues resigned. In Tamil Nadu NCM led by C. Rajagopalachari, S. Satyamurthi and Periyar E.V.R. Kerala – anti-jenmi struggles. Rampa revolt (coastal Andhra tribals) under Alluri Sitarama Raju; Malabar (Mappila) rebellion 1921.
  • Feb 1922 – Gandhi threatened mass civil disobedience at Bardoli.
  • Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur dist., U.P.) – 5 Feb 1922: police fired on 3,000-strong procession; mob burnt police station, 22 policemen killed → Gandhi suspended NCM; CWC ratified at Bardoli. Nehru and Bose critical. Gandhi jailed 6 years. Khilafat redundant after Mustafa Kemal Pasha abolished the Caliphate.

4.5 Swarajist Party

  • C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru – council entry = "Swarajists / pro-changers"; Swarajya Party formed within Congress (1923). In TN, Satyamurti joined the Swarajists.
  • "No-changers": Rajagopalachari, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad – constructive programme (spinning, temperance, Hindu-Muslim unity, removing untouchability).
  • Swarajists won 42 of 101 elected seats in Central Assembly; later co-opted; "responsivists" split. Communal riots; Gandhi's 21-day fast (1924 – extra).
  • Left: Nehru and Bose spread leftism; S.A. Dange, M.N. Roy, Muzaffar Ahmed + Singaravelu (TN) founded peasants' and workers' parties. Conspiracy cases: Kanpur, Meerut, Kakori. Naujawan Bharat Sabha, Hindustan Republican Association. Kakori case – Ram Prasad Bismil and Ashfaqullah sentenced to death, 17 jailed. Bhagat Singh, Chandrashekar Azad, Rajguru killed Saunders (Lahore lathi-charge, death of Lajpat Rai). Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt bombed Central Assembly 8 April 1929.

4.6 Simon Commission – Nehru Report – Lahore Congress

  • Indian Statutory Commission (Simon Commission) – all-white members. Madras Congress 1927 resolved boycott (League and Hindu Mahasabha too). Slogan "Simon Go Back".
  • Motilal Nehru Report; All-Parties meet Calcutta, Dec 1928 failed on communal representation.
  • Calcutta Congress Dec 1928 (president Motilal) → Jawaharlal to preside 1929.
  • Lahore Congress 1929 (Jawaharlal Nehru) – goal complete independence (Poorna Swaraj); tricolour hoisted 31 Dec 1929; 26 January to be Independence Day; civil disobedience under Gandhi.
  • Dandi March – against salt tax; 375 km, Sabarmati → Dandi; 78 followers; reached on 25th day, 6 April 1930; picked up salt.
  • Vedaranyam Salt Satyagraha (TN): led by Rajaji (just elected TN Congress president); from Tiruchirappalli (150 miles) to Vedaranyam (then Thanjavur district); 13 April – 28 April 1930; route via Kumbakonam, Semmangudi, Thiruthuraipoondi; Thanjavur collector J.A. Thorne warned public against sheltering marchers.

4.7 Round Table Conferences

  • Congress boycotted 1st RTC. Gandhi released Jan 1931; Gandhi–Irwin Pact – 5 March 1931: civil disobedience ended; release of non-violent prisoners; salt for own consumption; peaceful picketing of liquor/foreign cloth shops. Viceroy refused to commute Bhagat Singh's death sentence. Karachi Congress ratified the pact.
  • 2nd RTC – Gandhi sole Congress representative; returned empty-handed → CDM renewed; Nehru, Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan, Gandhi arrested; Congress banned; >1 lakh arrested. CDM suspended May 1933, withdrawn May 1934.

4.8 Dr B.R. Ambedkar and separate electorates

  • Born in Mahar caste, son of an army man; first matriculate of his community; Elphinstone College (graduated 1912); scholarship from Maharaja of Baroda → Columbia University (MA, PhD); law and economics in London.
  • 1916 paper "Castes in India" (Anthropology conference; published in Indian Antiquary). Before Southborough (Franchise) Committee first demanded separate electorates + reserved seats for untouchables.
  • Journal Mook Nayak ("leader of the dumb"); Bahishkrit Hitakarini Sabha (association for welfare of excluded); member of Bombay Legislative Council; Mahad Satyagraha – right to public tanks and wells.
  • Communal Award – Ramsay MacDonald, Aug 1932: separate electorates for depressed classes. Gandhi's fast unto death in Yerwada jail → Poona Pact (1932): no separate electorate but reserved seats; carried into Constitution.
  • Parties: Independent Labour Party 1937; Scheduled Caste Federation 1942. Defence Advisory Committee 1942; then member of Viceroy's council; Chairman of Drafting Committee; Law Minister in Nehru cabinet.

Map facts (p. 56, 59): Champaran (Bihar) – indigo; Kheda (Gujarat) – peasant satyagraha; Ahmedabad – mill workers; Jallianwala Bagh (Punjab); Chauri Chaura (U.P.); Dandi (Gujarat) – CDM; Mahad (Maharashtra); Vedaranyam, Madras, Tuticorin (TN); Noakhali riots.

Glossary: Dyarchy – இரட்டையாட்சி; hartal – கடையடைப்பு; monopoly – ஏகபோக உரிமை; fissiparous – பிளவுண்டாக்குகிற.

Unit 5 – Period of Radicalism in Anti-imperialist Struggles (PDF p. 65–76)

  • CPI formed at Tashkent (Uzbekistan, USSR), Oct 1920 by M.N. Roy, Abani Mukherji, M.P.T. Acharya, Mohammad Ali, Mohammad Shafiq. Formally founded on Indian soil at the all-India Communist Conference, Dec 1925 (held at Kanpur – extra), HQ Bombay (Singaravelar attended).
  • First radicals reached Peshawar 3 June 1921, arrested as Bolsheviks; 5 conspiracy cases 1922–27: first Peshawar, then Kanpur (Bolshevik) 1924, Meerut 1929.

5.1 Kanpur Conspiracy Case 1924

  • Charged under Sec. 121-A IPC (deprive King Emperor of sovereignty). Judge H.E. Holmes (earlier, as Gorakhpur Sessions Judge, sentenced 172 peasants to death in Chauri Chaura case).
  • 13 accused originally (incl. M.N. Roy, Muzaffar Ahmad, Shaukat Usmani, S.A. Dange, M. Singaravelar, M.R.S. Velayudhan, Nalini Gupta…); 8 charge-sheeted; Ghulam Hussain turned approver; Roy (Germany) and R.L. Sharma (Pondicherry) in absentia; Singaravelar released on bail (ill-health). Muzaffar Ahmed, Shaukat Usmani, Nalini Gupta, S.A. Dange – 4 years RI.
  • M. Singaravelar (18 Feb 1860 – 11 Feb 1946), Madras: early Buddhist; first in Congress; with Thiru.Vi. Kalyanasundaram organised trade unions in South India; first May Day celebration in India – 1 May 1923; organiser of South Indian Railway strike, Golden Rock, Tiruchirappalli, 1928.

5.2 Meerut Conspiracy Case 1929

  • Strikes: Kharagpur railway workshop (1927), Liluah (1928), Calcutta scavengers (1928), Bengal jute mills (1929), Golden Rock, Tiruchi (July 1928), Bombay textile (April 1928). Acts: Trade Disputes Act 1928, Public Safety Bill 1928.
  • 32 arrested (8 Congressmen; 3 British communists – Philip Spratt, Ben Bradley, Lester Hutchinson); Sec 121A; tried at Meerut to avoid jury. Defence: K.F. Nariman, M.C. Chagla; Gandhi and Nehru visited. Verdict 16 Jan 1933: 27 convicted; Romain Rolland and Albert Einstein protested; reduced on appeal July 1933.

5.3 Bhagat Singh and Kalpana Dutt

  • Bhagat Singh: born 28 Sept 1907, Jaranwala, Lyallpur dist. (now Pakistan); father Kishan Singh, mother Vidyavati Kaur. Naujawan Bharat Sabha; Hindustan Republican Association (founded by Sachin Sanyal and Jogesh Chatterji) → reorganised Sept 1928 as Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA) with Chandrashekhar Azad, Rajguru, Sukhdev.
  • Killed Saunders (mistaken for SP James A. Scott, responsible for assault on Lajpat Rai, Dec 1928 – book); attempt on Irwin's train, Dec 1929.
  • Assembly bomb 8 April 1929 – no one killed; on day of Trade Disputes Bill.
  • Second Lahore Conspiracy Case (Saunders murder): Jatindra Nath Das died after 64-day hunger strike. Death sentence 7 Oct 1930; Bhagat Singh, Rajguru, Sukhdev hanged 23 March 1931, Lahore jail, shouting Inquilab Zindabad. They asked to be shot as war prisoners. Essay "Why I am an Atheist".
  • Surya Sen (school teacher) – Indian Republican Army (named after Irish Republican Army); Chittagong armoury raid 18 April 1930; Dhalghat clash 13 June 1932 (Capt. Cameron killed; widow Savitri Debi); Surya Sen arrested Feb 1933, hanged 12 Jan 1934.
  • Kalpana Dutt (1913–1995) (Kalpana Joshi after marrying P.C. Joshi) – transportation for life ("waging war against the King Emperor"); book Chittagong Armoury Raiders' Reminiscences.

5.4 Karachi Congress 1931

  • March 1931, president Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel; resolution on Fundamental Rights and Duties and economic policy – "manifesto of Congress for independent India"; political and economic freedom inseparable; equal access to public places. Became Part III (Fundamental Rights) and partly Part IV (DPSP) of the Constitution.

5.5 Great Depression (1929–39)

  • Began with Wall Street crash, North America. Indian exports fell from Rs 311 crore (1929–30) to Rs 132 crore (1932–33); prices of jute, cotton fell; Kisan Sabhas (rent reduction, debt relief, abolition of zamindari); no-rent/no-tax campaigns. Only positive effect: cheap land and labour helped some Indian industries.

5.6 Industrial development

  • First Indian cotton mill – Cowasjee Nanabhoy Davar (Parsi), Bombay, 1854 (Bombay Spinning and Weaving Co.). American Civil War (1861–65) boon to cotton. Mills: 47 (1875–76) → 271 (1913–14); 129 in Bombay Presidency by 1914.
  • First passenger train 1853, Bombay–Thane; railways employed 98,723 (1911).
  • First jute mill – Calcutta, 1855; 64 mills by 1914 (European-owned).
  • Bengal Coal Company 1843, Raniganj – Dwarakanath Tagore (Rabindranath's grandfather); coal grew after 1892.
  • TISCO – 1907, Sakchi (Bihar); earlier Bengal Iron Co. (1875), Bengal Iron and Steel Co. (1889); output 31,000 t (1912–13) → 1,81,000 t (1917–18). J.N. Tata (1839–1904), Navsari – "father of Indian modern industry"; trading co. 1868; mill named "Swadeshi" at Kurla; Dorabji Tata realised steel plant; Tata Hydroelectric Co. 1910; Indian Institute of Science, Bangalore.
  • Industrial Commission 1916. First Indian paper mill – Couper Paper Mill, Lucknow, 1882; cement 1904, Madras – South Indian Industries Ltd; govt leather factory Kanpur 1860; National Tannery, Calcutta 1905; Kolar gold mining (Mysore).
  • Imported cotton cloth share: 44% (1929–30) → 20.5% (1933–34). Scindia Steam Navigation Co. (1919) pioneer; took over Bombay Steam Navigation Co. (1939).
  • Tamil Nadu in Depression: Stanes Mill (Coimbatore Spinning and Weaving Mills) 1896; 29 mills/ginning factories in Coimbatore 1929–37; Madukkarai cement factory 1932; sugar factories 2 → 11 (1931–36); rice mills, oil mills, cinema.

Unit 6 – Communalism in Nationalist Politics (PDF p. 77–87)

  • Under Mughals, official/court language Persian; after 1857 Muslims lost land, jobs → withdrew, hated British; British later used Muslim middle class to check Congress. Three strands: Indian, Hindu and Muslim nationalism.

6.1 Origin and growth of communalism

  • (a) Hindu revivalism – Arya Samaj (1875) (Sarvepalli Gopal); Annie Besant identified with Hindu nationalists ("revival… of ancient religions").
  • (b) Muslim consciousness – Aligarh movement; British built Aligarh college, backed Syed Ahmed Khan; Wahabis (purify Islam) to Khilafatists. Bengal govt order, 1870s – Urdu replaced by Hindi and Perso-Arabic by Nagri script in courts → Muslim fears.
  • (c) Divide and Rule (பிரித்தாளும் ஆட்சி) – Bombay Governor Elphinstone: "Divide et Impera was the old Roman motto and it should be ours."
  • (d) Congress – leadership secular; refused resolution (3rd session) to make cow-killing a penal offence; rule: no resolution if delegates of the affected community object.
  • (e) Syed Ahmed Khan – founder of Aligarh movement; at first supported Congress, then turned to collaboration with British. Pro-Congress Muslims: Badruddin Tyabji, Rahmatullah Sayani (Mumbai), Nawab Syed Mohammed Bahadur (Chennai), A. Rasul (Bengal). Syed Ameer Ali – first Indian in London Privy Council. Only 2 of 72 delegates at 1st Congress were Muslims.
  • (f) Local body elections (1880s) – communalised; Lal Chand (Punjab Hindu Sabha, later Arya Samaj) described municipal councils seated as "Muhammedans versus Hindus".
  • (g) Weak-kneed Congress – Swadeshi-era Muslim supporters called "Congress touts". Tilak used Ganapati festival; Tilak, Aurobindo, Lajpat Rai used religious symbols. Nehru: communalism = "products of middle class infighting".

6.2 All India Muslim League

  • Simla Deputation – 1 Oct 1906: 35 Muslim elites under Aga Khan met Viceroy Minto (proportionate jobs, Muslim judges, seats in Viceroy's council) → catalyst for AIML (founded Dhaka, 30 Dec 1906 – extra; book's sentence reads "…in 1907"). Supported partition of Bengal; demanded separate electorates.
  • Objectives: loyalty to British Government; protect Muslims' political rights; prevent hostility towards other communities. First centrally organised party exclusively for Muslims.
  • Separate (communal) electorate – only Muslims vote for Muslim candidates. Minto–Morley 1909: 8 of 27 elected non-official seats in Imperial Legislative Council for Muslims; provinces: Madras 4, Bombay 4, Bengal 5. A British officer to Lady Minto: "pulling of 62 million people from joining the ranks of seditious opposition". Lucknow Pact 1916 sealed separate identity.

6.3 All India Hindu Mahasabha

  • After 5th Punjab Hindu Conference (Ambala) and 6th (Ferozepur) → first all-India Hindu conference at Haridwar, 1915; HQ Dehra Dun; U.P. sabha HQ Allahabad. Urban, north Indian; little response in Madras and Bengal. Defunct 1920–22 (Khilafat unity).
  • Suddhi movement (Dayanand Saraswati – Arya Samaj); Swami Shradhananda revived Mahasabha with cow-protection propaganda (1921).
  • Malabar rebellion 1921 – agrarian (Muslim peasants vs British and Hindu landlords), but communal; Gandhi asked Muslim leaders for public apology.
  • After NCM suspension (1922) and abolition of Caliphate (1924) – riots. Varanasi Mahasabha conference, Aug 1923: 968 delegates, 56.7% from U.P.; U.P., Punjab, Delhi, Bihar 86.8%; Madras, Bombay, Bengal only 6.6%.
  • Allahabad: Motilal Nehru vs Madan Mohan Malaviya (1923 municipal polls); DM Crosthwaite blamed Malaviya family.
  • 1924 Lala Lajpat Rai advocated partition of Punjab into Hindu and Muslim provinces. Mahasabha slogan "Akhand Hindustan".
  • Delhi Conference of Muslims – 20 March 1927: would give up separate electorates if (1) Sind separated from Bombay, (2) reforms for NWFP and Baluchistan, (3) representation by population in Punjab and Bengal, (4) 33% seats in Central Legislature. Motilal Nehru and S. Srinivasa (Iyengar) got AICC to accept; failed. Gandhi: issue "passed out of human hands".
  • Calcutta All Parties Convention 1928 – Mahasabha rejected Jinnah's amendments → Muslims turned to Govt.
  • RSS founded 1925 (1,00,000 volunteers); K.B. Hedgewar, V.D. Savarkar, M.S. Golwalkar – Hindu Rashtra; Savarkar: "We Hindus are a Nation by ourselves". Congress barred members from Mahasabha/RSS in 1934; CWC made Mahasabha membership a disqualification Dec 1938.
  • (d) Communal Award (Ramsay MacDonald) after RTCs; 2nd RTC delegates chosen on communal basis.

6.4–6.6 Congress ministries to Direct Action

  • 1937 elections: Congress won 7 of 11 provinces, largest in 3 more; League only 4.8% of Muslim votes.
  • WWII – Viceroy Linlithgow declared India at war without consultation → Congress ministries resigned. League observed "Day of Deliverance" – 22 Dec 1939.
  • Pakistan idea: Mohammad Iqbal, League session Allahabad 1930 (N-W Indian Muslim state); name pushed by Rahmat Ali (Cambridge student). Two-Nation Theory first from Sir Wazir Hasan, Bombay League session 1937.
  • Lahore Resolution – 23 March 1940 (p. 83 also prints 26 March): contiguous Muslim-majority areas (N-W and E zones) to be "Independent States".
  • League boycotted Quit India 1942. 1946 elections: League won all 30 Muslim seats in Central Legislative Assembly.
  • Cabinet Mission 1946 – led by Secretary of State Pethick-Lawrence (with Cripps, A.V. Alexander); Cripps drafted plan. 3-tier federation; centre only foreign affairs, defence, communications (+ their finances); groups: A – Bombay, Madras, U.P., Bihar, Orissa, C.P.; B – Punjab, Sind, NWFP, Baluchistan; C – Bengal, Assam. Provinces could opt out.
  • 29 July 1946 League rejected plan → Direct Action Day – 16 Aug 1946 → 4 days of killing in Calcutta. Mountbatten succeeded Wavell.

Glossary: communalism – வகுப்புவாதம்; revivalism – மீட்புவாதம்; jizya – non-Muslims' poll tax; shariat – Islamic law; communal award – வகுப்புவாத தீர்ப்பு.

Unit 7 – Last Phase of Indian National Movement (PDF p. 88–103)

Individual Satyagraha, August Offer, Bose's exit

  • August Offer – 8 Aug 1940, Linlithgow: Dominion status at unspecified future date; expanded Viceroy's Executive Council; War Advisory Council; minority rights; Indians to draft constitution after war.
  • Individual Satyagraha (Congress reply): began 17 Oct 1940 – Vinoba Bhave first, near Paunar Ashram (Maharashtra); satyagrahi informed District Magistrate and declared help to British war effort wrong. Suspended Dec 1940; revived Jan 1941; withdrawn Aug 1941.
  • Congress membership: 4.5 million (1938–39) → 1.4 million (1940–41).
  • Subhas Bose isolated, resigned; AICC Calcutta elected Rajendra Prasad; Bose founded Forward Bloc; removed from all Congress posts Aug 1939.

7.1 Cripps Mission (March 1942)

  • Japanese sweep: Pearl Harbour 7 Dec 1941; Philippines, Indo-China, Indonesia, Malaya, Burma fell. Roosevelt and Chiang Kai-shek pressed Churchill. CWC Dec 1941 offered cooperation if independence promised.
  • Sir Stafford Cripps (Labour member of Churchill's war cabinet): Dominion Status + constitution-making body after war (elected by provincial assemblies + nominated princely-state members); any province could stay out (prospect of Pakistan). Nehru: "profoundly depressed". Gandhi's description "post-dated cheque" – extra. Rejected.
  • Bose's Azad Hind Radio broadcast from Germany, March 1942.

7.2 Quit India Movement

  • Gandhi, 16 May 1942: "Leave India to God. If that is too much, then leave her to anarchy." CWC at Wardha, 14 July 1942 resolved mass civil disobedience; C. Rajaji and Bhulabhai Desai resigned from CWC. Call "Do or Die" – "fight to the finish" (Bombay AICC, 8 Aug 1942 – extra).
  • All leaders arrested 9 Aug 1942 early morning. Tata Steel, Jamshedpur shut 13 days from 20 Aug; Ahmedabad textile strike >3 months.
  • 57 battalions; aircraft strafed protesters. Linlithgow to Churchill: "by far the most serious rebellion since 1857".
  • Second phase in villages (food-grain prices up 60 points); from late Sept 1942 attacks on telegraph, railways. Parallel governments: Tamluk Jatiya Sarkar (Midnapore, Bengal, till Sept 1944); Satara. Leaders: Jayaprakash Narayan, Achyut Patwardhan, Asaf Ali, Yusuf Meherally, Ram Manohar Lohia (socialists). (Aruna Asaf Ali hoisted the flag at Gowalia Tank, Bombay, 9 Aug 1942 – extra.)
  • Gandhi's 21-day fast in jail from 10 Feb 1943.
  • By end-1943: 91,836 arrested; 1,060 shot dead; 208 police outposts, 332 railway stations, 945 post offices damaged; 205 policemen defected. R.H. Niblett (Azamgarh collector) – "white terror". Collective fines.
  • Clandestine radio from Bombay – Usha Mehta; heard as far as Madras.
  • Gandhi released on health grounds 6 May 1944. Wavell Viceroy from Oct 1943.

7.3 Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose and the INA

  • Bose arrested 3 July 1940 (Defence of India Act); escaped Calcutta night of 16–17 Jan 1941; via Kabul and USSR on an Italian passport to Berlin (March 1941); met Hitler and Goebbels → only Azad Hind Radio.
  • INA first: Mohan Singh (British Indian Army officer, Malaya) with Japanese help; after fall of Singapore 45,000 POWs; ~40,000 enlisted by end-1942.
  • Bose reached Singapore 2 July 1943; met PM Tojo in Tokyo; Provisional Government of Free India – 21 Oct 1943, Singapore; declared war on Britain and allies; recognised by Axis powers.
  • Rani of Jhansi Regiment (women) – commanded by Dr Lakshmi (Captain Lakshmi Sahgal), daughter of Ammu Swaminathan of Madras.
  • 6 July 1944 – Bose, over Azad Hind Radio from Rangoon, called Gandhi "Father of the Nation", sought blessings for "India's last war of independence".
  • INA battalion under Shah Nawaz with Japanese march on Imphal (late 1944) – failed; surrender mid-1945.
  • INA Trials – Red Fort, Delhi: Shah Nawaz Khan, P.K. Sehgal, G.S. Dhillon; Nehru wore lawyer's gown again; Muslim League, Akali Dal, Hindu Mahasabha joined protests; INA week; found guilty but C-in-C remitted sentences, freed 6 Jan 1946.
  • Simla Conference 25 June – 14 July 1945 failed.

7.4 Royal Indian Navy Revolt (Feb 1946)

  • Rating B.C. Dutt of HMIS Talwar arrested for writing "Quit India" → strike of 1,100 ratings, 18 Feb 1946 (racism, bad food, abuse). Spread to Castle and Fort barracks, Bombay; HMIS Hindustan, Karachi (19 Feb); ≈20,000 ratings, 78 ships, 20 shore establishments; RIAF units (Bombay, Poona, Calcutta, Jessore, Ambala) and Jabalpur sepoys struck.
  • Ships hoisted Congress, Communist and Muslim League flags together. Revolt without leadership; Sardar Patel got it ended. "Last act of rebellion" of the freedom struggle.

7.5 Rajaji Proposals and Wavell Plan

  • Lahore Resolution 1940 changed Muslims from "minority" to "nation"; Jinnah sole spokesman.
  • Rajaji Formula (C.R. Formula) – April 1944: post-war commission to demarcate contiguous Muslim-majority districts + plebiscite of adults on Pakistan; mutual agreement on defence, communication etc. if partitioned; border districts could choose; implementation only after full transfer of power. Gandhi–Jinnah talks (July/Sept 1944) on it failed.
  • Wavell Plan – June 1945 (Wavell had convinced Churchill in London, March 1945): Executive Council all Indians except Viceroy and C-in-C; equal caste-Hindu and Muslim members; separate representation for Scheduled Castes; begin talks on new constitution. Leaders incl. Nehru, Patel, Congress president Maulana Abul Kalam Azad released.
  • Simla Conference (25 June – 14 July 1945) failed – League claimed exclusive right to nominate all Muslims.
  • April 1946 League legislators' convention, Delhi – Pakistan = "sovereign independent state" (Bengal and Assam in NE; Punjab, NWFP, Sind, Baluchistan in NW). Azad rejected it.
  • Clement Attlee (Labour) replaced Churchill – independence certain.

7.6 Cabinet Mission and Mountbatten Plan

  • Cabinet Mission (book here: headed by Cripps, with A.V. Alexander and Pethick-Lawrence) arrived March 1946 – national government + elected constitution-making body; loose confederation, no partition. Jinnah accepted 6 June 1946; Nehru (AICC, 7 July 1946) accepted; League withdrew 29 July 1946.
  • Interim Government: invitations 15 June 1946 to 14 men; Congress proposed Zakir Hussain – League objected. 12 Aug 1946 Viceroy invited Nehru (Congress president); members announced 25 Aug 1946 (Nehru, Patel, Rajendra Prasad, Asaf Ali, C. Rajaji, Sarat Chandra Bose, John Mathai, Baldev Singh, Shafaat Ahmed Khan, Jagjivan Ram, Syed Ali Zaheer, C.H. Bhabha). Nehru took office 2 Sept 1946.
  • Reconstituted 26 Oct 1946 – League members: Liaquat Ali Khan, I.I. Chundrigar, A.R. Nishtar, Ghazanfar Ali Khan, Jogendra Nath Mandal (Jinnah stayed out). Liaquat's "socialistic budget" (March 1947) hit Indian industrialists.
  • July–Aug 1946 elections: Congress 199 of 210 general seats; League 76 (Muslim seats). Only 207 members attended first session of Constituent Assembly, 9 Dec 1946.
  • Gandhi stayed in Beliaghata (Calcutta); reached Delhi 9 Sept 1946 (Muslims in Purana Qila camps); toured riot-hit Noakhali (East Bengal).
  • Attlee, 20 Feb 1947: British to leave by June 1948. Mountbatten replaced Wavell 22 March 1947.
  • Mountbatten Plan: split Punjab (West → Pakistan) and Bengal (East → Pakistan). CWC accepted partition 1 May 1947; date advanced to 15 Aug 1947; AICC (15 June 1947) passed resolution moved by Govind Ballabh Pant accepting partition.

Glossary: dominion status – தன்னாட்சி அந்தஸ்து; Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, Japan – Tripartite Pact) – அச்சு நாடுகள்; prisoners of war – போர்க் கைதிகள்; partition – பிரிவினை.

Unit 8 – Reconstruction of Post-colonial India (PDF p. 104–118)

  • Attlee, 20 Feb 1947 – withdrawal by 30 June 1948; Mountbatten Viceroy 22 March 1947; 3 June 1947 plan advanced date to 15 Aug 1947 (only 72 days); power to two dominions. Mountbatten Plan accepted by AICC on 14 June 1947 (book here; Unit 7 says 15 June).
  • Akali Dal chose India. Gandhi assassinated 30 Jan 1948. Nehru's "Tryst with destiny" speech, night of 14/15 Aug 1947.

8.1 Consequences of Partition

  • Sir Cyril Radcliffe (lawyer, never in India) arrived 8 July 1947; chaired two Boundary Commissions (Punjab, Bengal), each with 2 Muslim + 2 Hindu judges; ~5 weeks; based on faulty 1941 census; award submitted 9 Aug 1947, published after 15 Aug.
  • Punjab: 62,000 sq miles to Pakistan (1.58 crore people, 1.185 crore Muslims); East Punjab 37,000 sq miles (1.26 crore; 43.75 lakh Muslims). Bengal: West Bengal 28,000 sq miles (about a quarter Muslims); East Bengal 49,400 sq miles (3.91 crore; 29% Hindus) = Eastern Bengal + Sylhet (Assam) + Khulna + Chittagong Hill Tracts; Murshidabad, Malda, Nadia stayed in India. East Pakistan became Bangladesh, Dec 1971.
  • After partition: 42 million Muslims in India; 20 million non-Muslims in Pakistan. Aug–Nov 1947: 4.5 million came from West Pakistan; 5.5 million Muslims left India. Refugee columns = "kafila"; 2–5 lakh killed, 15 million displaced. Gandhi fasted on 15 Aug 1947, away from Delhi. W.H. Auden's poem "Partition" on Radcliffe.
  • Nehru–Liaquat (Delhi) Pact – 8 April 1950 – minorities' confidence.
  • Films/books: Tamas (Govind Nihalani), Garam Hawa (M.S. Sathyu), Train to Pakistan (Khushwant Singh).

8.2 Making of the Constitution

  • Principle that Indians make their own constitution: Gandhi 1922; formal Congress demand 1934 (rejected British White Paper).
  • Provincial elections July–Aug 1946 (1935 Act; limited franchise) → provincial assemblies elected Constituent Assembly (224 seats per book here). Congress got Ambedkar elected from Bombay → Chairman, Drafting Committee.
  • First meeting 9 Dec 1946; Rajendra Prasad elected chairman (president); Objectives Resolution – Nehru, 13 Dec 1946; Constitution adopted 26 Nov 1949. Sources: Objectives Resolution (clause 5 → Fundamental Rights), Karachi resolution 1931, UDHR (10 Dec 1948).
  • Features: universal adult franchise, autonomous Election Commission, independent judiciary, sovereign legislature, FRs and DPSPs.

8.3 Merger of Princely States

  • 500+ states. By 15 Aug 1947 all except Kashmir, Junagadh, Hyderabad signed Instrument of Accession (Defence, External Affairs, Communications). Instrument of Accession introduced in Government of India Act 1935.
  • All India States People's Conference (Dec 1945, April 1947): states refusing to join Constituent Assembly = hostile; generous privy purse (மன்னர் மானியம்). Achieved by Sardar Patel (Home + States Ministry).
  • People's movements: Punnapra–Vayalar struggle in Travancore against Diwan Sir C.P. Ramaswamy; Praja Mandals; Orissa tribal agitations (Nilagiri, Dhenkanal, Talcher); Mysore movement.
  • Hyderabad – Nizam declared independence; Razakars militia; Telangana people's movement (communists); "police action" within 48 hours (Operation Polo, Sept 1948 – extra).
  • Junagadh ruler wanted Pakistan against people's wishes.
  • Kashmir – Maharaja Hari Singh wanted independence; National Conference's "Quit Kashmir" agitation; Pakistani raiders Oct 1947 → Instrument of Accession signed (at Patel's instance). Article 370 special status – abolished 2019; J&K made Union Territory.

8.4 Linguistic reorganisation of States

  • Nagpur Congress 1920 – linguistic PCCs. Nehru Report 1928, Section 86 – provinces on linguistic basis. Congress 1945 election manifesto – provinces on linguistic and cultural basis.
  • Pattabhi Sitaramayya – first to raise demand for Andhra province (31 Aug 1946); conference 8 Dec 1946.
  • Linguistic Provinces Commission (3 members; set up by Rajendra Prasad, 17 June 1948; = S.K. Dar Commission – extra) – report 10 Dec 1948 against linguistic states (Andhra, Karnataka, Kerala, Maharashtra).
  • JVP Committee (Jawaharlal, Vallabhbhai, Pattabhi; from Jaipur Congress) – report 1 April 1949: "narrow provincialism", "while language is a binding force, it is also a separating one" – postpone.
  • Potti Sriramulu fast from 19 Oct 1952, died 15 Dec 1952 → Andhra (first linguistic state; Andhra State formed 1 Oct 1953 – extra).
  • Article 3 – Parliament may form new States, increase/diminish area, alter boundaries.
  • States Reorganisation Commission – Fazl Ali (chair), K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru; report Oct 1955 → States Reorganisation Act 1956: Andhra Pradesh (with Hyderabad), Kerala (Travancore-Cochin + Malabar district of Madras), Karnataka (Mysore + parts of Bombay, Madras).
  • Gujarat from Bombay – May 1960; Punjab trifurcated 1966 (Punjab, Haryana, Himachal Pradesh). (Madras State renamed Tamil Nadu – 14 Jan 1969 – extra.)

8.5 India's foreign policy

  • Architect: Nehru. Principles: anti-colonialism, anti-imperialism, anti-apartheid/racism, non-alignment, Afro-Asian unity, non-aggression, non-interference, mutual respect, world peace.
  • Asian Relations Conference – March 1947 (20+ countries); Dec 1948 conference on Dutch re-colonisation of Indonesia; Colombo 1954.
  • India first to recognise People's Republic of China – 1 Jan 1950; China occupied Tibet 1950. 1954 treaty – Panch Sheel: (1) mutual respect for territorial integrity and sovereignty, (2) mutual non-aggression, (3) non-interference in internal affairs, (4) equality and mutual benefit, (5) peaceful co-existence.
  • Bandung (Afro-Asian) Conference – April 1955, Indonesia; Nehru projected Chou En-lai; 10-point Bandung Declaration on world peace (UN Charter principles) → Belgrade 1961 – birth of NAM (Nehru, Nasser of Egypt, Tito of Yugoslavia).
  • Dalai Lama fled to India 1959; Oct 1959 Chinese fired near Kongka pass, Ladakh (5 policemen killed). 8 Sept 1962 China attacked Thagla ridge → 1962 war. Self-respect restored by 1971 Bangladesh war.

Glossary: constitution – அரசமைப்பு; Constituent Assembly – அரசமைப்பு நிர்ணய சபை; Objectives Resolution – குறிக்கோள் தீர்மானம்; princely states – சுதேச அரசுகள்; privy purse – மன்னர் மானியம்; linguistic – மொழிவாரி.

Unit 9 – Envisioning a New Socio-Economic Order (PDF p. 119–133)

  • 1947: ~80% rural, dependent on agriculture; industry only 13% of GDP (1950); TISCO best-known heavy industry.
  • Socialistic pattern of society – Preamble "sovereign, socialist, secular democratic republic"; reduce inequality, end exploitation, prevent concentration of wealth; land reforms; public sector; Five Year Plans – Soviet model = "Nehruvian Socialism".
  • Disguised unemployment – surplus people in agriculture whose removal would not reduce output. Backwardness: institutional + technological factors; institutional tackled first.

9.1 Land reforms (agriculture = State subject)

  • Three British revenue systems: Permanent Settlement / zamindari (Bengal, N. India), Ryotwari (S. India; ryot = cultivator, pays directly), Mahalwari (village collectively).
  • (a) Zamindari abolition: by 1949 U.P., M.P., Bihar, Madras, Assam, Bombay legislated; West Bengal only 1955. 1st Amendment 1951 and 4th Amendment 1955 blocked court challenges. Completed by 1956 – most successful reform; ~30 lakh tenants got 62 lakh ha; compensation paid Rs 16,420 lakh (~¼ of due). Many zamindars evicted tenants claiming "personal cultivation".
  • (b) Tenancy reform: ~half of cultivated land under tenancy; rents ≥50%. Aims: regulate rent (fixed at ¼ to ⅓ of produce), secure tenant rights, confer ownership. Largely failed (oral, unrecorded leases); some success in Kerala and West Bengal.
  • (c) Land ceiling: Tamil Nadu first in 1961; unit "landholder" till 1972, then "family" (TN second ceiling act 1972). Exemptions (orchards, plantations, trusts, sugarcane) misused. Only ~65 lakh ha surplus to ~55 lakh tenants (~1 ha each).
  • Bhoodan – Vinoba Bhave – voluntary gift of surplus land.
  • Appraisal: economic dream unmet; socially, zamindari abolition effective; peasants politically aware.

9.2 Development of agriculture

  • Green Revolution: HYV seeds of wheat and rice – 1965, irrigated areas; needed water, tractors, fertilisers, pesticides. Self-sufficiency within 20 years. Rice 35 → 104 million t (1960–61 to 2011–12); wheat 11 → 94 million t. Buffer stocks with FCI; PDS. Milk and eggs up. Negatives: regional disparity, chemical overuse → organic farming.
  • Poverty line = expenditure needed to buy food grains giving recommended calories. Programmes: Community Development, Panchayati Raj, targeted schemes.
  • IRDP (1980–1999): assets to rural poor; all 5,011 blocks; 600 families/block over 1980–85 (15 million families); subsidy shared equally Centre–States: 25% small farmers, 33.3% marginal farmers/agri labourers, 50% tribal households; bank loans for rest; 53.5 million households by 1999; assets: dairy 50%, non-farm 25%, minor irrigation 15%. Restructured 1999 as self-employment programme.
  • MGNREGA (NREGA 2005, renamed after Gandhi): 100 days of wage work/year per rural household (adult members, unskilled manual); legal right to demand work; self-targeting, all rural households; by Gram Panchayats; job cards; work within 15 days else unemployment allowance; site within 5 km; no contractors; wage : material = 60 : 40; one-third women, equal wages; payment to bank/post office accounts. 2006–12: ~Rs 1,10,000 crore wages, 1,200 crore person-days; good in TN, AP, Rajasthan; ~¼ rural households participate yearly.

9.3 Development of industry

  • Gandhian model (village/cottage) vs adopted Nehruvian model (heavy industry, state-owned).
  • Industrial Policy 1948 – 4 categories: (1) state monopolies – atomic energy, railways, arms; (2) 18 industries of national importance under govt control; (3) mixed; (4) private.
  • Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 – Schedule A (state monopoly), B (state + private), C (rest).
  • Industries (Development and Regulation) Act 1951 – licence needed for new units/expansion. Policy statements: 1973 (big houses to backward areas), 1977 (Janata – rural/small industries), 1980 (Congress – balanced growth).
  • Nationalised: coal mines, petroleum, banking, insurance.
  • Public sector: 5 enterprises (1951) → 225 (2012); investment Rs 29 crore → 7.3 lakh crore. Long-gestation projects. Steel plants Bhilai (Chhattisgarh), Rourkela (Odisha), Durgapur (WB), Bokaro (Jharkhand); BHEL, HMT – with Britain, Germany, Russia. BHEL first at Bhopal, then Tiruchirappalli, Hyderabad, Haridwar.
  • Crisis by 1991: half of profit from petroleum; spread into hotels, TV sets; political locations, overstaffing, bureaucratic management → disinvestment from 1991.
  • Industrial Policy 1991 – Liberalisation: licences removed, bigger private role, disinvestment, closure of sick units. Positives: consumer goods, FDI, ease of doing business; negatives: income disparities, informal-sector jobs.

9.4 Five Year Plans

  • Planning Commission – 1950 (USSR model). 12 Plans (1951–2017) + 3 annual plans 1966–69 (plan holiday). 2015 – replaced by NITI Aayog.
  • 1st Plan 1951–56: agriculture; outlay Rs 3,870 crore; agriculture + irrigation 31%. 11th Plan outlay >Rs 36.44 lakh crore. Public sector 60–70% of outlay (2nd–6th Plans).
  • 2nd Plan 1956–61 = Mahalanobis Plan (P.C. Mahalanobis) – heavy industry; industry share 6% (1st) → ~24%.
  • First two plans ~4% growth = "Hindu rate of growth" (as the book uses it). 4th Plan (1969–74) – poverty alleviation. Targets met from 6th Plan. 8th Plan (1992–97) – liberalisation; >7% growth thereafter (except 9th). 12th Plan 2012–17 last.
  • Achievements: bigger economy, national and per-capita income, industrial and agricultural output, diversified economy.

9.5 Education, Science and Technology

  • Literacy 18.3% (1951) → 74% (2011); female 65%, male 82%. 2014–15: 12.72 lakh primary/upper primary schools, 2.45 lakh secondary/HSS, 38,498 colleges, 43 Central, 316 State, 122 Deemed, 181 State Private Universities. Drop-out schemes: SSA, RMSA, Samagra Shiksha Abhiyan.
  • IISc Bangalore – 1909 – only science research institute before Independence (Tata endowment – J.N. Tata; book prints "J.R.D. Tata" – and Maharaja of Mysore).
  • TIFR – 1945, Homi J. Bhabha (Tata funding). First national labs: National Chemical Laboratory, Pune; National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi. CSIR – umbrella body. Atomic Energy Commission – nuclear power and weapons.
  • ICAR – agricultural research; 67 agricultural universities, 3 in Tamil Nadu.
  • IITs: first Kharagpur, then Delhi, Bombay, Kanpur, Madras; now 23 IITs, 31 NITs, ~25 IIITs.

Glossary: socialism – சமதர்மக் கோட்பாடு; tenancy – குத்தகை; land ceiling – நில உச்சவரம்பு; public sector – பொதுத்துறை; literacy – எழுத்தறிவு.
