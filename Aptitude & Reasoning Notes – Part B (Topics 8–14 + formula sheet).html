Topic 8 · Volume – 3D (Days 23, 24, 27)

Read: Class 8 Maths Ch 2 §2.4 Three-dimensional shapes (faces, edges, vertices, nets), PDF p. 72–79; Class 9 Maths Ch 7 Mensuration §7.4–7.5 Surface area and volume of cuboid and cube, PDF p. 259–269; Class 10 Maths Ch 7 Mensuration §7.2 Surface area, §7.3 Volume, combined solids and conversion of solids, PDF p. 275–306.

In the exam: usually 1–2 Q (cylinder/cone/sphere volume or CSA, litres in a tank, melting and recasting).

Concept

  • Volume (கனஅளவு) = space occupied by a solid (cm³, m³). Capacity of a container = volume it holds (litres).
  • Lateral / curved surface area (LSA/CSA) = area of the side walls only; total surface area (TSA) = CSA + area of the ends (top and bottom).
  • Euler's formula for solids (Class 8 §2.4): F + V − E = 2 (cube: 6 + 8 − 12 = 2).
  • Melting / recasting: the volume stays the same; number of small solids = volume of big ÷ volume of one small.
  • Frustum = the part of a cone left when the top is cut off by a plane parallel to the base (bucket, glass tumbler).

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • Ratio questions – write the formula as a ratio and cancel π and constants: V₁/V₂ = (r₁²h₁)/(r₂²h₂).
  • Number of balls / coins / bricks – divide volumes directly; for spheres from a sphere: (R/r)³ (radius 8 → balls of radius 1: 8³ = 512).
  • % change in volume of a cube: (1 + x/100)³ − 1; in surface area: (1 + x/100)² − 1 (edge +50% → SA +125%, V +237.5%).
  • Pick r a multiple of 7 to make π = 22/7 cancel; check whether the options are in terms of π.
  • Litres – compute in m³ and × 1000, or in cm³ and ÷ 1000.
  • Roller / road – area covered in one revolution = CSA of the roller (2πrh).

Solved examples (TNPSC style)

2. A cubical tank holds 64,000 litres of water. Find the length of its side. (Class 9 Ex 7.14)
64,000 L = 64 m³ → a = ∛64 = 4 m.

3. A conical tent has radius 7 m and height 24 m. Find the canvas needed, and its length if the canvas is 4 m wide. (Class 10 Ex 7.5)
l = √(49 + 576) = 25 m; CSA = 22/7 × 7 × 25 = 550 m²; length = 550 ÷ 4 = 137.5 m.

4. A cylinder has radius 7 cm and height 10 cm. Find its volume, CSA and TSA.
V = 22/7 × 49 × 10 = 1540 cm³; CSA = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 440 cm²; TSA = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 17 = 748 cm².

5. How many lead balls of radius 1 cm can be made from a solid sphere of radius 8 cm?
(8/1)³ = 512.

6. A hemispherical bowl has radius 21 cm. How many litres does it hold?
(2/3) × 22/7 × 21³ = 19,404 cm³ = 19.404 litres.

7. A garden roller is 3 m long with diameter 2.8 m. What area does it level in 8 revolutions? (Class 10 Ex 7.3 type)
CSA = 2 × 22/7 × 1.4 × 3 = 26.4 m² → × 8 = 211.2 m².

8. Each edge of a cube is increased by 50%. Find the % increase in its surface area.
(1.5)² = 2.25 → 125% increase.

Topic 9 · Time and Work (Days 29, 31, 33, 35)

Read: Class 8 Maths Ch 4 Life Mathematics §4.5 Compound Variation (proportion, multiplicative-factor and formula methods), PDF p. 146–153; §4.6 Time and Work, §4.6.1 Sharing of money, Exercise 4.4–4.5, PDF p. 153–157; Class 7 Term 1 Ch 4 §4.3 Inverse Proportion, PDF p. 82–85.

In the exam: 1–3 Q a year (together/alone, men–days–hours, wages sharing, pipes; 2024 asked a men + women wages problem).

Concept

  • The whole work is taken as 1 unit (a wall, a road, filling a tank, eating food). Work is assumed uniform.
  • If a person finishes the work in n days, his one day's work = 1/n; if he does 1/n per day, he needs n days.
  • More workers → fewer days (inverse); more work → more days (direct). A chain of two or more such variations = compound variation (direct–direct, direct–inverse, inverse–direct, inverse–inverse).
  • Not every problem is proportional: frying one vadai takes 5 min does not mean 20 vadais take 100 min (textbook note).
  • Wages are shared in the ratio of work done, i.e. in the ratio of one-day works 1/x : 1/y : 1/z (when they work the same number of days).

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • LCM method – take total work = LCM of the days; each person's per-day units = LCM ÷ his days. A 20, B 30 → total 60 units; A 3/day, B 2/day → together 5/day → 12 days. Avoids fractions.
  • "k times as fast and takes d days less" – times are in ratio 1 : k, difference = (k − 1) parts = d (Ex 4.25: 3 times, 24 days less → 1 part = 12 → A 12, B 36).
  • Men + women / men + boys – convert to one-day earnings or work, solve two equations, or find "1 man = how many women".
  • Left in between – work done by the one who stays for the extra days first; remaining work ÷ joint rate = days together.
  • Check the answer – together time is always less than the faster person's time.

Solved examples (TNPSC style)

1. (TNPSC 2024) 3 men and 4 women earn ₹3,780 in 7 days. 11 men and 13 women earn ₹15,040 in 8 days. In what time will 7 men and 9 women earn ₹12,400? (a) 9 (b) 12 (c) 10 (d) 11 days
Per day: 3m + 4w = 540; 11m + 13w = 1880. ×11 and ×3: 33m + 44w = 5940, 33m + 39w = 5640 → 5w = 300 → w = 60, m = 100. 7m + 9w = 700 + 540 = 1240/day → 12400 ÷ 1240 = 10 days → (c).

2. A and B together do a work in 16 days; A alone in 48 days. B alone? (Class 8 Ex 4.23)
1/16 − 1/48 = 2/48 = 1/24 → 24 days (or xy/(y − x) = 16 × 48/32 = 24).

3. P and Q can do a work in 20 and 30 days. They start together; Q leaves after some days and P finishes the rest in 5 days. After how many days did Q leave? (Ex 4.24)
P's 5 days = 1/4; remaining 3/4 at (1/20 + 1/30) = 1/12 per day → 3/4 × 12 = 9 days.

4. A works 3 times as fast as B and takes 24 days less. Together? (Ex 4.25)
A 12, B 36 days → 12 × 36/48 = 9 days.

5. X, Y, Z can do a job in 4, 6, 10 days. They work together and get ₹31,000. Shares? (Ex 4.26)
1/4 : 1/6 : 1/10 = 15 : 10 : 6 (sum 31) → ₹15,000, ₹10,000, ₹6,000.

6. 48 men working 7 hours a day do a work in 24 days. In how many days will 28 men working 8 hours a day do it? (Ex 4.22, inverse–inverse)
48 × 7 × 24 = 28 × 8 × D → D = 36 days.

7. A mat 180 m long is made by 15 women in 12 days. How long will 32 women take to make a 512 m mat? (Ex 4.20, direct–inverse)
15 × 12/180 = 32 × D/512 → D = 12 × (512/180) × (15/32) = 16 days.

8. Pipes A and B fill a tank in 10 h and 15 h; pipe C empties it in 12 h. If all are opened together, when will the tank be full?
1/10 + 1/15 − 1/12 = (6 + 4 − 5)/60 = 1/12 → 12 hours.

Concept

  • Average / arithmetic mean (சராசரி) = sum of observations ÷ number of observations.
  • Ages: write present ages as x (or ratio parts kx), then form one equation from the "years ago / years hence" condition. The difference of two ages never changes.
  • Cost price (C.P., அடக்க விலை) = price at which an article is bought; selling price (S.P., விற்பனை விலை) = price at which it is sold. Profit/gain = S.P − C.P; loss = C.P − S.P. Profit or loss % is always on C.P.
  • Marked price (M.P.) = price on the tag; discount (தள்ளுபடி) is always on M.P.; S.P = M.P − discount. Overhead expenses (repairs, transport, labour) are added to C.P.
  • GST (Goods and Services Tax): the only common tax on almost all goods and services; types CGST, SGST, IGST (UTGST for union territories); shared equally by Centre and State; slabs 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%; egg, honey, milk, salt exempted; petrol, diesel outside GST (Class 8 p. 133).
  • Speed = distance ÷ time; for a fixed distance, speed and time are in inverse proportion.

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • Deviation method for averages – assume a mean, add the deviations: 47, 52, 49, 55, 47 → assume 50: −3 + 2 − 1 + 5 − 3 = 0 → mean 50.
  • Ages by options – put each option into the conditions; usually faster than equations.
  • Ratio of C.P : S.P = a : b → profit % = (b − a)/a × 100 (5 : 7 → 40%).
  • Sell at loss x%, want gain y% → new S.P = old S.P × (100 + y)/(100 − x) (4275 × 105/95 = 4725).
  • Successive % formula for discounts and mark-ups (a + b + ab/100 with signs).
  • Convert speed first – when lengths are in metres, change km/h to m/s with × 5/18.

Solved examples (TNPSC style)

78.4 + (96 − 69)/25 = 78.4 + 1.08 = 79.48 → (b).

2. The ages of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 5. After 6 years the ratio will be 5 : 6. Find their present ages.
(4k + 6)/(5k + 6) = 5/6 → 24k + 36 = 25k + 30 → k = 6 → 24 and 30 years.

3. By selling a bicycle for ₹4,275 a shopkeeper loses 5%. At what price should he sell it to gain 5%? (Class 8 Ex 4.9)
C.P = 4275 × 100/95 = 4,500 → S.P = 4500 × 105/100 = ₹4,725.

4. The C.P of 16 boxes equals the S.P of 20 boxes. Gain or loss %? (Ex 4.8)
(16 − 20)/20 × 100 = −20 → 20% loss.

5. Find the single discount equal to successive discounts of 25% and 20%. (Ex 4.11)
25 + 20 − (25 × 20)/100 = 40%.

6. Vaidegi sold two sarees for ₹2,200 each, gaining 10% on one and losing 12% on the other. Overall? (Class 8 Ex 4.5 Q5)
C.P₁ = 2200 × 100/110 = 2000; C.P₂ = 2200 × 100/88 = 2500; total C.P 4500, S.P 4400 → loss 100 = 2 2/9 % loss.

7. A train 150 m long runs at 54 km/h. How long does it take to cross a pole?
54 × 5/18 = 15 m/s → 150 ÷ 15 = 10 seconds.

8. A man goes to a place at 40 km/h and returns at 60 km/h. Average speed?
2 × 40 × 60/100 = 48 km/h.

Concept

  • A sequence (தொடர்வரிசை) is a list of numbers in a definite order following a rule; find the rule and continue it.
  • Arithmetic progression (AP, கூட்டுத் தொடர்வரிசை): constant difference d. Geometric progression (GP, பெருக்குத் தொடர்வரிசை): constant ratio r.

Concept

  • A die (பகடை) is a cube: 6 faces, 8 vertices (corners), 12 edges. Euler: F + V − E = 2 (6 + 8 − 12 = 2).
  • Each face has 4 adjacent faces and 1 opposite face. In any one view you see 3 faces that meet at a corner – they are all adjacent, never opposite.
  • On a standard die opposite faces add up to 7: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4 (extra; use only when the question says "standard die").
  • Probability (நிகழ்தகவு) P(E) = number of favourable outcomes / total outcomes; 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1; P(sure event) = 1; P(impossible) = 0; P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
  • One die: 6 outcomes; two dice: 6 × 6 = 36; one coin 2; two coins 4 {HH, HT, TH, TT}; n coins 2n.

3. (TNPSC 2024) Rameela says, "Ragavi's father Raman is the only son of my father-in-law Ramanathan." How is Ramani, the sister of Ragavi, related to Ramanathan? (a) Wife (b) Sister (c) Daughter (d) Grand daughter
Raman = only son of Rameela's father-in-law → Raman is Rameela's husband. Ragavi and Ramani are Raman's daughters → Ramani is Ramanathan's son's daughter = granddaughter → (d).

4. (TNPSC 2025) D, F, G, H, I and M sit in a row. I and M are in the centre; F and D are at the ends; G sits to the left of D (the key reads this as immediately left). Who is to the right of F? (a) H (b) I (c) M (d) G
Six seats 1–6 (facing us, left = lower number). I, M in seats 3–4. G immediately left of D is possible only with D in seat 6, G in seat 5 → F in seat 1, H in seat 2 → right of F is H → (a).
