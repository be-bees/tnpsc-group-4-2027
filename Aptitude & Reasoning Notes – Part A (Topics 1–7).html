Concept

  • Simplification (சுருக்குதல்) = reduce a numerical expression to one number by doing the operations in a fixed order.
  • Order of operations (செயல்பாடுகளின் வரிசை) – the textbook calls it BIDMAS: Bracket → Indices (powers, roots) → Division → Multiplication → Addition → Subtraction. Same as BODMAS (O = Of/Orders).
  • D and M are of equal rank; A and S are of equal rank – do them from left to right (textbook: "operations done from left to right").
  • Brackets: innermost first – ( ) then { } then [ ]. A bar (vinculum, ‾) over numbers is done even before ( ) (extra).
  • Textbook story (Class 6): 5 cups of buttermilk at ₹6, price up by ₹2 → correct bill is 5 × (6 + 2) = ₹40, not (5 × 6) + 2 = ₹32 – brackets decide the answer.

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • Unit-digit check – when options differ in the last digit, compute only the last digit (e.g. 23 × 47 ends in 1).
  • Digit-sum (casting out 9s) – to check a big product: digit-sum of answer = digit-sum of (digit-sums multiplied). Eliminates wrong options fast.
  • Approximation – when options are far apart and numbers are "ugly" (39.97, 600.03), round to 40, 600 and compute.
  • × 5 = × 10 ÷ 2; × 25 = × 100 ÷ 4; × 125 = × 1000 ÷ 8; × 99 = × 100 − itself.
  • Squaring a number ending in 5: n5² = n(n+1) followed by 25 (65² = 6·7 | 25 = 4225). Near 50: (50 + a)² = 2500 + 100a + a² (53² = 2809). Near 100: 97² = (100−3)² = 9409; 999² = 998001.
  • a² − b² pattern – whenever you see "x² − y²" or "(x² − y²)/(x − y)", factorise: 87² − 13² = 100 × 74 = 7400.
  • a² + 2ab + b² pattern – 885×885 + 115×115 + 2×885×115 = (885 + 115)² = 10,00,000.
  • Anything0 = 1 – look for a zero power first; it can kill half of a long expression (TNPSC 2025).
  • Continued fraction 1 + 1/(1 + 1/(1 + 1/2)) – start from the bottom line and move up.
  • Perfect square/cube making – prime-factorise, find the unpaired (square) or incomplete triplet (cube) factors; multiply/divide by them.

Concept

  • Per cent (சதவீதம்) comes from Latin per centum = "per hundred"; symbol %. x% = x/100; 1% = 1/100 = 0.01.
  • Percentage is a way of comparing quantities when the totals differ (Geetha 475/600 vs Seetha 425/500 → 79.17% vs 85%, so Seetha did better).
  • Proper fraction → less than 100%; improper fraction → more than 100%. All parts of a whole add up to 100% (96% passed → 4% failed).
  • Uses: profit, loss, discount, tax (GST), interest, population growth, depreciation (Class 8 p. 127).

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • x% of y = y% of x – when one is easy: 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2; 48% of 50 = 50% of 48 = 24.
  • Build from 10% and 1% – 10% = shift decimal one place; 5% = half of 10%; 15% = 10% + 5%; 1% = shift two places.
  • Use fraction equivalents – 12.5% = 1/8, 33⅓% = 1/3, 16⅔% = 1/6, 62.5% = 5/8: "62.5% of 640" = 5/8 × 640 = 400.
  • Assume 100 – when no base value is given (income, price, population), start with 100.
  • Successive % formula x + y + xy/100 – for "increase then decrease", "two successive discounts", "length and breadth both change" (area change).
  • Equal % up then down always gives a loss of x²/100 % (10% up then 10% down → 1% down).
  • A fall can never be more than 100% – Class 8 "Think" box: 200 → 600 is a 200% rise, but 600 → 200 is only a 66⅔% fall, because the base is different.
  • Reverse %: "after 20% increase it is 96" → 96 × 100/120 = 80 (do not subtract 20% of 96!).

Solved examples (TNPSC style)

1. If x% of 600 is 450, find x. (Class 8 Ex 4.1)

2. When a number is decreased by 25% it becomes 120. Find the number. (Class 8 Ex 4.2)
75% of N = 120 → N = 120 × 100/75 = 160.

3. A man's income is increased by 10% and then decreased by 10%. Find the net change. (Class 8 Ex 4.5)
Net = 10 − 10 + (10 × −10)/100 = −1 → 1% decrease. (Check: 100 → 110 → 99.)

4. The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what per cent must a family reduce its consumption so that expenditure stays the same?
r/(100 + r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

5. In an election between two candidates, the winner got 58% of the valid votes and won by 2240 votes. Find the total valid votes.
Margin = 58% − 42% = 16% → 16% of V = 2240 → V = 2240 × 100/16 = 14,000.

Take 500 boys, 300 girls: failed = 80 + 24 = 104 out of 800 = 13% → passed = 87%.

7. A student needs 35% to pass. He got 180 marks and failed by 30 marks. Find the maximum marks.
35% of Max = 180 + 30 = 210 → Max = 210 × 100/35 = 600.

8. If 48% of 48 = 64% of x, then x = ? (a) 64 (b) 56 (c) 42 (d) 36 (Class 8 Ex 4.1 Q15)

Concept

  • Factor divides a number exactly; multiple = number × 1, 2, 3 …. Prime has exactly 2 factors (2 is the only even prime; 1 is neither prime nor composite).
  • HCF (மீப்பெரு பொது வகுத்தி – மீ.பொ.வ) = Highest Common Factor = GCD (Greatest Common Divisor): the largest number that divides all the given numbers exactly. Written (a, b).
  • LCM (மீச்சிறு பொது மடங்கு – மீ.சி.ம) = Least Common Multiple: the smallest number that is exactly divisible by all the given numbers.
  • Co-prime / relatively prime: HCF = 1 (e.g. 7 and 13; any two consecutive integers are co-prime).
  • Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every integer > 1 can be written as a product of primes in exactly one way (apart from order), e.g. 32760 = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 × 13. Primes are the "building blocks" (like atoms).

Methods of finding HCF (Class 8 §7.6.1)

MethodHowExample
1. Factorisation (listing)List all factors, pick the biggest common one20: 1,2,4,5,10,20; 15: 1,3,5,15 → HCF 5 (20 mangoes + 15 apples shared among max 5 children: 4 mangoes + 3 apples each)
2. Prime factorisationHCF = product of common primes with lowest powers; LCM = product of all primes with highest powers18 = 2·3², 27 = 3³ → HCF 3² = 9; LCM 2·3³ = 54
3. Repeated division (Euclid)Divide larger by smaller; divide the divisor by the remainder; repeat till remainder 0; last divisor = HCF396 = 300×1 + 96; 300 = 96×3 + 12; 96 = 12×8 + 0 → HCF 12
4. Repeated subtractionSubtract smaller from larger until both are equal; that equal value = HCF (GCD(a, b) = GCD(a − b, b))144, 120 → 24, 120 → 24, 96 → … → 24, 24 → HCF 24
  • Euclid's division lemma: for positive integers a, b there are unique q, r with a = bq + r, 0 ≤ r < b (dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder). Euclid wrote "Elements" (13 volumes) – "Father of Geometry". The word "algorithm" comes from the 9th-century Persian mathematician Al-Khwarizmi.
  • HCF of three numbers: find d = HCF(a, b), then HCF(d, c). (HCF of 396, 504, 636 = 12.)
  • LCM by common division: divide all numbers by a common prime, bring down the rest, continue; LCM = product of all divisors and the last row.

Divisibility rules (extra – needed for quick factorising)

  • 2 last digit even; 4 last two digits ÷ 4; 8 last three digits ÷ 8; 5 ends 0/5; 10 ends 0.
  • 3 digit-sum ÷ 3; 9 digit-sum ÷ 9; 6 = divisible by 2 and 3.
  • 11 (sum of digits in odd places − sum in even places) = 0 or a multiple of 11 (e.g. 9174: (9 + 7) − (1 + 4) = 11 ✓).

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • Other number = HCF × LCM ÷ given number – the most-asked 1-line question.
  • Option test – the answer for HCF must divide every given number; the answer for LCM must be divisible by every given number. Test options instead of computing.
  • HCF of big numbers – HCF also divides their difference: HCF(1218, 1914) divides 696 → check factors of 696 only.
  • Ratio of fractions → whole numbers – multiply by LCM of denominators (Topic 4, Ex 2).
  • Clock time – convert LCM in minutes/seconds to hours and add to the start time.

Concept

  • Ratio (விகிதம்) = comparison of two quantities of the same kind and same unit by division; a : b (read "a is to b"), also written a/b or "a to b".
  • A ratio has no unit (8 km : 4 km = 2 : 1, not 2 km : 1 km). Convert to the same unit first: 4 km : 400 m = 4000 : 400 = 10 : 1.
  • Each number is a term (first term = antecedent, second = consequent – extra). Order cannot be reversed: 5 : 1 ≠ 1 : 5. 12 boys : 12 girls = 1 : 1.
  • A ratio a : b has a + b parts in all (5 m thread cut at 3 m → 3 : 2).
  • Proportion (விகித சமம்): two equal ratios, a : b :: c : d ("a is to b as c is to d"). Extremes a, d; means b, c. Proportionality law / cross product: ad = bc.
  • Unitary method: find the value of one unit (division), then of the required number of units (multiplication).
  • Direct proportion (நேர் விகிதம்): both increase or both decrease, x/y = k (constant) → x₁/y₁ = x₂/y₂ (shirts and price; distance and time at constant speed; SI and number of years).
  • Inverse proportion (எதிர் விகிதம்): one increases, the other decreases, xy = k → x₁y₁ = x₂y₂ (workers and days; speed and time for a fixed distance; soldiers and days of food).

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • One part = total ÷ (sum of ratio terms) – every "divide ₹X in the ratio" sum. The total must be a multiple of the sum of terms – use this to reject options.
  • Make the common term equal – A : B = 2 : 3, B : C = 4 : 5 → B = LCM(3, 4) = 12 → 8 : 12 : 15.
  • Chains "A gets double of B, B double of C" → C = 1, B = 2, A = 4 → 4 : 2 : 1.
  • Ratio given with fractions (½ : ⅓ : ¼ or 1/3 : 1/4 : 1/5 : 1/6) – multiply by LCM of denominators; minimum number = sum of the resulting whole numbers.
  • Decide direct or inverse first: "more → more" = direct (multiply across); "more → less" = inverse (product constant). Then one line: new = old × (ratio of the changing quantity).
  • Coins – value ratio = number ratio × face value (₹1, 50p, 25p in 5 : 6 : 8 → values 5 : 3 : 2).
  • "If x : y = 3 : 4, find (3x + 2y) : (5x − y)" – just put x = 3, y = 4 → 17 : 11.

Concept

  • Principal / Sum (அசல்) P = money borrowed or deposited. Interest (வட்டி) I = extra money paid for using it. Amount (தொகை) A = P + I.
  • Rate r% per annum (p.a.) = ₹r interest on every ₹100 for one year (8% p.a. → ₹8 per ₹100 per year).
  • Simple interest (தனி வட்டி): interest is calculated on the same original principal for the whole period, so it is the same every year (it grows in a straight line). Interest earned is never added to the principal.
  • Selvam's example (textbook): ₹10,000 at 15% → interest for 1 year ₹1,500 → amount ₹11,500.

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

Concept

  • Compound interest (கூட்டு வட்டி): the interest of each period is added to the principal, and the new amount becomes the principal for the next period – "interest on interest". C.I = Amount − Principal.
  • Conversion period = the period after which interest is added (yearly, half-yearly, quarterly). Quarterly → 4 periods a year, rate per period = r/4, number of periods = 4n.
  • CI = SI for the first conversion period; after that CI > SI because the principal keeps growing.
  • Textbook illustration: ₹20,000 at 10% for 4 years → CI ₹9,282 (amounts 22000, 24200, 26620, 29282) vs SI ₹8,000. ₹1,000 at 10% for 3 years → ₹1,331.
  • Also used for population growth, growth of bacteria/cells, depreciation of machines and vehicles.
  • Box fact: in 1626 Peter Minuit bought Manhattan Island for 24 dollars; at 5% compounded monthly it would be over 5.5 billion dollars by 2020.

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • Fraction form of rate: 10% = 1/10 → P : A for 1 year = 10 : 11; 2 years 100 : 121; 3 years 1000 : 1331. 5% → 20 : 21 (2 yrs 400 : 441). 4% → 25 : 26 (625 : 676). 20% → 5 : 6. Use when P is a multiple of the denominator.
  • 2-year CI = 2r + r²/100 % of P: ₹12,000 at 5% → 10.25% of 12,000 = 1,230.
  • Successive % formula (Topic 2) works for CI: two years at 10% = 10 + 10 + 1 = 21%.
  • Half-yearly: halve the rate, double the periods (4% p.a. for 1½ years half-yearly = 2% for 3 periods).
  • CI − SI questions: never compute both; use P(r/100)² or P(r/100)²(3 + r/100).
  • Year-by-year table for 2–3 years is quick and error-free when numbers are round.

Concept

  • Perimeter (சுற்றளவு) = length of the boundary (unit: cm, m). Area (பரப்பளவு) = surface enclosed (sq. units: cm², m²).
  • Circle: radius r, diameter d = 2r; circumference/diameter is always the same number π ≈ 22/7 ≈ 3.14.
  • Arc = part of the circumference; sector = region between two radii and an arc (minor/major sector); segment = part cut off by a chord; central angle θ of a sector; whole circle = 360°, n equal sectors → each 360°/n (quadrant = 90°).
  • Combined shapes: split into known shapes, add or subtract areas. Any quadrilateral can be split by a diagonal into two triangles.
  • Heron of Alexandria (Greek, c. 10–75 CE) gave the formula for the area of a triangle from its three sides.

Shortcut tricks (and when to use them)

  • % change in area (sides change by x% and y%) – use x + y + xy/100. Every side up by x% → area up by 2x + x²/100 (radius +10% → area +21%; side doubled → area ×4).
  • Choose r as a multiple of 7 with π = 22/7; if the answer has decimals like .14 or .28, π = 3.14 was used.
  • Circumference ↔ area without π twice: from C get r = C × 7/44; area of circle with circumference 44 = 154, with 88 = 616, with 132 = 1386.
  • Sector area = lr/2 when arc length is given (no angle needed).
  • Ring area = π × (sum of radii) × (width) – one multiplication.
  • Field book (extra): offsets along a base line AC form right triangles and trapeziums; add ½ × gap × (sum of offsets) for each piece on both sides.

4. The parallel sides of a trapezium are 12 cm and 18 cm and the distance between them is 8 cm. Area?
½ × 8 × (12 + 18) = 120 cm².

5. A circular garden of radius 14 m has a 7 m wide path all round it outside. Find the area of the path.

8. The length of a rectangle is increased by 20% and the breadth decreased by 10%. Change in area?
20 − 10 + (20 × −10)/100 = 8% increase. (Also: Kamalesh's circular table of radius 70 cm and Tharun's quadrant table of radius 140 cm have the same area, 15,400 cm² – Class 8 Ex 2.4: doubling r makes area ×4, a quadrant takes ¼.)
