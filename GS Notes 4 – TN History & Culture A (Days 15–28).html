Palayam and Palayakkarar system (பாளையக்காரர் முறை)

  • Palayam = a domain, a military camp, or a little kingdom. Palayakkarar = holder of a little kingdom as a feudatory of a greater sovereign; the British called them Poligars.
  • Palayam given for valuable military service. The system was earlier practised under Prataba Rudhra of Warangal (Kakatiya kingdom).
  • Introduced in Tamil Nadu by Viswanatha Nayaka when he became Nayak ruler of Madurai in 1529, with the support of his minister Ariyanathar. Traditionally 72 Palayakkarars.
  • Duties: collect revenue, administer territory, settle disputes, maintain law and order. Police duties = Padikaval / Arasu Kaval.
  • System lasted about 200 years (from Madurai Nayaks until British takeover).
  • Eastern Palayams: Sattur, Nagalapuram, Ettayapuram, Panchalankurichi. Western Palayams: Uthumalai, Thalavankottai, Naduvakurichi, Singampatti, Seithur. (Mnemonic: Kattabomman = east; Puli Thevar = west)
  • First resistance to the East India Company's territorial aggrandisement: Puli Thevar of Nerkattumseval (Tirunelveli region, now Tenkasi district). Then Velunachiyar, Kattabomman, Marudhu brothers, Dheeran Chinnamalai → culmination: Vellore Revolt 1806. Background: Company had defeated the French in the three Carnatic Wars.

Palayakkarars' Revolt 1755–1801 – (a) Puli Thevar (1755–1767)

  • March 1755: Mahfuzkhan (brother of the Nawab of Arcot) sent with Company army under Colonel Heron to Tirunelveli; Madurai fell easily. Heron abandoned the attack on Puli Thevar (no cannon, supplies, pay) → retired to Madurai → Heron recalled and dismissed.
  • Puli Thevar had influence over the western Palayakkarars.
  • Three Pathan officers (agents of Nawab Chanda Sahib): Mianah, Mudimiah, Nabikhan Kattak – commanded Madurai & Tirunelveli; supported Palayakkarars against Arcot Nawab Mohamed Ali; Puli Thevar was close to them.
  • Confederacy: all Maravar palayams joined except Sivagiri; Ettayapuram and Panchalankurichi also did not join. Supported by rajas of Ramanathapuram and Pudukottai. Sought help of Hyder Ali and the French – Hyder could not help (busy fighting the Marathas).
  • Battle of Kalakadu: Mahfuzkhan (1000 Company sepoys + 600 Nawab's men + Carnatic cavalry) vs Puli Thevar, helped by 2000 soldiers from Travancore → Mahfuzkhan's troops routed.
  • 1756–1763: Tirunelveli palayakkarars under Puli Thevar in constant rebellion, aided by the Raja of Travancore.
  • Yusuf Khan (= Khan Sahib; before conversion Marudhanayagam) sent by the Company; waited for big guns from Tiruchirappalli – arrived September 1760. Battered Nerkattumseval fort ~2 months.
  • 16 May 1761: Puli Thevar's three forts – Nerkattumseval, Vasudevanallur, Panayur – captured by Yusuf Khan.
  • English took Pondicherry → French eliminated → palayakkarar unity broke; Travancore, Seithur, Uthumalai, Surandai switched sides.
  • Yusuf Khan negotiated with palayakkarars without informing the Company → charged with treachery, hanged 1764.
  • Puli Thevar returned from exile, recaptured Nerkattumseval 1764; defeated by Captain Campbell 1767; escaped, died in exile.
  • Ondiveeran: led an army unit of Puli Thevar (not Kattabomman); oral tradition – his hand was chopped off in battle; he called it a reward for penetrating the enemy fort.

(b) Velunachiyar (1730–1796)

  • Born 1730, only daughter of Raja Sellamuthu Sethupathy of Ramanathapuram. Trained in valari, stick fighting, weapons, horse riding, archery; knew English, French, Urdu.
  • At 16 married Muthu Vadugar, Raja of Sivagangai; daughter Vellachinachiar.
  • 1772: Nawab of Arcot + Company troops under Lt. Col. Bon Jour stormed Kalaiyar Kovil palace; Muthu Vadugar killed.
  • She lived under protection of Gopala Nayak at Virupachi (near Dindigul) for 8 years.
  • Dalavay Thandavarayanar wrote to Hyder Ali on her behalf asking for 5,000 infantry + 5,000 cavalry; she explained her problems in Urdu. Hyder Ali ordered his Commandant Syed (Dindigul fort) to help.
  • With help of Gopala Nayak and Hyder Ali she recaptured Sivagangai; crowned Queen with help of the Marudhu brothers. First female ruler/queen to resist British colonial power in India.
  • Kuyili: her faithful friend; led women's unit named after Udaiyaal (a shepherd girl killed for not revealing information on Kuyili). Kuyili walked into the British arsenal (1780) after setting herself on fire, destroying the ammunition.
  • Gopala Nayak (Palayakkarar of Virupachi): led the Dindigul League with Lakshmi Nayak of Manaparai and Poojai Nayak of Devadanapatti; inspired by Tipu Sultan; resisted from Coimbatore, later joined Oomaidurai; fought at Aanamalai hills (peasant support); overpowered by British in 1801.

(c) Veerapandya Kattabomman (1790–1799)

  • Became Palayakkarar of Panchalankurichi at age 30 on death of father Jagavira Pandya Kattabomman. Company administrators James London and Colin Jackson first thought him peaceful.
  • Treaty of 1781: Nawab assigned Carnatic revenue to the Company during the Mysore war (1/6 kept for Nawab's family) → Company appointed Collectors to collect tax from palayams → humiliation and force = bone of contention.
  • Arrears 1798: 3310 pagodas. Collector Jackson (Madras Govt refused him an army). 18 Aug 1798 – ordered Kattabomman to meet him at Ramanathapuram; refused audience at Courtallam and Srivilliputhur; met at Ramanathapuram on 19 Sept 1798 – made to stand 3 hours.
  • Escape: minister Sivasubramanianar captured; Oomaithurai helped Kattabomman escape; Lieutenant Clarke killed at the fort gate.
  • Madras Council committee: William Brown, William Oram, John Casamajor. Governor Edward Clive released Sivasubramanianar, suspended Jackson. Committee sat 15 Dec 1798 → Kattabomman not guilty; Jackson dismissed; new Collector S.R. Lushington. Arrears cleared except 1080 pagodas.
  • South Indian Confederacy formed by Marudhu Pandiyar of Sivagangai (leader) with Gopala Nayak (Dindigul) and Yadul Nayak (Aanaimalai). Lushington prevented Kattabomman meeting Marudhu brothers. Kattabomman tried to win over Sivagiri (a Company tributary) → seen as a challenge.
  • Siege of Panchalankurichi: May 1799 – Lord Wellesley ordered forces from Tiruchirappalli, Thanjavur, Madurai to Tirunelveli; commander Major Bannerman; Travancore troops joined British. 1 Sept 1799 ultimatum; 5 Sept army at Panchalankurichi. Bannerman sent Ramalinganar (who gathered the fort's secrets). Clash at Kolarpatti → Sivasubramanianar captured.
  • Kattabomman fled to Pudukottai; betrayed by rajas of Ettayapuram and Pudukottai. Sivasubramanianar executed at Nagalapuram (13 Sept). Mock trial by Bannerman 16 Oct 1799; Kattabomman admitted all charges; hanged from a tamarind tree in the old fort of Kayathar (near Tirunelveli). Memory kept alive by folk ballads.

(d) Marudhu Brothers and South Indian Rebellion (1800–1801)

  • Periya Marudhu (Vella Marudhu) 1748–1801 and Chinna Marudhu 1753–1801 – generals of Muthu Vadugar; restored the throne to Velunachiyar after the Kalaiyar Kovil battle. Worked with Oomathurai; plundered Nawab's granaries.
  • Revolt of 1800 = "Second Palayakarar War" in British records = South Indian Rebellion.
  • Confederacy: Marudhu Pandyars (Sivagangai), Gopala Nayak (Dindigul), Kerala Verma (Malabar), Krishnappa Nayak and Dhoondaji (Mysore). Met at Virupachi, April 1800. Uprising broke out in Coimbatore, June 1800 → Ramanathapuram, Madurai. Palayakkarars of Coimbatore, Sathyamangalam, Tarapuram hanged.
  • Feb 1801: Kattabomman's brothers Oomathurai and Sevathaiah escaped Palayamkottai prison → Kamudhi → Chinna Marudhu took them to Siruvayal (his capital). Panchalankurichi fort rebuilt; retaken by Colin Macaulay (April). Marudhus refused to hand over fugitives; Col. Agnew and Col. Innes marched on Sivagangai.
  • Tiruchirappalli Proclamation (June 1801) – proclamation of Independence by the Marudhu Pandyars; early call to unite against the British across region, caste, creed and religion; pasted on walls of the Nawab's palace in Tiruchirappalli fort and Srirangam temple. Chinna Marudhu raised ~20,000 men. British reinforcements from Bengal, Ceylon, Malaya. Rajas of Pudukkottai, Ettayapuram, Thanjavur sided with British.
  • Rebels retreated to Piranmalai and Kalayarkoil. Sivagangai annexed 1801. Marudhu brothers executed at Tirupathur fort (near Ramanathapuram) on 24 Oct 1801. Oomathurai & Sevathaiah beheaded at Panchalankurichi, 16 Nov 1801. 73 rebels exiled to Penang (Malaya).
  • Carnatic Treaty, 31 July 1801: British took direct control of Tamilagam; Palayakkarar system ended – forts demolished, armies disbanded.

(e) Dheeran Chinnamalai (1756–1805)

  • Born Theerthagiri, 1756, Kongu region; trained in silambam, archery, horse riding.
  • Seized tax money of Tipu's Diwan Mohammed Ali; told him "Chinnamalai" (between Sivamalai and Chennimalai) took it → name Dheeran Chinnamalai. Defeated Diwan's contingent at Noyyal river bed.
  • After Tipu's death built a fort and fought without leaving the place → Odanilai. Guerrilla attacks. Captured with brothers, imprisoned at Sankagiri; hanged atop Sankagiri Fort, 31 July 1805.

Vellore Revolt, 10 July 1806

  • Background: Company got Salem, Dindigul after war with Tipu 1792; Coimbatore annexed after Anglo-Mysore War 1799; Raja of Thanjavur (vassal since 1798) gave up sovereignty 1799. Treaty of 1801 with Nawab of Arcot: ceded North Arcot, South Arcot, Tiruchirappalli, Madurai, Tirunelveli.
  • Grievances: low salary, poor promotion, disrespect to social/religious sentiments, peasant distress, famine 1805; Tipu's sons and family interned in Vellore Fort.
  • Trigger: new military regulation by Commander-in-Chief Sir John Cradock – no caste marks or earrings in uniform; clean-shaven chin, uniform moustache; new turban with leather cockade (animal skin) – most objectionable.
  • Outbreak, 10 July 1806 (early hours): sepoys of 1st and 23rd regiments. First victim Colonel Fancourt (garrison commander); then Col. McKerras (23rd regt); Major Armstrong shot; ~a dozen officers killed incl. Lt. Elly, Lt. Popham.
  • Rebels proclaimed Fateh Hyder (Tipu's eldest son) ruler; hoisted the tiger flag of Mysore.
  • Major Cootes informed Col. Gillespie (cavalry commandant, Arcot), who arrived 9 am with Captain Young's squadron and crushed it brutally – 800 dead in the fort; 600 confined at Tiruchirappalli & Vellore.
  • Consequences: 6 blown from guns, 5 shot, 8 hanged; Tipu's sons sent to Calcutta; Gillespie got 7,000 pagodas; Cradock, Adjutant General Agnew and Governor William Bentinck removed and recalled; regulations withdrawn.
  • Organisers: Subedars Sheik Adam, Sheik Hamid, Jamedar Sheik Hussain (2nd bn, 23rd regt); Jamedar Sheik Kasim (1st bn, 1st regt).
  • Estimate: failed – no outside help; foreshadowed 1857 but no civil rebellion followed. Echoes at Bellary, Walajabad, Hyderabad, Bengaluru, Nandydurg, Sankaridurg.

Glossary: protege – dependent; aggrandizement – raising one's power; cockade – ribbon ornament on hat (தொப்பியை அணிசெய்யும் குஞ்சம்); interned – imprisoned; ultimatum – final demand (இறுதி எச்சரிக்கை).

Unique feature of TN freedom struggle: from the beginning it was a fight for independence from British rule and from the social disabilities of the caste system.

9.1 Early nationalist stirrings

  • Madras Native Association (MNA), 1852 – earliest organisation in South India for larger public (not sectarian) interests. Founders: Gazulu Lakshminarasu, Srinivasanar & associates. Mostly merchants; aim: reduce taxation; protested government support to Christian missionaries. Agitation against torture of peasants by revenue officials → Torture Commission → abolition of the Torture Act (land revenue collected by torture). Ceased to exist by 1862.
  • T. Muthuswami – first Indian Judge of Madras High Court, 1877; the European-owned press criticised it → need for an Indian newspaper.
  • The Hindu, 1878 – started by G. Subramaniam, M. Veeraraghavachari and four other friends; vehicle of nationalist propaganda.
  • Swadesamitran – Tamil nationalist periodical by G. Subramaniam, 1891; became a daily in 1899.
  • Other native papers that followed: Indian Patriot, South Indian Mail, Madras Standard, Desabhimani, Vijaya, Suryodayam, India.
  • Madras Mahajana Sabha (MMS), 16 May 1884 – earliest organisation in South India with clear nationalist objectives. Founders: M. Veeraraghavachari, P. Anandacharlu, P. Rangaiah. First president: P. Rangaiah; secretary: P. Anandacharlu. Demands: simultaneous civil services exams in England and India; abolish Council of India in London; reduce taxes and civil/military expenditure (many later adopted by INC).

Moderate phase

  • December 1884 meeting at the Theosophical Society, Adyar – Dadabhai Naoroji, K.T. Telang, Surendranath Banerjee + G. Subramaniam, Rangaiah, Anandacharlu (pre-INC meeting).
  • 1st INC session 1885, Bombay: 72 delegates, 22 from Madras.
  • 2nd session 1886, Calcutta – president Dadabhai Naoroji.
  • 3rd session 1887, Madras at Makkis Garden (now Thousand Lights) – president Badruddin Tyabji; of 607 delegates, 362 from Madras Presidency.
  • Madras Presidency then included coastal Andhra & Rayalaseema, Karnataka (Bengaluru, Bellary, South Canara), Kerala (Malabar), Odisha (Ganjam).
  • Moderates = constitutional methods, hall meetings, in English. Tamil moderates: V.S. Srinivasanar, P.S. Sivasamy, V. Krishnasamy, T.R. Venkatramanar, G.A. Natesan, T.M. Madhava Rao, S. Subramaniar.

9.2 Swadeshi Movement (after Partition of Bengal, 1905)

  • Calcutta Congress programme: Swadeshi enterprise, boycott of foreign goods, national education.
  • TN leaders: V.O. Chidambaranar, V. Chakkaraiyar, Subramania Bharati, Surendranath Arya. Tamil used on the public platform for the first time to mobilise people. Bharati's songs stirred emotions.
  • Journals: Swadesamitran and India. Extremist Bipin Chandra Pal toured Madras.
  • Swadeshi Steam Navigation Company (1906) at Thoothukudi by V.O.C.; ships Gallia and Lavo; plied Thoothukudi – Colombo.
  • Tirunelveli Uprising (1908): V.O.C. + Subramania Siva organised mill workers of Thoothukudi and Tirunelveli; strike at European-owned Coral Mills; coincided with release of Bipin Chandra Pal – meeting to celebrate it → both arrested for sedition. V.O.C. first given two life imprisonments; made to pull the oil press in prison. Riots in Tirunelveli: police station, court, municipal office burnt; 4 killed in police firing.
  • Bharati moved to Pondicherry (French) to avoid arrest; followed by Aurobindo Ghosh, V.V. Subramanianar.

Revolutionary activities

  • Pondicherry = safe haven. Revolutionaries trained at India House, London and Paris: M.P.T. Acharya, V.V. Subramanianar, T.S.S. Rajan. Radical papers from Pondicherry: India, Vijaya, Suryodayam.
  • Bharata Matha Society (1904) – secret society started by Nilakanta Brahmachari; aim: kill British officials.
  • Ashe murder: Vanchinathan of Senkottai shot Robert W.D'E. Ashe, Collector of Tirunelveli, at Maniyachi Junction, 17 June 1911; then shot himself.

Annie Besant and Home Rule Movement

  • Moderates disappointed with Minto–Morley reforms (no responsible govt).
  • Annie Besant – Irish lady, leader of Theosophical Society; Home Rule League 1916 on the Irish model. Assisted by G.S. Arundale, B.P. Wadia, C.P. Ramaswamy. Home rule with only nominal allegiance to the Crown.
  • Newspapers: New India and Commonweal. Quote: "Better bullock carts and freedom than a train deluxe with subjection." Asked to pay security under Press Act 1910.
  • Books: How India Wrought for Freedom, India: A Nation; a pamphlet on self-government.
  • George Joseph – barrister, born Chengannur (Alappuzha, Kerala), settled in Madurai; organised Home Rule League in Madurai; called "Rosaappu Durai". (Exercise: "Vaikom Hero" = EVR Periyar.)

9.3 Non-Brahmin movement and challenge to Congress

  • Madras Dravidian Association, 1912 – secretary C. Natesanar; June 1916 he set up the Dravidian Association Hostel for non-Brahmin students.
  • South Indian Liberal Federation (SILF), 20 Nov 1916 – meeting of ~30 non-Brahmins at Victoria Public Hall, Chennai, led by P. Thyagarayar, Dr. T.M. Nair, C. Natesanar (= Justice Party).
  • Justice Ministry: Congress boycotted 1920 elections; Justice Party won 63 of 98 elected seats; A. Subburayalu = first Chief Minister. After 1923 elections Raja of Panagal formed the ministry.

Rowlatt Act and Satyagraha (1919)

  • Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act 1919 = Rowlatt Act (after Sir Sidney Rowlatt) – imprisonment without judicial process ("Black Act").
  • 18 March 1919: Gandhi addressed meeting on Marina Beach. 6 April 1919: hartal; fasting and prayer at Marina. Madras Satyagraha Sabha formed; speakers Rajaji, Kasturirangar, S. Satyamurty, George Joseph. Workers' meeting addressed by Thiru.Vi.Ka, B.P. Wadia, V.O.C. Workers, students, women took part in large numbers.

Khilafat Movement

  • After Jallianwala Bagh (General Dyer acquitted and rewarded) and the humiliation of the Caliph of Turkey.
  • TN Khilafat Day 17 April 1920, presided by Maulana Shaukat Ali; another conference at Erode. Vaniyambadi = epicentre of Khilafat agitation.

9.4 Non-Cooperation Movement

  • Leaders in TN: C. Rajaji and E.V. Ramaswamy (Periyar). Rajaji worked closely with Yakub Hasan, founder of the Madras branch of the Muslim League.
  • No-tax campaign in Thanjavur; boycott of councils, schools, courts, foreign goods; workers' strikes; temperance movement (anti-liquor, toddy-shop picketing) = an important aspect in TN.
  • Nov 1921 civil disobedience decided → Rajaji, Subramania Sastri, EVR arrested. Visit of Prince of Wales, 13 Jan 1922 boycotted (2 killed in repression).
  • Withdrawn 1922 after Chauri Chaura (22 policemen killed).

Swarajists vs Justicites; Subbarayan Ministry

  • No-changers (boycott councils): Rajaji, Kasturirangar, M.A. Ansari. Pro-changers → Swaraj Party by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru; in TN led by S. Srinivasanar and S. Satyamurti.
  • 1926 Madras elections: Swarajists won majority but did not accept office; supported independent P. Subbarayan to form ministry.
  • Swarajists did not contest 1930 → Justice Party won; Justice Party in office till 1937.

Simon Commission boycott; Neill Statue

  • Simon Commission 1927 (Sir John Simon) – all-white. Simon Boycott Propaganda Committee, Madras – president S. Satyamurti. Arrived Madras 18 Feb 1929 – black flags.
  • Neill Statue agitation (1927): James Neill of the Madras Fusiliers – brutal at Kanpur in 1857; statue on Mount Road. Moved to Madras Museum when Rajaji's Congress ministry (1937) took office. (Exercise match: S.N. Somayajulu – Removal of Neill Statue.)

9.5 Civil Disobedience Movement

  • Madras INC session 1927 declared complete independence as goal. Lahore 1929 – Poorna Swaraj; 26 Jan 1930 Nehru hoisted flag on the banks of the Ravi.
  • Vedaranyam Salt March: led by Rajaji; from Tiruchirappalli 13 April 1930 → Vedaranyam (Thanjavur dist.) 28 April 1930. March song by Namakkal V. Ramalinganar ("A war is ahead sans sword, sans bloodshed…"). 12 volunteers broke salt law; Rajaji arrested. Participants: T.S.S. Rajan, Rukmani Lakshmipathi, Sardar Vedarathnam, C. Swaminathar, K. Santhanam.
  • T. Prakasam and K. Nageswara Rao – satyagraha camp at Udayavanam near Madras → arrests → hartal. Tiruvallikeni clash, 27 April 1930 – 3 dead. Salt satyagraha attempted at Rameswaram.
  • Rukmani Lakshmipathi – first woman to pay penalty for violating salt laws.
  • Bhashyam (Arya) hoisted the national flag atop Fort St. George, 26 Jan 1932.
  • Tirupur Kumaran (O.K.S.R. Kumaraswamy) – died holding the flag, Tirupur, 11 Jan 1932 → "Kodikatha Kumaran".

First Congress Ministry (1937) and Anti-Hindi agitation

  • 1937 elections (under GoI Act 1935): Congress won, Justice Party trounced. Rajaji formed first Congress ministry; prohibition experimentally in Salem; introduced sales tax to compensate. Resigned when India was dragged into WW II.
  • Anti-Hindi agitation: against compulsory Hindi in schools. EVR led; anti-Hindi Conference at Salem. Supported by Scheduled Castes Federation and Muslim League. Natarajan and Thalamuthu died in prison. Rally Tiruchirappalli → Madras; 1200+ arrested incl. EVR. Governor removed compulsory Hindi after the ministry resigned.

9.6 Quit India (1942)

  • 8 Aug 1942 resolution; "Do or Die". K. Kamaraj escaped arrest by getting down at Arakkonam; worked underground.
  • Strikes: Buckingham and Carnatic Mills, Madras Port Trust, Madras Corporation, Electric Tramway; students; many joined the INA.

Glossary: hegemony – மேலாதிக்கம்; obnoxious – வெறுக்கப்படுகிற; seditious – ஆட்சிக்கு எதிரான; picket – மறியல்; boycott – புறக்கணி; repression – அடக்குமுறை.

Unit 5 · Social and Religious Reform Movements in the 19th Century – background

  • English education produced an English-educated middle class influenced by Western ideas and Christianity; reformers tried to harmonise Indian and Western cultures; fought sati, female infanticide, child marriage, superstitions.
  • Reformist movements: Brahmo Samaj, Prarthana Samaj, Aligarh Movement. Revivalist: Arya Samaj, Ramakrishna Mission, Deoband Movement.
  • Challengers of oppressive social structure: Jyotiba Phule (Pune), Narayana Guru and Ayyankali (Kerala), Ramalinga Adigal and Iyothee Thassar (Tamil Nadu).

5.1 Early reform movements in Bengal

  • Raja Rammohan Roy (1772–1833) – knew Sanskrit, Arabic, Persian, English, Bengali; monotheism, anti-idolatry (based on Upanishads); against sati, child marriage, polygamy; for widow remarriage and women's education, English and Western science. His campaign led to William Bentinck's abolition of sati, 1829.
  • Brahmo Samaj, 20 Aug 1828 – imageless temple in Calcutta; "no religion should be reviled"; appeal limited to intellectuals/enlightened Bengalis.
  • Maharishi Debendranath Tagore (1817–1905) (father of Rabindranath) carried on after 1833; laid down four articles of faith: (1) in the beginning only the one Supreme Being existed, who created the universe; (2) He alone is God of Truth, Infinite Wisdom, Goodness, Power – eternal, omnipresent, One without second; (3) salvation depends on belief in Him and His worship; (4) belief = loving Him and doing His will.
  • Keshab Chandra Sen (1838–84) joined 1857; split 1866 → Keshab's Brahmo Samaj of India; Debendranath's group = Adi Brahmo Samaj. Keshab married his 14-year-old daughter to an Indian prince → opponents formed the Sadharan Samaj.
  • Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar (1820–1891) – argued Hindu scriptures were progressive (no sanction for widow burning or ban on widow remarriage); pioneer of modern Bengali prose; girls' schools; → Widows' Remarriage Reform Act 1856. Credited with the first age of consent in the IPC 1860: marriage age 10, raised to 12 (1891) and 13 (1925); Age of Consent Committee (1929) found the law remained on paper.
  • Prarthana Samaj, Bombay 1867 – founder Dr. Atma Ram Pandurang (1825–1898); members R.C. Bhandarkar, Justice M.G. Ranade; inter-caste dining and marriage, widow remarriage, uplift of women and depressed classes.
  • Mahadev Govind Ranade (1842–1901) founded Widow Marriage Association (1861), Poona Sarvajanik Sabha (1870), Deccan Education Society (1884).

5.2 Hindu revivalism

  • Arya Samaj, 1875 – Swami Dayanand Saraswati (1824–83), settled in Punjab; book Satyarthaprakash; strict monotheism, anti-idolatry, rejected Brahman ritual domination; "Go back to the Vedas"; counter-conversion ceremony suddhi; Dayananda Anglo-Vedic (DAV) schools and colleges.
  • Ramakrishna Paramahamsa (1836–86) – priest of Dakshineswar near Kolkata; Kali worshipper; all religions lead to salvation; "Jiva is Siva".
  • Ramakrishna Mission – established by Vivekananda (on Christian missionary model) after Ramakrishna's death (1886); education, health care, disaster relief. (Belur Math pictured.)
  • Swami Vivekananda (Narendra Nath Datta, 1863–1902) – practical Vedanta of service to humanity; cultural nationalism; 1893 World Congress of Religions, Chicago; inspired Swadeshi-era militant youth.
  • Theosophical Society – Madame H.P. Blavatsky (1831–1891) and Col. H.S. Olcott (1832–1907); founded USA 1875; shifted to Adyar, Chennai, 1886; promoted study of Upanishads and Gita; revival of Buddhism.
  • Annie Besant (1847–1933) – president after Olcott's death; Home Rule League; papers New India and Commonweal.

5.3 Anti-caste movements

  • Jyotiba Govindrao Phule (b. 1827, Maharashtra) – first school for "untouchables", Poona 1852; Satyashodak Samaj (Truth-Seekers Society); opposed child marriage, supported widow remarriage; with wife Savitribai Phule; orphanages and widow homes; book Gulamgiri (Slavery).
  • Narayana Guru (1854–1928), Kerala – poet in Malayalam, Tamil, Sanskrit; Sri Narayana Dharma Paripalana (SNDP) Yogam; temple at Aruvipuram open to all; followers Kumaran Asan, Dr. Palpu.
  • Ayyankali – b. 1863, Venganoor (Thiruvananthapuram); Sadhu Jana Paripalana Sangam, 1907.

5.4 Islamic reforms

  • Sir Sayyid Ahmed Khan (b. Delhi) – modern education; scientific society; translated English science books into Urdu; advised loyalty to British.
  • Aligarh Movement – Mohammedan Anglo-Oriental College, Aligarh, 1875 → university 1920.

3.1 Sources for the study of early Tamil society

Four sources: (1) classical Tamil literature, (2) epigraphy (inscriptions), (3) archaeological excavations and material culture, (4) non-Tamil and foreign literature.

Classical Sangam literature

  • Corpus = Tholkappiyam + Pathinen Melkanakku (18 major) + Pathinen Kilkanakku (18 minor) + five epics.
  • Tholkappiyam (Tholkappiyar) – earliest written work on Tamil grammar; its third section describes poetic conventions → information on social life.
  • Pathinen Melkanakku = Pathupattu (10 idylls) + Ettuthogai (8 anthologies) – oldest classical texts. Kilkanakku = later.
  • Ettuthogai: Nattrinai, Kurunthogai, Paripaadal, Pathittrupathu, Aingurunuru, Kalithogai, Akanaanuru, Puranaanuru.
  • Pathupattu: Thirumurugatrupadai, Porunaratrupadai, Perumpanatruppadai, Sirupanatrupadai, Mullaipaattu, Nedunalvaadai, Maduraikanchi, Kurinjipaattu, Pattinappaalai, Malaipadukadam.
  • Pathinen Kilkanakku – 18 texts on ethics and morals; pre-eminent: Thirukkural by Thiruvalluvar – 1330 couplets on morality, statecraft, love.
  • Five epics (Kappiyams): Silappathikaaram, Manimekalai, Seevaka Chinthamani, Valaiyapathi, Kundalakesi.

Epigraphy

  • Epigraphy = study of inscriptions (on stone, copper plates, coins, rings). Development of script marks beginning of the historical period.
  • Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions in 30+ sites in TN, mostly on cave surfaces/rock shelters – abodes of mostly Jaina monks; a drip-line cut to keep rain out; inscriptions below drip-lines; stone beds for monks. Sites: Mangulam, Muttupatti, Pugalur, Arachalur, Kongarpuliyankulam, Jambai; many around Madurai, along trade routes.
  • Vandalism = destroying heritage/others' property (e.g., writing names on inscriptions).
  • Hero stones (Nadukal) – memorials to those who died in battles and cattle raids (cattle = wealth; Mullai = pastoral). Tholkappiyam describes the procedure for erecting them.
    • Sangam Age hero stones with Tamil-Brahmi: Pulimankombai and Thathapatti (Theni dist.), Porpanaikottai (Pudukkottai dist.) – no images/sculptures.
    • Pulimankombai – Vaigai valley, Theni; discovered 2006; inscription "Kudalur Akol pedu tiyan antavan kal" = stone of Tiyan Antavan killed in a cattle raid at Kudalur.
    • Post-Sangam and Pallava hero stones (with images, names) abound around Chengam, Tiruvannamalai district.
  • Inscribed pottery (names in Tamil-Brahmi, ownership): Arikkamedu, Azhagankulam, Kodumanal, Keezhadi; abroad at Berenike and Quseir al-Qadhim (Egypt) and Khor Rori (Oman) → trade with West Asia/Red Sea. Names in Tamil and some in Prakrit (language of common people of North India in Mauryan times). Ship motif on pottery from Azhagankulam.

Archaeological sites and material culture

  • Archaeological excavation = systematic digging to recover material evidence.
  • Early historic sites: Arikkamedu, Azhagankulam, Uraiyur, Kanchipuram, Kaveripoompattinam, Korkai, Vasavasamudram, Keezhadi, Kodumanal (TN) and Pattanam (Kerala).
  • Arikkamedu (near Puducherry) – Sangam port excavated by ASI; by Mortimer Wheeler (British), J.M. Casal (French), A. Ghosh and Krishna Deva; planned town, warehouse, streets, tanks, ring wells.
  • ASI = Central agency; TN has the Tamil Nadu State Department of Archaeology. Laws: Indian Treasure Trove Act 1878; Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958; Antiquities and Art Treasures Act 1972.
  • Finds: brick structures, beads, bangles, cameos (images carved raised on precious stone), intaglios (images carved recessed, below surface).
  • Coins: first introduced as medium of exchange in the Sangam Age; Chera, Chola, Pandya, punch-marked and Roman coins. Punch-marked coins (earliest coins in India, mostly silver) at Kodumanal and Bodinayakkanur. Roman coins concentrated in Coimbatore region; also Azhagankulam, Karur, Madurai; used as bullion (metal ingots) and ornaments.

  • Sangam Age = Early Historic period; marked off from prehistory by Sangam literature and Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions.

3.4 Society

  • Transition from tribe/chiefdom → kingdom. Clan communities: Panar, Paratavar, Eyinar, Uzhavar, Kanavar, Vettuvar, Maravar. Priests = Antanars. North Indian caste system did not take root; division by five eco-zones and occupations.
  • Women: mothers, heroines, foster-mothers, Panar women, dancers, poets, royals. Poetess Vennikkuyathiyar (village Venni). Women guarding Thinai fields; Umanar women selling salt; some died with husbands.

3.5 Economy

  • Mixed economy by eco-zone. Crops: paddy, sugarcane, millets; wet and dry farming. Rice varieties sennel (red), vennel (white), aivananel. Rice grains in burial urns at Adichanallur and Porunthal. Forest people: punam (shifting cultivation). Pastoralism = nomadic cattle/sheep/goat rearing.
  • Pottery by Kalamceyko (potters): black ware, russet-coated painted ware, black-and-red ware (Porunthal finds).
  • Iron smelting – furnaces with terracotta pipes at Kodumanal and Guttur.
  • Ornaments: poor – clay, terracotta, iron, leaves; rich – precious stones, copper, gold. Carnelian (etched) beads. Gold smelting at Pattanam (Kerala); gold ornaments at megalithic sites Suttukeni, Adichanallur, Kodumanal and towns Arikkamedu, Keezhadi, Pattanam; Roman gold coins made into jewellery.
  • Glass beads: industry at Arikkamedu and Kudikkadu (near Cuddalore).
  • Pearl fishery: Pamban coast; a pearl found at Keezhadi. Shell bangles – Parathavars collected conch from Pamban Island.
  • Textiles: spindle whorls and cloth at Kodumanal; fine cloth kalingam; Periplus mentions Tamil textiles.
  • Traders: vanikan and nigama (guild) in Tamil-Brahmi inscriptions; gold, cloth, salt merchants; salt merchants = Umanars (bullock carts with family).
  • Vessels: Kalam, Pahri, Odam, Toni, Teppam, Navai.
  • Barter primary (rice for fish; salt = equal measure of rice). Coin hoards of Cheras, Cholas, Pandyas, Malayaman.
  • Overseas trade: Roman ships used monsoon winds. Exports: spices (pepper), ivory, precious stones. Imports: gold, silver, copper, precious stones. Yavanar = Westerners (Greeks, Romans, West Asians) – from Greek Ionia.
  • Berenike (Red Sea, Egypt): Indian jar with 7.5 kg pepper, teak, Tamil-Brahmi potsherd. Quseir al-Qadhim: three Tamil-Brahmi names Panaiori, Kanan, Cattan.
  • Akanaanuru poem 149: Yavana ships came with gold, returned with pepper at Muciri.
  • Khuan Luk Pat, Thailand: touchstone inscribed "Perumpatankal" (goldsmith Perumpattan). SE Asia = Suvarna Bhumi.

3.6 Emergence of towns and ports

  • Sangam Age = first urbanisation in Tamilagam: brick buildings, roof tiles, ring wells, planned streets, store houses.
  • Ports: Arikkamedu, Kaveripoompattinam, Azhagankulam, Korkai (east coast); Pattanam (Kerala). Inland trade centres: Kanchipuram, Uraiyur, Karur, Madurai, Kodumanal.

3.7 Faith and belief; 3.8 Fine arts

  • Animism (anangu), ancestor worship, hero worship, many deities; Jainism (Tamil-Brahmi caves), Yagna, Buddhism in some centres.
  • Ritual dance Veriyatal; poetry, music, dance, cuisine; antimony rods (kohl sticks) of copper/bronze for eyebrows.

Thirukkural – the basic facts (from the Tamil and SS textbooks)

  • Author Thiruvalluvar; believed to be ~2000 years ago (Tamil textbooks); Class 11 Economics: generally placed in the Sangam age, ~3rd century CE; Class 11 History: written in the so-called Kalabhra period (post-Sangam) with the 18 minor works. Class 7 SS: Valluvar "believed to be a Jain scholar"; Class 8 SS: Thirukkural and Naladiyar listed as Jain contributions.
  • One of the Pathinen Kilkanakku (18 minor/didactic works on ethics) – the pre-eminent one.
  • Muppal (3 parts): Arathuppal 38 + Porutpal 70 + Inbathuppal 25 = 133 adhikarams × 10 = 1330 kurals.
  • Iyals: Aram – Payiraviyal, Illaraviyal, Thuravaraviyal, Oozhiyal (4); Porul – Arasiyal, Amaichiyal, Ozhibiyal (3); Inbam – Kalaviyal, Karpiyal (2). (Class 11 Pol. Sci. splits Porutpal's 70 chapters as: Politics/Arasu 25, Ministry 10, Defence/Fort 2, Wealth 1, Army 2, Friendship 17, Citizens/Kudi 13 – "7 parts".)
  • Other names of the Kural: Ulaga Podhumarai, Muppal, Podhumarai, Poyyamozhi, Vayurai Vazhthu, Deiva Nool, Tamil Marai, Mudhumozhi, Porulurai.
  • Names of Valluvar: Nayanar, Devar, Mudharpavalar, Deiva Pulavar, Nanmuganar, Madhanupangi, Sennappodhar, Perunavalar.
  • First Tamil book with the prefix "Thiru" (Class 7 Tamil). Begins with அ (akaram) and ends with ன் (nakara ottru).
  • 10 ancient commentators: Dharumar, Manakkudavar (the first commentator), Thamaththar, Nachar, Parithi, Parimelazhagar (considered best), Thirumalaiyar, Mallar, Paripperumal, Kalingar. Anthology praising it = Thiruvalluva Malai.
  • First printed 1812 (Class 10 SS p. 122 and Class 9 Tamil); first printed original text by Thanjai Gnanaprakasar. Only chapter appearing twice: Kurippu Arithal. Word "kodi" in 7 places; "ezhu" in 8 kurals.
  • First complete English translation: G.U. Pope (1886 – extra). Translated into 100+ languages (Tamil textbooks); Class 12 Pol. Sci. box: "perhaps the only ancient secular text from India translated into 60 languages". (extra: Veeramamunivar (Beschi) translated Aram and Porul into Latin.)
  • Gandhi started learning Tamil in South Africa; was attracted by G.U. Pope's Tamil handbook and greatly by the Thirukkural (Class 6 Tamil T3).
  • Thirukkural Munusamy (V. Munisamy) – spread the Kural through classes and talks (Class 7 Tamil T2).

1. Significance as secular literature

  • Class 11 Pol. Sci. box "Thirukkural – a truly secular ethical work": in Valluvar's time there were Buddhists, Jains, Hindus, agnostics, atheists, but "secularism" as a concept was unknown. Valluvar was a believer – one chapter (Kadavul Vazhthu) praises God – yet he advocates no religion and does not even refer to any religion.
  • Class 9 Tamil: it does not foreground any race, caste or country → hence Ulaga Podhumarai (universal scripture). "Thirukkural – the Tamil people's contribution to world culture".
  • Its God is named only by attributes: "அகர முதல எழுத்தெல்லாம் ஆதி பகவன் முதற்றே உலகு" (Kural 1 – extra) – no sectarian deity named.
  • Claimed by Jains, Saivites, Vaishnavites, Buddhists, Christians alike – a sign of its non-sectarian character (extra).

2. Universal values – equality and humanism

  • Kural 972: "பிறப்பொக்கும் எல்லா உயிர்க்கும் சிறப்பொவ்வா செய்தொழில் வேற்றுமை யான்" – "All human beings are equal by birth; they differ in characteristics because of the different qualities of their actions" (Class 11 Pol. Sci.).
  • Contrast stressed by the textbook: Manu, Plato and Aristotle held that human beings were not born equal; Valluvar held a radically different view.
  • Periyar replaced "giving in marriage" with "valkaithunai" (life companion) – a word from the Thirukkural (Class 10 SS p. 126) → gender equality.
  • Sharing and compassion: "பகுத்துண்டு பல்லுயிர் ஓம்புதல்" – share food and protect all lives (Kural 322 – extra). Purity of mind = all virtue: "மனத்துக்கண் மாசிலன் ஆதல் அனைத்தறன்" (Kural 34 – extra).

3. Relevance to socio-politico affairs – Valluvar's political thought (Class 11 Pol. Sci.)

  • Teacher's box (p. 13): Thiruvalluvar is considered one of the first political thinkers of India; Kautilya's Arthasastra (c. 300 BCE) concentrated on political economy, the Kural on political governance. Book's MCQ answer: "best political thinker in Tamil Nadu" = Thiruvalluvar.
  • In Arathupal Valluvar is an enunciator of morals; in Inbathupal a poet; in Porutpal a political philosopher.
  • Six components of a state – Kural 381: "படை குடி கூழ் அமைச்சு நட்பு அரண் ஆறும் உடையான் அரசருள் ஏறு" – army, people, wealth (food), minister, friends, fort; who has all six is a lion among kings.
  • Nature of a king – 25 chapters, from Iraimatchi (ch. 39, Qualities of a ruler) to Idukkan Azhiyamai (ch. 63, unfazed in trouble); king named 46 times (Arasar, Vendhar, Nilan Aandavar, Mannavar).
  • Qualities of king (Kurals 382, 383): courage, liberality, wisdom & energy, knowledge, firm decisions. Kural 734: a kingdom should be free from excessive starvation, irremediable epidemics and destructive foes.
  • Welfare state – Kural 388: "முறைசெய்து காப்பாற்றும் மன்னவன் மக்கட்கு இறையென்று வைக்கப் படும்" – the king who rules justly and protects is regarded as God by the people.
  • Features of state – Kural 384: "அறனிழுக்கா தல்லவை நீக்கி மறனிழுக்கா மானம் உடைய தரசு" – uphold virtue, remove vice, courage and honour.
  • Ideal rule – Kural 542: "வானோக்கி வாழும் உலகெல்லாம் மன்னவன் கோல்நோக்கி வாழுங் குடி" – as the world looks to rain, the people look to the king's sceptre ("Kol" = ideal rule). Kural 544: "குடிதழீஇக் கோலோச்சும் மாநில மன்னன் அடிதழீஇ நிற்கும் உலகு" – the world embraces the feet of the king who embraces his people.
  • Against tyranny – chapter Kodungonmai (Cruel Sceptre); Kural 555: "அல்லற்பட்டு ஆற்றாது அழுதகண் ணீரன்றே செல்வத்தைத் தேய்க்கும் படை" – the tears of suffering people are the weapon that wears away the ruler's wealth (tears equated with an army). Book: the Kural was a "funeral sound" for Hitler, Mussolini, the Czar.
  • Government budget – Kural 385: "இயற்றலும் ஈட்டலும் காத்தலும் காத்த வகுத்தலும் வல்ல தரசு" – four duties: Iyatral (create/identify sources of revenue), Eettal (collect), Kaathal (protect), Vaguththal (allocate for welfare) – accepted as a basic idea of a socialistic society.
  • Relevance today: ideals apply to democratic governance; educational qualities ("Kalvi", "Idukkan Ariyamai") meant for all people; "People's welfare" is the foundation of his politics.

4. Relevance to economic affairs (Class 11 Economics 7.8.1)

  • Economic ideas mostly in Porutpal; his work is marked by pragmatic idealism; he is a "fundamental thinker".
  • Rain = basis of stable economic life (chapter Vaan Sirappu): "It is rain that both ruins and aids the ruined to rise".
  • Factors of production: land, labour, capital, organisation, time, technology – "unfailing harvest, competent men, men of undiminishing wealth are the components of an economy" (book prints "Kural 61"; the couplet is Kural 731 "தள்ளா விளையுளும் தக்காரும் தாழ்விலாச் செல்வரும் சேர்வது நாடு" – extra).
  • Agriculture = most fundamental activity; farmers are the linch-pin of the world (Kural 1032); "the ploughmen alone live as freemen; the rest follow" (Kural 1033 "உழுதுண்டு வாழ்வாரே வாழ்வார்…" – extra).
  • Public finance: creation, collection, management of revenue, public expenditure (= Kural 385's four steps).
  • Balanced budget: limited revenue is no misfortune if expenditure is kept within bounds – Kural 478 "ஆகாறு அளவிட்டிது ஆயினும் கேடில்லை போகாறு அகலாக் கடை" (quoted at the head of Class 12 Economics ch. 9 "Fiscal Economics"). "Budget for a surplus, if possible… never budget for a deficit."
  • Main public expenditure: defence, public works, social services.
  • Against external assistance – Kural 739: countries depending on outside help are not countries at all → self-sufficient economy.
  • Poverty = root of all evils; freedom from hunger = a fundamental freedom. Wealth = a means, not an end; acquire by honourable means; condemned hoarding; industry is real wealth, labour the greatest resource.
  • Welfare state elements: health without disease, abundant wealth, good crops, prosperity and happiness, full security.

5. Relevance to everyday life, impact on humanity, philosophical content

  • Everyday life: Arathupal's Illaraviyal deals with household life – love, hospitality (Virunthombal), gratitude, patience, non-envy, truthfulness; "there is nothing it does not contain, nothing it does not say" (இல்லாததும் இல்லை, சொல்லாததும் இல்லை – Class 6 Tamil). Education: "கற்க கசடறக் கற்பவை கற்றபின் நிற்க அதற்குத் தக" (Kural 391 – extra).
  • Practical use today: school syllabus (108 chapters, 2017); court bail condition (Mettupalayam); Kural quoted at the head of an economics chapter; Periyar's "valkaithunai".

Unit 1 · Advent of the Europeans – sources of modern India

  • Portuguese, Dutch, French, Danes, English kept state papers; archives at Lisbon, Goa, Pondicherry, Madras. Sources: written (literature, travel accounts, diaries, autobiographies, pamphlets, govt documents, manuscripts) and material (paintings, statues, buildings, coins, museums).
  • Ananda Rangam (Ananda Ranga Pillai) – Dubash (translator) at Pondicherry for French trade; diaries 1736–1760 – the only written secular record of that period ("a name to conjure with in the annals of Tamil history").
  • National Archives of India (NAI), New Delhi – one of the largest in Asia; George William Forrest = "Father of NAI".
  • Tamil Nadu Archives (TNA) = former Madras Record Office, Chennai – one of the oldest/largest in South India; mostly English; also Dutch, Danish, Persian, Marathi (few French, Portuguese, Tamil, Urdu). 1642 volumes of Dutch records (Cochin & Coromandel, 1657–1845); Danish records 1777–1845. Dodwell published the first calendar of Madras records, 1917.
  • Buildings: St. Francis Church (Cochin), St. Louis Fort (Pondicherry), St. George Fort (Madras), St. David Fort (Cuddalore, built 1690). National Museum, Delhi (largest, 1949).
  • Coins/notes: first coinage under the Crown 1862; RBI 1935; first RBI note Jan 1938 – ₹5 with George VI.

Portuguese

  • 1453 Turks captured Constantinople → land route closed → search for sea routes.
  • Prince Henry "the Navigator"; Bartholomew Diaz reached southern tip of Africa 1487 (patron King John II).
  • Vasco da Gama – via Mozambique with an Indian pilot → Calicut 1498, received by Zamorin. Pedro Alvares Cabral 1500 (13 ships) – conflict with Zamorin. Da Gama's 2nd voyage 1501 (20 ships) – factory at Cannanore; then Calicut, Cochin. Cochin = first capital of the Portuguese. 3rd voyage 1524; died at Cochin, Dec 1524.
  • Francisco de Almeida (1505–09) – first Governor; "Blue Water Policy" (naval power). Lost at Chaul (son killed) to Egypt–Turkey–Gujarat fleet; won at Diu (1509) → naval supremacy.
  • Alfonso de Albuquerque (1509–15) – real founder of Portuguese power; captured Goa from Bijapur, Nov 1510; Ormuz 1515; encouraged marriages with Indian women; friendly with Vijayanagar.
  • Nino de Cunha (1529–38) – capital Cochin → Goa, 1530; Bassein 1534 (from Bahadur Shah of Gujarat); Diu 1537; Salsette 1548.
  • Possessions: Goa, Daman, Diu, Salsette, Bassein, Chaul, Bombay; Hooghly; Santhome. Brought tobacco; printing press at Goa 1556; book on Indian medicinal plants printed at Goa 1563. By 1739 only Goa, Diu, Daman.

Dutch

  • United East India Company of the Netherlands, 1602; first factory Masulipatnam 1605; Amboyna 1605 (Spice Islands). HQ Pulicat → Nagapattinam (1690).
  • Trade: silk, cotton, indigo, rice, opium; monopoly in black pepper. Factories: Pulicat, Surat, Chinsura, Kasimbazar, Patna, Nagapattinam, Balasore, Cochin.
  • Amboyna massacre 1623 (10 English + 9 Javanese killed). Defeated by English at Bedera, 1759; wiped out by 1795.
  • Dutch in TN: Portuguese at Pulicat since 1502; Dutch settlement at Pulicat 1610; Fort Geldria 1613; diamonds exported. Others: Nagapattinam, Punnakayal, Porto Novo, Cuddalore, Devanampatinam.

British

  • Charter by Queen Elizabeth, 31 Dec 1600; Governor + court of 24 directors.
  • Captain Hawkins at Jahangir's court 1608. Thomas Best defeated Portuguese near Surat 1612 → factory at Surat 1613 (Jahangir). Nicholas Downton victory 1614. Sir Thomas Roe (sent by James I) at Jahangir's court 1615 – commercial treaty; centres Surat, Agra, Ahmedabad, Broach.
  • East coast: first factory Masulipatam 1611 (Golconda).
  • 1639 Francis Day got Madras on lease from Chennappa Nayaka (ruler of Chandragiri) → Fort St. George = first fort built by the British.
  • Bombay: dowry of Catherine to Charles II; to Company 1668 at £10/year.
  • Job Charnock – Sutanuti factory 1690; zamindari of Sutanuti, Kalikata, Govindpur 1698 → Calcutta; fortified 1696; named Fort William 1700.
  • Plassey 1757, Buxar 1764 → political power; Company rule till 1858, then Crown.

Danish, French, Swedish

  • Danish East India Company – charter by Christian IV, 17 March 1616; Tranquebar 1620 (called Danesborg), Serampore 1676 (HQ); sold all to British 1845. King of Denmark sent Ziegenbalg – printing press at Tranquebar.
  • French East India Company 1664 – by Colbert (minister of Louis XIV). Francois Caron 1667 → first factory Surat; Marcara – Masulipatam 1669 (Golconda). Pondicherry 1673 by (Francois) Martin (grant from Sher Khan Lodi of Bijapur) – Fort St. Louis. Chandernagore 1673 (Shaista Khan). Also Mahe, Karaikal, Balasore, Kasimbazar. France = last European country to come as traders. Dupleix Governor 1742 (succeeded Dumas).
  • Swedish East India Company, Gothenburg, 1731.
  • British success: commercial competitiveness, govt support, naval superiority, national character.

Vellore Revolt 1806 (Class 8 version – note differences from Class 10)

  • Tipu's family + ~3,000 ex-servants/soldiers of Hyder and Tipu near Vellore; fort garrison partly recruited in Tirunelveli after 1800.
  • William Cavendish Bentinck Governor of Madras 1803; regulations 1805–06 enforced by C-in-C Sir John Cradock: shave beard, trim moustache, no religious marks or earrings.
  • Immediate cause: June 1806 – new turban like a European hat with a cross badge = "Agnew's turban" (Military General Agnew).
  • 10 July: 1st and 23rd Regiments; first victim Col. Fancourt; Fateh Hyder proclaimed ruler; Tipu's tiger-striped flag hoisted. Major Cootes rode to Ranipet to inform Col. Gillespie (Class 10: Gillespie at Arcot). 113 Europeans and ~350 sepoys killed (Class 8 figure).
  • Effects: regulations withdrawn; Tipu's family sent to Calcutta; Bentinck removed. Failure: no leadership, poor organisation, divide and rule.
  • V.D. Savarkar called the Vellore revolt the prelude to the First War of Indian Independence (1857).

Unit 6 · Development of Industries in India

  • Edward Baines: "The birthplace of cotton manufacture is India". Bernier (Shah Jahan's reign) marvelled at manufactures; French traveller Tavernier admired the peacock throne.
  • Handicrafts = second-largest rural employment after agriculture. Centres: Saurashtra – bell metal, Vanga (Bengal) – tin, Dacca – muslin (Egyptian mummies of 2000 BCE wrapped in Indian muslin; 50 m fits in a matchbox).
  • Decline: loss of royal patronage; India made a raw-material exporter; machine-made goods (cotton gin, flying shuttle, spinning jenny, steam engine); one-way free trade and protective tariffs; de-industrialisation; Suez Canal cheapened British goods. Dadabhai Naoroji – Drain Theory.
  • Modern industry: Assam Tea Company 1839; coffee in South; jute. Cotton textile mill, Bombay 1854 (organised modern industry begins); jute at Rishra (Hooghly) 1855; first paper mill, Ballygunj 1870; woollen & leather – Kanpur. Steel by modern methods at Kulti 1874; TISCO, Jamshedpur 1907 (pig iron 1911, steel ingots 1912) – Jamshedji Tata.
  • Railways 2,573 km (1861) → 55,773 km (1914); Suez Canal cut distance by ~4,830 km; Swadeshi: cotton mills 194 → 273, jute 36 → 64.
  • CII (1985) – non-govt, not-for-profit, 9,000+ members.

Unit 7 · Urban Changes during the British Period

  • Ancient towns: Harappa, Mohenjodaro, Varanasi, Allahabad, Madurai. Medieval (fort/port cities): Delhi, Hyderabad, Jaipur, Lucknow, Agra, Nagpur. European coastal towns: Surat, Daman, Goa, Pondicherry.

8. Last-minute "firsts" of Tamil Nadu

  • First Indian judge of Madras HC – T. Muthuswami (1877).
  • Earliest South Indian public-interest body – Madras Native Association 1852; first with clear nationalist aims – Madras Mahajana Sabha 1884.
  • First CM of Madras Presidency – A. Subbarayalu (1920).
  • First trade union in India – Madras Labour Union 1918; first May Day – 1923.
  • First legislature to give women the vote – Madras 1921.
  • First non-European language in print – Tamil (Thambiran Vanakkam, Goa, 1578); Thirukkural printed 1812.
  • First fort built by the British – Fort St. George (23 April 1640); first municipal corporation – Madras 1688.
  • First Tamil paper with political cartoons – Bharati's India.
