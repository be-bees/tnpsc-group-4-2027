Gap 1 · Exponent laws in one step – zero power, negative power, equal bases, reciprocal (with Day 1)

Asked: 2016 Q191, 2018 Q116, 2018 Q126

Source: Class 8 Maths Ch 1 Numbers – §1.3.5 Multiplicative inverse (PDF p. 22, exercise p. 25) and laws of exponents (PDF p. 46–48). The laws themselves are in Topic 1; this section is the "solve for the power" method and the zero/reciprocal facts that past papers asked as one-liners.

Method

  • Same base on both sides → equate the powers. am × an = ap ⇒ m + n = p. Do not expand the numbers.
  • If bases differ, rewrite both as powers of one prime (4 = 2², 8 = 2³, 25 = 5², 1/27 = 3−3).
  • (a/b)−n = (b/a)n – a negative power flips the fraction.
  • Zero exponent rule: a0 = 1 for every non-zero a (Class 8 p. 46): 70 = 1, e0 = 1, π0 = 1, (−5)0 = 1. But 0n = 0 for n ≥ 1.
  • Reciprocal (multiplicative inverse): two numbers whose product is 1; reciprocal of a/b is b/a (Class 8 p. 22). 0 has no reciprocal (1/0 is not defined; Class 8 Ex 1.2 asks "the rational number ___ does not have a reciprocal", PDF p. 24) – so "the multiplicative inverse exists for all rational numbers" is false. 1 and −1 are their own reciprocals (extra). The reciprocal keeps the sign: reciprocal of −3/2 is −2/3.

Formulas

RuleUse
am · an = am+n; am ÷ an = am−n; (am)n = amncombine one side into a single power
ax = ay (a ≠ 0, 1, −1) ⇒ x = yequate the powers
a0 = 1 (a ≠ 0); a−m = 1/am; (a/b)−m = (b/a)mzero and negative powers
a × (1/a) = 1 (a ≠ 0)reciprocal; none for 0

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2016 If (7/12)−4 × (7/12)3x = (7/12)5, then x = ? (a) −1 (b) 1 (c) 2 (d) 3
    Same base: −4 + 3x = 5 → 3x = 9 → x = 3 → (d).
  2. PYQ 2018 The value of e0 is (a) e (b) 1 (c) 0 (d) ∞
    Any non-zero number to the power 0 is 1; e ≈ 2.718 is non-zero → 1 → (b).
  3. PYQ 2018 Reciprocal of 0 is (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) ∞ (d) no reciprocal
    No number × 0 gives 1 → no reciprocal → (d).
  4. Solve 4x = 8x−1.
    22x = 23(x−1) → 2x = 3x − 3 → x = 3 (check: 4³ = 64 = 8²).
  5. Solve 5x−2 × 25 = 125.
    5x−2 × 5² = 5³ → x − 2 + 2 = 3 → x = 3.
  6. Find (2/3)−3 and (2−1 + 3−1)−1.
    (3/2)³ = 27/8; (1/2 + 1/3)−1 = (5/6)−1 = 6/5 (add first, then flip).

Quick test

  1. (3/5)−2 = (a) 9/25 (b) 25/9 (c) −9/25 (d) 6/10
  2. If 32x−1 = 243, then x = (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 5/2 (d) 4
  3. 70 + 07 = (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) 7 (d) 8
  4. The multiplicative inverse of −3/2 is (a) 3/2 (b) −2/3 (c) 2/3 (d) −3/2
  5. Which number is its own reciprocal? (a) 0 (b) 2 (c) −1 (d) 1/2
Show answers

Answers: 1-b ((5/3)² = 25/9), 2-b (243 = 3⁵ → 2x − 1 = 5), 3-b (1 + 0), 4-b (sign stays, fraction flips), 5-c (−1 × −1 = 1; 0 has no reciprocal)

Gap 2 · Square roots – nested radicals, infinite radicals, perfect-square digit tests (with Day 1)

Asked: 2016 Q142, 2024 Q141, 2016 Q138

Source: square roots by prime factors and the last-digit rule are in Topic 1 (Class 8 §1.6–1.7). Surds: Class 9 Maths Ch 2 §2.6 (PDF p. 66–74). The nested / infinite radical shortcuts below are standard aptitude methods (extra), each checked by computing the value.

Method

  • Finite nested root √(a + √(b + √(c + …))): start at the innermost root and work outwards; every stage gives a perfect square in exam questions.
  • Infinite product root x = √(a√(a√(a…))): the part under the first root repeats, so x = √(a·x) → x² = ax → x = a.
  • Infinite sum root x = √(a + √(a + …)) → x² = a + x. If a = n(n + 1) then x = n + 1. Infinite difference root √(a − √(a − …)) → x² = a − x → x = n.
  • Finite product root with k radicals: √(a√(a√a)) (3 roots) = a7/8; in general a(2k − 1)/2k.
  • Fill-the-digit perfect squares: a square ending in 5 always ends in 25, and the digits before 25 equal n(n + 1) where the root is "n5": 35² = 12|25 (3 × 4 = 12), 55² = 30|25 (5 × 6 = 30), 85² = 72|25 (8 × 9 = 72) (extra).
  • Quick rejects (extra): a perfect square never ends in 2, 3, 7, 8 (Topic 1); its digit sum reduces to 1, 4, 7 or 9.

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2016 √(609 + √(248 + √(60 + √(7 + √81)))) = ? (a) 20 (b) 25 (c) 16 (d) 9
    √81 = 9 → √(7 + 9) = 4 → √(60 + 4) = 8 → √(248 + 8) = 16 → √(609 + 16) = √625 = 25 → (b).
  2. PYQ 2024 √(5√(5√(5…))) = ? (a) 5³ (b) 5⁰ (c) 5² (d) 5¹
    x = √(5x) → x² = 5x → x = 5 = 5¹ → (d).
  3. PYQ 2016 Which digit in the blank makes 3_25 a perfect square? (a) 1 (b) 0 (c) 4 (d) 6
    Ends in 25, so the front part "3_" must be n(n + 1): 30 = 5 × 6 → 3025 = 55² → 0 → (b). (3125, 3425, 3625 are not squares.)
  4. √(12 + √(12 + √(12 + …))) = ?
    12 = 3 × 4 → value 4 (check: 4² = 12 + 4).
  5. Simplify √(2√(2√2)).
    3 roots → 27/8 (= 21/2 · 21/4 · 21/8).
  6. Which is not a perfect square: 1521, 2025, 3136, 4357?
    4357 ends in 7 → not a square. (1521 = 39², 2025 = 45², 3136 = 56².)

Quick test

  1. √(30 + √(30 + √(30 + …))) = (a) 5 (b) 6 (c) 7 (d) 30
  2. √(7√(7√(7…))) = (a) √7 (b) 49 (c) 7 (d) 1
  3. √(10 + √(25 + √(108 + √(154 + √225)))) = (a) 4 (b) 6 (c) 8 (d) 10
  4. The digit in the blank that makes 7_25 a perfect square is (a) 0 (b) 2 (c) 4 (d) 6
  5. √(6 − √(6 − √(6 − …))) = (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 6 (d) −3
Show answers

Answers: 1-b (30 = 5 × 6 → 6), 2-c (x² = 7x), 3-a (15 → 13 → 11 → 6 → 4), 4-b (72 = 8 × 9 → 7225 = 85²), 5-a (6 = 2 × 3 → 2)

Gap 3 · Algebraic identities for fast simplification (with Day 1)

Asked: 2016 Q145, 2016 Q146, 2018 Q124

Source: Class 9 Maths Ch 3 Algebra §3.4 Algebraic identities (PDF p. 105–108; a + b + c = 0 ⇒ a³ + b³ + c³ = 3abc is used in an example on PDF p. 107). The basic identities are listed in Topic 1; this section is about spotting them in number questions and the x ± 1/x family.

Method

  • Look at the shape before multiplying: a·a + 2·a·b + b·b is (a + b)²; a·a − b·b is (a + b)(a − b); then cancel.
  • Three cubes with a + b + c = 0 (e.g. 16 + 7 − 23 = 0) → answer is 3abc, no cubing needed.
  • Given x ± 1/x = k: square it for x² + 1/x², cube it for x³ ± 1/x³ (the product x · 1/x = 1 makes the cross terms constants).
  • Fractions of the type (a³ + b³)/(a² − ab + b²) or (a³ − b³)/(a² + ab + b²) reduce to a + b or a − b.

Formulas

GivenResult
(a + b)²/(a² − b²)(a + b)/(a − b)
a + b + c = 0a³ + b³ + c³ = 3abc
a³ + b³ + c³ − 3abc(a + b + c)(a² + b² + c² − ab − bc − ca)
x + 1/x = kx² + 1/x² = k² − 2; x³ + 1/x³ = k³ − 3k
x − 1/x = kx² + 1/x² = k² + 2; x³ − 1/x³ = k³ + 3k
(a³ ± b³)/(a² ∓ ab + b²)a ± b

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2016 (1.75 × 1.75 + 2 × 1.75 × 0.75 + 0.75 × 0.75)/(1.75 × 1.75 − 0.75 × 0.75) = ? (a) 3.5 (b) 6.25 (c) 1 (d) 2.5
    (a + b)²/(a² − b²) = (a + b)/(a − b) = 2.5/1 = 2.5 → (d).
  2. PYQ 2016 16³ + 7³ − 23³ = ? (a) −7728 (b) 7028 (c) 7728 (d) −7718
    a = 16, b = 7, c = −23, a + b + c = 0 → 3abc = 3 × 16 × 7 × (−23) = −7728 → (a).
  3. PYQ 2018 If y − 1/y = 6, then y³ − 1/y³ = ? (a) 216 (b) 222 (c) 234 (d) 228
    k³ + 3k = 216 + 18 = 234 → (c).
  4. If x + 1/x = 5, find x² + 1/x² and x³ + 1/x³.
    25 − 2 = 23; 125 − 15 = 110.
  5. (0.87³ + 0.13³)/(0.87² − 0.87 × 0.13 + 0.13²) = ?
    a + b = 0.87 + 0.13 = 1.

Quick test

  1. If x + 1/x = 4, then x² + 1/x² = (a) 14 (b) 16 (c) 18 (d) 12
  2. 25³ − 15³ − 10³ = (a) 11250 (b) −11250 (c) 3750 (d) 0
  3. (3.5² − 1.5²)/(3.5 − 1.5) = (a) 2 (b) 5 (c) 3.5 (d) 7
  4. If x − 1/x = 3, then x³ − 1/x³ = (a) 27 (b) 30 (c) 36 (d) 18
  5. (1.2³ + 0.8³)/(1.2² − 1.2 × 0.8 + 0.8²) = (a) 0.4 (b) 2 (c) 1 (d) 2.4
Show answers

Answers: 1-a (16 − 2), 2-a (25 − 15 − 10 = 0 → 3 × 25 × (−15) × (−10)), 3-b (a + b), 4-c (27 + 9), 5-b (a + b)

Gap 4 · Equations from word problems – linear and simple quadratic (with Day 1)

Asked: 2016 Q144, 2018 Q117, 2018 Q169, 2019 Q138

Source: Class 8 Maths Ch 3 Algebra §3.8 Linear equation in one variable (PDF p. 103–105): "an equation containing only one variable with its highest power as one is called a linear equation in one variable" (also called a simple equation).

Method

  • Call the quantity asked x; write every other quantity in terms of x; one sentence → one equation.
  • Expand brackets, bring x-terms to one side and numbers to the other, divide. Keep the sign when moving a term across "=".
  • Fractions in the statement ("one-third of …") → take x as a multiple of the denominator, or multiply the whole equation by the LCM.
  • Two unknowns with a square (x² − y², xy) → test the options in both conditions; it is faster than solving the quadratic.
  • A linear equation ax + b = 0 (a ≠ 0) has exactly one (a unique) solution x = −b/a (extra – basic algebra).

Formulas

WordsWrite
first is twice the second; third is one-third of the firstsecond = x, first = 2x, third = 2x/3
three consecutive integers / consecutive odd (even) numbersx, x + 1, x + 2 / x, x + 2, x + 4
difference of squares is d; product is px² − y² = d; xy = p
ratio a : b of two numbers whose sum is Sak + bk = S
ax + b = 0x = −b/a (one solution)

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2016 If 3(t − 3) = 5(2t + 1), then t = ? (a) −2 (b) 2 (c) −3 (d) 3
    3t − 9 = 10t + 5 → −7t = 14 → t = −2 → (a).
  2. PYQ 2018 How many solutions does a linear equation in one variable have? (a) three (b) unique solution (c) two (d) no solution
    ax + b = 0 → x = −b/a only → unique solution → (b).
  3. PYQ 2018 The sum of three numbers is 264. The first is twice the second and the third is one-third of the first. The second number is (a) 48 (b) 72 (c) 54 (d) 64
    Second = x, first = 2x, third = 2x/3: 2x + x + 2x/3 = 264 → 11x/3 = 264 → x = 72 → (b) (numbers 144, 72, 48).
  4. PYQ 2019 The difference of the squares of two positive numbers is 45. The square of the smaller number is four times the larger. The numbers are (a) 3, 15 (b) 9, 5 (c) 3, 16 (d) 9, 6
    Test options: 9² − 6² = 81 − 36 = 45 ✓ and 6² = 36 = 4 × 9 ✓ → (d). (Algebra: y² = 4x → x² − 4x − 45 = 0 → (x − 9)(x + 5) = 0 → x = 9, y = 6.)
  5. A number increased by 12 equals three times the number decreased by 8. Find it.
    x + 12 = 3x − 8 → 2x = 20 → x = 10.

Quick test

  1. If 5(x − 2) = 3(x + 4), then x = (a) 9 (b) 11 (c) 10 (d) 12
  2. The sum of three consecutive integers is 72. The largest is (a) 23 (b) 24 (c) 25 (d) 26
  3. The sum of three numbers is 98. The ratio of the first to the second is 2 : 3 and of the second to the third is 5 : 8. The second number is (a) 20 (b) 30 (c) 48 (d) 58
  4. The product of two consecutive positive integers is 132. They are (a) 10, 11 (b) 11, 12 (c) 12, 13 (d) 13, 14
  5. If 7x − 3 = 4x + 9, then x = (a) 2 (b) 3 (c) 4 (d) 6
Show answers

Answers: 1-b (2x = 22), 2-c (23, 24, 25), 3-b (10 : 15 : 24, sum 49 → × 2 → 20, 30, 48), 4-b (11 × 12 = 132), 5-c (3x = 12)

Gap 5 · HCF and LCM of algebraic expressions and polynomials (with Day 8)

Asked: 2025 Q193, 2019 Q193, 2016 Q200, 2018 Q121

Source: Class 10 Maths Ch 3 Algebra §3.3 GCD and LCM of polynomials, §3.3.3 relationship between LCM and GCD (PDF p. 99–103; 2025 Q193 is Example 3.10 on PDF p. 100); Class 9 Maths Ch 3 §3.7 GCD (PDF p. 120–121).

Method

  • Monomials (a³b⁴, ab⁵ …): HCF = HCF of the numbers × each common letter to its lowest power; LCM = LCM of the numbers × every letter to its highest power.
  • Polynomials – factorisation method (Class 10 p. 101): break each into factors (take out common factor, a² − b², a³ ± b³, split the middle term, factor theorem); HCF = common factors with lowest powers; LCM = all factors with highest powers × LCM of the numerical coefficients.
  • Long-division (Euclid) method (Class 10 p. 99): divide the polynomial with the bigger leading coefficient by the other; then divide the divisor by the remainder (drop any constant factor such as −7); repeat until the remainder is 0 – the last divisor is the GCD.
  • Exam shortcut: find a root of each cubic by trying x = ±1, ±2, ±3, ±1/2, ±3/2; divide it out; the quadratic left over is usually the common factor.
  • Product rule: for two polynomials, f(x) × g(x) = GCD × LCM (Class 10 §3.3.3, p. 103). For two different natural numbers LCM > HCF; in general HCF ≤ LCM and HCF divides LCM.

Formulas

ItemHCF (GCD)LCM

Factorisations to know: a² − 1 = (a − 1)(a + 1); x³ − 1 = (x − 1)(x² + x + 1); x³ + 1 = (x + 1)(x² − x + 1); x² + 2x + 1 = (x + 1)²; x⁴ − 1 = (x² + 1)(x + 1)(x − 1).

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2016 LCM of a³b⁴, ab⁵ and a²b⁷ is (a) a⁷b³ (b) a³b⁷ (c) a²b⁵ (d) ab⁵
    Highest powers: a³, b⁷ → a³b⁷ → (b). (HCF would be ab⁴.)
  2. PYQ 2025 HCF of x³ + x² − x + 2 and 2x³ − 5x² + 5x − 3 is (a) x² − x + 1 (b) x² + x − 1 (c) −7x² + 7x − 7 (d) 7x² − 7x + 7
    x = −2 makes the first zero (−8 + 4 + 2 + 2 = 0) → x³ + x² − x + 2 = (x + 2)(x² − x + 1). x = 3/2 makes the second zero → 2x³ − 5x² + 5x − 3 = (2x − 3)(x² − x + 1). Common factor x² − x + 1 → (a). Textbook long division: 2x³ − 5x² + 5x − 3 − 2(x³ + x² − x + 2) = −7x² + 7x − 7 = −7(x² − x + 1); drop −7; x³ + x² − x + 2 = (x + 2)(x² − x + 1), remainder 0. Options (c), (d) are traps – a GCD is taken without the constant factor.
  3. PYQ 2019 LCM of 3(a − 1), 2(a − 1)², (a² − 1) is (a) (a + 1)²(a + 1) (b) (a − 1)²(a + 1) (c) 6(a − 1)(a + 1)² (d) 6(a − 1)²(a + 1)
    a² − 1 = (a − 1)(a + 1). Numbers: LCM(3, 2, 1) = 6; factors: (a − 1)², (a + 1) → 6(a − 1)²(a + 1) → (d).
  4. PYQ 2018 Correct relation between GCD and LCM of two different numbers: I GCD = LCM, II GCD ≤ LCM, III LCM ≤ GCD, IV LCM > GCD
    GCD divides both numbers, LCM is a multiple of both; for two different numbers they cannot be equal → LCM > GCD (IV).
  5. LCM of 8x⁴y² and 48x²y⁴ (Class 10 Example 3.12, p. 102).
    LCM(8, 48) = 48; x⁴, y⁴ → 48x⁴y⁴.
  6. HCF and LCM of x² − 5x + 6 and x² − 4.
    (x − 2)(x − 3) and (x − 2)(x + 2) → HCF x − 2, LCM (x − 2)(x − 3)(x + 2). Check: HCF × LCM = (x − 2)²(x − 3)(x + 2) = product of the two.

Quick test

  1. HCF of 12x³y² and 18x²y⁵ is (a) 6x³y⁵ (b) 6x²y² (c) 36x³y⁵ (d) 3x²y²
  2. LCM of x² − 1 and x² + 2x + 1 is (a) (x + 1)(x − 1) (b) (x + 1)²(x − 1) (c) (x + 1)² (d) (x − 1)²(x + 1)
  3. HCF of x³ − 1 and x² − 1 is (a) x + 1 (b) x² + x + 1 (c) x − 1 (d) 1
  4. The HCF of two polynomials is (x + 1) and their product is (x + 1)²(x − 2)(x + 3). Their LCM is (a) (x + 1)(x − 2)(x + 3) (b) (x − 2)(x + 3) (c) (x + 1)²(x − 2) (d) (x + 1)³
  5. HCF of a²b³, a³b and ab² is (a) a³b³ (b) ab (c) a²b (d) ab²
Show answers

Answers: 1-b (HCF(12, 18) = 6, lowest powers), 2-b ((x + 1)(x − 1) and (x + 1)²), 3-c ((x − 1)(x² + x + 1) and (x − 1)(x + 1)), 4-a (product ÷ HCF), 5-b (lowest powers a¹, b¹)

Gap 6 · Ratio – the k-method and ordering several ratios (with Day 9)

Asked: 2018 Q176, 2018 Q177

Source: ratio basics and cross-multiplication for two ratios are in Topic 4 (Class 6 Ch 3, Class 7 Ch 4). The k-method below is the standard way to handle "a/x = b/y = c/z" (extra).

Method

  • k-method: when a/x = b/y = c/z, put each equal to k → a = xk, b = yk, c = zk. Substitute; k cancels in any expression of equal degree. Same for a : b : c = x : y : z.
  • Ordering many ratios: turn each into a decimal (2 decimals are enough), or compare two at a time by cross-multiplying (a/b > c/d if ad > bc).
  • Quick sense check: a ratio like n : (n + 1) or n : (2n − 1) gets closer to 1 (or to ½) as n grows; work it out rather than guess when the numbers are close.
  • Given (a + b)/(a − b) = m/n → cross-multiply: n(a + b) = m(a − b) → a : b = (m + n) : (m − n) (extra; check by substitution).

Formulas

GivenWrite

(a + b)/(a − b) = m/na : b = (m + n) : (m − n)

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2018 If a/3 = b/4 = c/7, then (a + b + c)/c = ? (a) 7 (b) 2 (c) 1/2 (d) 1/7
    a = 3k, b = 4k, c = 7k → 14k/7k = 2 → (b).
  2. PYQ 2018 Which is the biggest ratio: 2 : 3, 3 : 5, 4 : 7, 5 : 8?
    0.667, 0.600, 0.571, 0.625 → 2 : 3 is biggest (order 4 : 7 < 3 : 5 < 5 : 8 < 2 : 3).
  3. If a/2 = b/3 = c/5, find (a + b + c)/b.
    (2 + 3 + 5)k/3k = 10/3.
  4. Arrange 3 : 7, 5 : 9, 4 : 11 in ascending order.
    0.43, 0.56, 0.36 → 4 : 11 < 3 : 7 < 5 : 9.
  5. Which is greater, 7 : 9 or 11 : 14?
    7 × 14 = 98, 9 × 11 = 99 → 7/9 < 11/14 → 11 : 14.
  6. If (a + b)/(a − b) = 5/3, find a : b.
    3a + 3b = 5a − 5b → 2a = 8b → a : b = 4 : 1 (check: 5/3 ✓).

Quick test

  1. If x/4 = y/5 = z/6, then (x + y + z)/y = (a) 3 (b) 15 (c) 1/3 (d) 5
  2. The largest of 3 : 4, 5 : 7, 7 : 10, 9 : 13 is (a) 3 : 4 (b) 5 : 7 (c) 7 : 10 (d) 9 : 13
  3. The smallest of 2 : 5, 3 : 7, 4 : 9, 5 : 12 is (a) 2 : 5 (b) 3 : 7 (c) 4 : 9 (d) 5 : 12
  4. If a/3 = b/5, then (2a + b)/(a + b) = (a) 11/8 (b) 8/11 (c) 3/5 (d) 1
  5. If (a + b)/(a − b) = 3, then a : b = (a) 3 : 1 (b) 2 : 1 (c) 1 : 2 (d) 4 : 1
Show answers

Answers: 1-a (15k/5k), 2-a (0.75 vs 0.71, 0.70, 0.69), 3-a (0.40 vs 0.43, 0.44, 0.42), 4-a (11k/8k), 5-b (a + b = 3a − 3b → a = 2b)

Gap 7 · Field book – area of a field from surveyor's offsets (with Day 22)

Asked: 2025 Q189

Source: not in the TN school Maths books (searched Class 6–10) – standard surveying/mensuration method (extra). Topic 7 has one line on it and the 2025 question; this is the full method. The pieces use only ½ × base × height (right triangle) and ½ × h × (a + b) (trapezium).

Method

  • A field book lists a straight base line (e.g. A to C) with distances measured from A, and offsets – perpendicular distances from the base line to the corners, written left or right of the middle column.
  • Sort the readings on each side separately by distance. On each side the field is: a right triangle (A → first offset), trapeziums (between consecutive offsets), a right triangle (last offset → C).
  • Area of each piece = ½ × (gap along the base) × (sum of the two offsets); at the ends one offset is 0.
  • Shortcut: area of one side = ½ × Σ [offset × (distance from the previous reading to the next reading on that side)], using A (0) and C (end) as the outer readings.
  • Total area = left side + right side. One offset on each side of a diagonal d → quadrilateral = ½ d (h₁ + h₂).

Method

  • "Longest pole/rod that can be placed in a room" = the space diagonal of the cuboid, √(l² + b² + h²). "Longest rod on the floor" = floor diagonal √(l² + b²).

Gap 10 · Statistics – range, standard deviation, coefficient of variation (with Day 30)

Asked: 2019 Q189

Source: Class 10 Maths Ch 8 Statistics and Probability §8.2 Measures of dispersion (PDF p. 310–318) and §8.3 Coefficient of variation (PDF p. 320–322). Topic 10 covers only the average (mean).

Method

  • Range R = L − S (largest − smallest); coefficient of range = (L − S)/(L + S) (p. 310).
  • Variance σ² = mean of the squares of the deviations from the mean; standard deviation σ = positive square root of the variance; σ has the same unit as the data (p. 311).
  • Adding the same constant to every value does not change σ (p. 315); multiplying every value by k multiplies σ by k (p. 316).
  • Coefficient of variation C.V. = (σ / x̄) × 100%, suggested by Karl Pearson; the data with the smaller C.V. is more consistent (stable) (p. 320). For σ from C.V.: σ = C.V. × x̄ / 100.

Formulas

First n natural numbersmean (n + 1)/2; variance (n² − 1)/12 (Example 8.10, p. 316)

Solved examples

  1. PYQ 2019 100 candidates have mean height 163.8 cm and coefficient of variation 3.2. Their standard deviation is (a) 3.23 (b) 4.91 (c) 5.24 (d) 6.38
    σ = 3.2 × 163.8/100 = 5.2416 ≈ 5.24 → (c).
  2. The mean of a data is 25.6 and its C.V. is 18.75. Find σ. (Class 10 Example 8.15, p. 321)
    σ = 18.75 × 25.6/100 = 4.8.
  3. Range and coefficient of range of 63, 89, 98, 125, 79, 108, 117, 68.
    R = 125 − 63 = 62; coefficient = 62/188 ≈ 0.33.
  4. SD of 2, 4, 6, 8, 10.
    Mean 6; squared deviations 16, 4, 0, 4, 16 → variance 40/5 = 8 → σ = √8 ≈ 2.83. Add 5 to each → σ still 2.83; multiply each by 3 → σ = 3 × 2.83 ≈ 8.49.
  5. Variance of the first 10 natural numbers.
    (100 − 1)/12 = 8.25.
  6. Heights: mean 155 cm, σ 8.5 cm; weights: mean 46.50 kg, σ 5.3 kg. Which varies more? (Class 10 Example 8.16, p. 321)
    C.V. 5.48% vs 11.40% → weights vary more (heights more consistent).

Gap 11 · AP and GP properties – symmetric terms, vanishing combinations, AP ↔ GP link (with Day 36)

Asked: 2018 Q122, 2018 Q165, 2018 Q173

Source: Class 10 Maths Ch 2 Numbers and Sequences – A.P. notes (PDF p. 66), G.P. notes and condition b² = ac (PDF p. 77), Exercise 2.6/2.7 problems (PDF p. 68, 79). Topic 11 has tₙ and Sₙ; these are the property questions.

Method

  • Sum of consecutive AP terms given → take them symmetric: 3 terms a − d, a, a + d; 4 terms a − 3d, a − d, a + d, a + 3d (common difference 2d) (p. 66). The sum then gives a at once.
  • Product of consecutive GP terms given → 3 terms a/r, a, ar; 4 terms a/r³, a/r, ar, ar³ (p. 77). The product gives a at once (a³ or a⁴).
  • Tests: a, b, c in AP ⇔ 2b = a + c; in GP ⇔ b² = ac (non-zero) (p. 77).
  • Vanishing combinations in an AP (extra): the coefficients are binomial numbers with alternating signs: t₁ − 2t₂ + t₃ = 0; t₁ − 3t₂ + 3t₃ − t₄ = 0; t₁ − 4t₂ + 6t₃ − 4t₄ + t₅ = 0 (the d's cancel because differences of a constant are 0).
  • AP ↔ GP (extra): if a, b, c are in AP then ka, kb, kc are in GP (ratio kd). Adding/multiplying every term of an AP by a constant keeps an AP (p. 66); multiplying every term of a GP by a non-zero constant keeps a GP.

    Take a/r³, a/r, ar, ar³ → product a⁴ = 625 → a = 5 → (c) (the answer marked). Note: this "a" is the textbook's symmetric value, not strictly the first term (which is a/r³); the question was loosely worded – choose 5.

Gap 14 · Letter arrangements, vowel-count coding and "≠" logic (with Day 44)

Asked: 2024 Q192, 2024 Q200, 2025 Q198

Source: past papers and standard reasoning rules (extra); letter counts checked in python. Topic 14 has coding by position and the a ≠ b ≠ c rule in one line; this section adds the methods.

Method

  • Patterned letter strings (AZBYCX…): write the whole string once with positions; AZ, BY, CX … pair letter n with letter 27 − n. Count only what is asked ("immediately to the left of", "between", "followed by").
