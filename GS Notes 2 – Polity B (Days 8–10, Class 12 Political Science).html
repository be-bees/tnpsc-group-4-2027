Unit 1 · Constitution of India (PDF p. 9–24)

1.1 Meaning, functions and significance of the Constitution

  • Colonial basis: British rule ran on Charters, Councils Acts and Government of India Acts; free India runs on a definite written constitution. The central legislative body was converted into the Constituent Assembly (constitution-making body).
  • Most critical function: provide basic rules allowing optimal coordination among members of the state; allocate power among segments of the state; define who are citizens; set Centre–State relationship; protect fundamental rights; express the political identity of the people.
  • Part IV (DPSP) has provisions for the government to make laws for social problems.
  • "Constitution is the Fundamental Law of the Land" (activity topic).
  • UK has no single written document – customs, conventions and historical precedents form its constitution.
  • Theocratic State = opposite of secular state: has an official (state) religion, high posts reserved for its followers. Examples: Pakistan, Vatican.
  • Formulation = how a constitution comes into being, who crafted it, their authority. Indian Constitution's legitimacy: drafted by a Constituent Assembly of people's representatives.
  • Referendum = referring a question directly to the electorate instead of the legislature. Neither the Indian Constitution nor its amendments were put to a referendum (book calls this a setback); model to study: Switzerland.
  • Secularism / 42nd Amendment (1976): Preamble changed from "Sovereign Democratic Republic" to "Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic"; "unity of the nation" → "unity and integrity of the nation". Enacted by Indira Gandhi during the National Emergency.
  • Separation of powers: power divided horizontally among legislature, executive, judiciary. Constitution is neither too rigid nor too flexible (basic structure limits + amending provisions).

Preamble (printed on PDF p. 8)

  • "WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA … SOVEREIGN SOCIALIST SECULAR DEMOCRATIC REPUBLIC … JUSTICE (social, economic, political); LIBERTY (thought, expression, belief, faith, worship); EQUALITY (status, opportunity); FRATERNITY (dignity of individual, unity and integrity of the Nation) … IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this 26th day of November 1949 … ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION."
  • Order of words: Sovereign → Socialist → Secular → Democratic → Republic. "We" = the People of India.

Making of the Indian Constitution

  • First meeting: 9 Dec 1946 (Monday, 11 a.m., Constitution Hall, New Delhi). First debate: "Election of Temporary Chairman" – Acharya J.B. Kripalani requested Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha to take the chair as temporary Chairman.
  • Re-assembled after partition as the Constituent Assembly for the remaining India on 14 Aug 1947.
  • Final meeting: 24 Jan 1950, title "Signing of the Constitution", chaired by Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
  • Debates: 12 volumes, 9 Dec 1946 – 24 Jan 1950 (Vol I 9–23 Dec 1946 … Vol XI 14–26 Nov 1949, Vol XII 24 Jan 1950).
  • Election: members indirectly elected by members of the Provincial Legislative Assemblies, as per the Cabinet Mission plan (Committee of the British Cabinet).
  • Seat ratio: roughly 1 seat : 1 million population. Provinces elected 292; princely states minimum 93. Provincial seats divided among Hindus, Muslims, Sikhs by population; voting by proportional representation with single transferable vote. Princely states decided their own method.
  • 284 members present on 26 Nov 1949 signed the Constitution as finally passed.
  • Drafting Committee photo (Feb 1948): N. Madhava Rao, Saiyid Muhammad Saadulla, Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (Chairman), Sir Alladi Krishnaswamy Ayyar, Sir B.N. Rao; standing S.N. Mukherjee, Jugal Kishore Khanna, Kewal Krishnan.
  • (extra) Objectives Resolution moved by Nehru 13 Dec 1946; Rajendra Prasad elected permanent President 11 Dec 1946; Drafting Committee set up 29 Aug 1947; Constitution came into force 26 Jan 1950; total time ≈ 2 years 11 months 18 days.

1.3 Salient features

  • Longest written constitution in the world.
  • Blend of rigidity and flexibility (depends on amending procedure).
  • Sovereign = manages internal/external affairs freely; Socialist added by 42nd Amdt 1976 – India follows a mixed economy; Secular = all religions equal, no state religion; Democratic = rule through elected representatives (universal adult franchise); Republic = head of state is elected, not a monarch.
  • Parliamentary system: executive responsible to legislature; stays in power only with majority confidence.
  • Single citizenship: Citizenship Act 1955 regulates acquisition/determination of citizenship. Modes: birth, descent, registration, naturalisation, incorporation of territory; also renunciation and termination; registration of Overseas Citizens of India.
  • Citizenship (Amendment) Bill 2015: introduced in Lok Sabha 27 Feb 2015; naturalisation needs residence/Government service in India for 12 months immediately before application – Bill lets Centre relax the 12-month requirement in extraordinary circumstances.
  • Universal adult franchise: "one person one vote"; age 18 and above; no discrimination by caste, religion, gender, race, status.
  • Independent and integrated judiciary: Supreme Court at apex, High Courts and lower courts below.
  • Fundamental Rights – Part III, six heads: Equality; Freedom; against Exploitation; Freedom of Religion; Cultural & Educational; Constitutional Remedies. Right to Property (Art. 31) removed by 44th Amdt 1978 → now Art. 300A, a legal right. Art. 32 = right to move SC directly = Right to Constitutional Remedies. FRs are justiciable but not absolute (reasonable restrictions).
  • Right to Education: 86th Amdt 2002 inserted Art. 21-A – free and compulsory education for 6–14 years; consequential law RTE Act 2009.
  • Box: "All human beings are born free and equal" – Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948).
  • Fundamental Duties: added by 42nd Amdt (1976), Part IV-A, Art. 51A; source USSR. List:
    • (a) abide by Constitution, respect its ideals, institutions, National Flag, National Anthem
    • (b) cherish noble ideals of the freedom struggle
    • (c) uphold sovereignty, unity and integrity of India
    • (d) defend the country, render national service when called
    • (e) promote harmony and common brotherhood; renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women
    • (f) value and preserve composite culture
    • (g) protect natural environment – forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife; compassion for living creatures
    • (h) develop scientific temper, humanism, spirit of inquiry and reform
    • (i) safeguard public property, abjure violence
    • (j) strive towards excellence
    • (k) parent/guardian to provide education to child/ward aged 6–14 (extra: added by 86th Amdt 2002 → total 11 duties)
  • Federal or unitary: "indestructible Union with destructible states"; becomes unitary during emergency; quasi-federal.
  • Parliamentary supremacy balanced with judicial review: book cites SC's power to review its own judgments "subject to law of Parliament or rules made by SC under Art. 145" (extra: review power itself is Art. 137).
  • DPSP (Part IV) – unique; directives for social and economic justice → welfare state. Includes equal pay for men and women, free compulsory primary education, right to work, public assistance in old age/unemployment/sickness/disablement, organisation of village panchayats, adequate livelihood, help to economically backward, distribution of wealth. Not justiciable but fundamental in governance.

1.4 Parliamentary democracy in India

  • Art. 79: Parliament = President + Council of States (Rajya Sabha) + House of the People (Lok Sabha). Bicameral: Upper House represents units (states), Lower House the people.
  • Art. 1(1): "India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."
  • Rajya Sabha conduct booklet: Rules of Procedure and Conduct of Business; discussions – half-an-hour discussion, short duration discussion, motions on matters of public interest.
  • Parliamentary vs Presidential: India = parliamentary; USA = presidential.

  • Gandhian freedom fighter, born 1895, Manmalai Medu, Virudhunagar district (parents Karuppasamy, Valliammal); joined Congress 1917; Salt March 1930 under Rajaji's influence.
  • Context: Potti Sriramulu's fast (1952) for a separate Telugu state → agitation in Madras State to change its name.

  • Only person in Indian history to end life by fasting in the Gandhian way.

Select Committee and joint sitting

  • Select Committee: small group of members for particular aspects; origin Westminster system. Rule 125 of Rajya Sabha Rules – motion to refer a bill to a select committee. Quorum = one-third; chairman has casting vote on a tie; may appoint sub-committee; procedural doubts referred to RS Chairman (final).
  • Joint sitting (called by President): when a bill is rejected by the other House, Houses disagree, or more than six months pass. Passed by majority of total members of both Houses present and voting. No joint sitting for Money Bill or Constitution Amendment Bill.
  • Joint sittings held: 6 & 9 May 1961 – Dowry Prohibition Bill 1959; 26 Mar 2002 – Prevention of Terrorism Bill 2002 (extra: also 1978 Banking Service Commission (Repeal) Bill).
  • Nehru quote: "Neither House, by itself, constitutes Parliament … closest cooperation between the two Houses."

Glossary

  • Act – bill passed by both Houses and assented by President. Clauses – numbered paragraphs of a bill. Motion – formal proposal to the House. Oath/affirmation – allegiance to Constitution before taking seat. Resolution – formal expression of opinion of the House. Standing Committee – permanent committee, elected/nominated yearly. State (Art. 12 sense) – Govt and Parliament of India, Govts and Legislatures of States, local and other authorities.

NITI Aayog = extra-constitutional body (Finance Commission, UPSC, ECI are constitutional). 42nd Amdt = "mini constitution" (bulkiest, most comprehensive).

Unit 2 · Legislature (PDF p. 25–49)

Introduction

  • Legislature = highest law-making body. Centre: Parliament (National Legislature); States/UTs: Legislative Assemblies.
  • Bicameral Parliament: Lok Sabha (House of the People, Lower House) + Rajya Sabha (Council of States, Upper House) – inspired by British parliamentary system and the bicameral system of the USA. Many States are unicameral (no Legislative Council).
  • Book: Parliament shares law-making with 28 States and 8 Union Territories; UTs are directly governed by the Union Government.

2.1 Union Legislature: the Parliament

  • Session = scheduled meeting to discuss agenda and approve bills/motions. Three sessions a year: Budget (Feb–May), Monsoon (July–Aug), Winter (Nov–Dec).
  • President summons Parliament; must have not less than two sessions a year. President's special address at the start of the first session each year (future policies/programmes).
  • Functions: legislative (law making), financial (money bill = budget), electoral (elects President and Vice-President), judicial (removal of President, Vice-President, SC and HC judges = impeachment), overseeing administration, ventilating grievances. Cabinet individually and collectively accountable to and removable by Lok Sabha.
  • Women's Reservation Bill = Constitution (108th Amendment) Bill: 33% seats for women in Parliament and State legislatures; passed by Rajya Sabha in 2010, stuck in Lok Sabha (cartoon: The Hindu, 13.3.2010). (extra) Finally enacted as the 106th Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam).

Lok Sabha (House of the People)

  • 543 members elected directly from 543 constituencies by voters aged 18+; plus 2 nominated Anglo-Indians → total 545; majority = 272. Nominated members cannot decide the government when majority is proved. (extra) Anglo-Indian nomination ended by the 104th Amendment (2020).
  • Maximum (figure in book): not more than 552 – up to 530 from States + up to 20 from UTs (book figure shows "530 … plus not more than 2 nominated Anglo-Indians").
  • A Lok Sabha constituency comprises six Assembly constituencies (book). Term 5 years (while the ruling party enjoys confidence). Leader of ruling party = Prime Minister, who selects the Council of Ministers.
  • Quorum = one-tenth of total members (both Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha).
  • Minimum age for Lok Sabha member (book MCQ) = 25 years.

Roles and responsibilities of the Speaker

  • Speaker is elected by Lok Sabha from its members; conducts debates, questions; guardian of members' privileges; administrative head of the Parliamentary Secretariat.
  • Allots time, expunges objectionable remarks, moves motion of thanks to President's speech (book), can expel members who violate rules; permission needed to move amendments to a bill; refers privilege questions to the Committee of Privileges.
  • Final authority on point of order; certifies Money Bills – decision whether a bill is a Money Bill is final.
  • Presides over joint sitting of Parliament (Deputy Speaker in his absence).
  • Grants recognition to the Leader of Opposition.
  • 52nd Amendment (anti-defection): Speaker can disqualify a member on grounds of defection.
  • Neutral; votes only in case of a tie (casting vote).
  • Nehru quote: the Speaker "represents the House … becomes a symbol of nation's freedom and liberty."

Council of States (Rajya Sabha)

  • 250 members max: 238 from States and UTs (elected) + 12 nominated by President (literature, science, art, social service). Called the second chamber; protects interests of States (like the US Senate).
  • Constituted on 3 April 1952.
  • Elected by elected MLAs by proportional representation – single transferable vote.
  • Not subject to dissolution – Permanent House; one-third retire every second year; member's term 6 years (can be re-elected).
  • Vice-President = ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha.
  • Functions: Legislative, Financial, Deliberative, Federal. Gets all bills except Money/Financial bills.
  • Seats by population (unlike US Senate's equal representation): Uttar Pradesh 31 (highest), Sikkim 1 (least), Tamil Nadu 18. Seat allocation fixed by the Fourth Schedule.
  • Qualifications: citizen of India; at least 30 years; elector in a parliamentary constituency. RP (Amendment) Act 2003 amended Sec. 3 of RP Act 1951 – removed the requirement of being a resident of the State; now an elector anywhere in India.

Powers and privileges of MPs (box)

  • Freedom of speech in Parliament; immunity from court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in Parliament/committee.
  • Immunity for publications under authority of either House; courts cannot inquire into proceedings of Parliament.
  • Immunity for newspaper publication of a substantially true report of proceedings (unless malice proved).
  • Freedom from arrest in civil cases during session and 40 days before and 40 days after.
  • Exemption from legal process and arrest within the precincts of the House.

2.2 Powers of Lok Sabha

  • Makes laws on Union and Concurrent Lists; exclusive authority over Money Bills; Rajya Sabha can only delay a money bill 14 days; Lok Sabha may accept/reject RS suggestions.
  • Prepares/presents budget; controls executive through questions, supplementary questions, resolutions, motions, no-confidence motion; amends Constitution; approves emergency; elects President and Vice-President; creates committees/commissions.
  • If PM loses confidence of Lok Sabha → entire government quits.

2.3 Law making process

  • Art. 120: business of Parliament in Hindi and English; presiding officer may allow a member to speak in his mother tongue.
  • Law introduced as a bill; becomes an Act only after President's assent. Two types: Money Bill and Non-Money (ordinary/public) Bill.
  • Stages: First Reading → Second Reading → Committee Stage → Report Stage → Third Reading.
  • First reading: introduction (mostly by ministers); a private member must give one month's notice to the Speaker/Chairman; no discussion – formal. Then published in the Gazette of India (may be published before introduction – then no leave motion needed).
  • Second reading: usually two days after first reading; four courses – consider at once / send to select committee / send to joint select committee / circulate for public opinion (State Gazettes; opinions from local bodies/associations).
  • Committee stage: mover selects members; Speaker/Chairman appoints one member and the committee's chairman.
  • Report stage = "most important stage" (book) – clause-by-clause debate, final shape. Third reading = formal approval by majority.
  • Deadlock (other House rejects, unacceptable amendments, or bill not returned in six months) → President calls joint session; Speaker (or Deputy Speaker) presides; majority vote.
  • President may return an ordinary bill once; if sent back again (with or without amendments) he cannot withhold assent.

Private Member Bills

  • Bill introduced by any MP other than a minister (ruling or opposition). Needs a month's notice; its rejection does not affect the government. Can be a Constitution amendment bill but not a money bill.
  • Time: Lok Sabha – last 2½ hours of every Friday; Rajya Sabha – 2.30–5.00 p.m. on alternate Fridays (text elsewhere says alternate Fridays 2–6 pm).
  • Only 14 private member bills passed by Parliament; last in 1970. Introduced/discussed: 13th LS 343/17, 14th LS 328/14, 15th LS 372/14; 16th LS 206 introduced, 6 discussed (at time of report).
  • Examples: first – Muslim Wakfs Bill 1952 (Syed Muhammed Ahmed Kasmi, LS, assent 21.05.1954); Parliamentary Proceedings (Protection of Publication) Bill 1956 – Feroze Gandhi; Hindu Marriage (Amendment) Bill 1956 – Dr Seeta Parmanand (RS); last – Supreme Court (Enlargement of Criminal Appellate Jurisdiction) Bill 1968 – Anand Narain Mulla (assent 09.08.1970). 10 passed via LS, 4 via RS.
  • Rights of Transgender Persons Bill 2014: private member bill by Tiruchi Siva (TN MP); passed by Rajya Sabha 24 April 2015; introduced in Lok Sabha 26 Feb 2016; first private member bill passed by any House in 36 years and by RS in 45 years.

2.5 Amendment process and procedure (Art. 368)

  • Art. 368: Parliament may amend by addition, variation or repeal, without changing the basic structure.
  • Bill may be introduced in either House; passed in each House by majority of total membership + two-thirds of members present and voting (special majority); each House separately – no joint sitting.
  • Federal provisions also need ratification by half of the State legislatures by simple majority; no time limit for States.
  • President must give assent – can neither withhold nor return. Special majority needed only at the third reading voting.

2.6 State Legislature – structure

  • State = second stratum of federal structure; Part VI, Arts. 152–237 (book: except J&K which then had its own constitution; extra – Art. 370 provisions ended in 2019).
  • Unicameral = only Legislative Assembly; Bicameral = Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council.
  • Governor: constitutional head of State; executive power vested in him; actions in his name. Appointed by President; term 5 years / during President's pleasure / until successor enters office. Eligibility: citizen, no office of profit, 35 years. Can be reappointed; can hold charge of two States.

2.7 Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister

  • Chief Minister = head of cabinet and council of ministers; real executive. Art. 164(1): CM appointed by the Governor; other ministers by Governor on CM's advice.
  • CM's powers: recommends appointment/designation/removal of ministers, allocates portfolios; presides over cabinet; sole channel of communication between council and Governor; scrutinises bills/papers; major appointments in practice on his advice.
  • Art. 163(1): council of ministers headed by CM to aid and advise the Governor except in his discretionary functions.
  • Ministers hold office during Governor's pleasure (in practice CM's); individually responsible to CM, collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly ("speak in one voice").
  • State Cabinet = cabinet ministers (council also has ministers of state and deputy ministers); its decisions bind all ministers.

Officials and committees in State Legislative Assembly

  • Speaker (elected by MLAs): presiding officer; interprets Constitution/rules within Assembly; adjourns/suspends sittings, suspends members; casting vote on tie; decisions on conduct/procedure not subject to judicial intervention; appoints chairmen of all committees.
  • Deputy Speaker: elected from among MLAs; presides in Speaker's absence; a panel member presides if both absent.

Committees of Parliament

  • Two kinds: Standing (permanent, constituted every year/periodically) and Ad hoc (temporary – Inquiry and Advisory committees).
  • Standing committees – six categories:
    1. Financial: Public Accounts Committee, Estimates Committee, Committee on Public Undertakings
    2. Departmental Standing Committees (24)
    3. To inquire: Petitions, Privileges, Ethics
    4. To scrutinise and control: Government Assurances, Subordinate Legislation, Papers Laid on the Table, Welfare of SCs and STs, Empowerment of Women, Joint Committee on Offices of Profit
    5. Day-to-day business: Business Advisory, Private Members' Bills and Resolutions, Rules, Absence of Members
    6. House-keeping/Service: General Purposes, House, Library, Joint Committee on Salaries and Allowances
  • Estimates Committee: examines estimates, suggests economy/alternative policies, administrative reform; tours to study schemes.
  • Public Accounts Committee: scrutinises Appropriation Accounts and CAG report (Civil); revenue receipts and disbursement.
  • Committee on Public Undertakings: examines audit reports/accounts, autonomy and efficiency of PSUs; CAG reports on PSUs.

Glossary (Unit 2)

  • Question Hour – first hour of a sitting for questions. Quorum – minimum members for valid business = one-tenth. No-confidence motion – if PM/CM loses confidence, govt quits. Immunity of member – exemption from legal process/arrest within precincts. Joint session – presided by Speaker of Lok Sabha. Impeachment – removal of President, VP, SC/HC judges.

Book MCQ answers worth knowing

  • Parliament = President + Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha; Head of Lok Sabha = Speaker; Head of Government = PM; Head of State = President; RS protects interests of States; RS elected by MLAs; Permanent House = RS; Money bill power = LS; Indian legislature = bicameral; Governor = constitutional head, appointed by President; CM = head of the government (State); executive power of State vested in Governor.

Unit 3 · Executive (PDF p. 50–70)

3.1 Introduction – form of the State

  • Preamble: India = Sovereign Socialist Secular Democratic Republic – an elected President with fixed tenure is head of State (unlike England's hereditary monarch).
  • President = supreme head of legislature, executive, judiciary and armed forces (formally); but unlike the US President (de facto, real executive), Indian President has no direct executive responsibility – real power lies with the Council of Ministers led by the PM, responsible to Parliament. India = parliamentary executive; USA = presidential executive.
  • Union Executive = President, Vice-President, Council of Ministers/Cabinet headed by PM. Arts. 52–78, Part V deal with the Union Executive.
  • Executive (meaning) = body with authority to initiate major policies, make decisions and implement them. Two organs of Union Govt: Union Legislature (Parliament) and Union Executive. Constitution provides a collegiate executive (Council of Ministers under PM).

3.2 President

  • Head of the Indian State; First Citizen; symbol of unity, integrity, solidarity.
  • Art. 52 – there shall be a President. Art. 53 – executive power of the Union vested in President, exercised directly or through subordinates.
  • Art. 58 – qualifications: citizen of India; 35 years; qualified for election as a Lok Sabha member; no office of profit under Union/State/local authority.
  • Nomination: at least 50 proposers + 50 seconders from the electoral college (book attributes this to Art. 52; extra – actually laid down by the Presidential and Vice-Presidential Elections Act 1952). Security deposit ₹15,000 in the Reserve Bank of India; forfeited if candidate fails to get 1/6 of votes polled.
  • Electoral college (indirect election): elected members of both Houses of Parliament + elected members of State Legislative Assemblies + elected members of Assemblies of UTs of Delhi and Puducherry.
  • System: proportional representation by single transferable vote, secret ballot → winner needs absolute majority (quota).
  • Electoral quota = [total valid votes ÷ (number to be elected + 1)] + 1 (extra: standard form of the book's formula). Example activity: 10,00,000 valid votes, 1 seat → quota 5,00,001.
  • Counting: each voter gets one ballot, marks preferences 1, 2, 3…; first-preference count; if no one reaches quota, candidate with fewest first preferences eliminated and his votes transferred by next preference, until someone reaches quota.
  • Oath: faithfully execute the office; preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law; devote himself to the service and well-being of the people. Administered by the Chief Justice of India (in his absence the senior-most SC judge).
  • Entitlements: rent-free official residence – Rashtrapati Bhavan, Delhi; emoluments fixed by Parliament; personal immunity for official acts; immune from criminal proceedings during term.

Term, impeachment, succession

  • Art. 56: term 5 years from entering office; resigns by letter to the Vice-President; continues till successor takes charge; eligible for re-election.
  • Art. 61 – impeachment: charge in either House by resolution signed by at least one-fourth of total members, after 14 days' notice; passed by two-thirds of total membership; other House investigates and passes by two-thirds of its total membership → removed from date of passing.
  • Vacancy: expiry, resignation, impeachment, death, disqualification/election void. On resignation/removal/death → election within six months; Vice-President acts meanwhile. If VP also vacant → Chief Justice of India, then senior-most SC judge. Acting President gets all powers, immunities and emoluments of President.

Functions and powers of the President

  • Convenes, addresses, prorogues Parliament; nominates 12 to Rajya Sabha and (book) 2 Anglo-Indians to Lok Sabha; veto over non-money bills (can return once); convenes joint sessions; ordinances (valid max six months, per book); veto over certain State laws; installs ministers, distributes portfolios, can dismiss a ministry lacking majority; can commute capital punishment.
  • Fact box: emergency powers are in Part XVIII. Activity: types of veto – absolute, suspensive, pocket, qualified.

3.3 Vice-President

  • Modelled on the USA; Art. 63; second highest office. Arts. 63–70 deal with the Vice-President.
  • Election: by elected members of both Houses of Parliament (book), PR-STV, secret ballot. (extra: since the 11th Amendment 1961 nominated members also vote – the electoral college is all members of both Houses.)
  • Qualifications: citizen; 35 years; eligible for Rajya Sabha membership; no office of profit.
  • Term 5 years; resigns to the President; removed by Rajya Sabha resolution by absolute majority agreed to by Lok Sabha; 14 days' notice.
  • Art. 64: ex-officio Chairman of Rajya Sabha; draws salary as RS Chairman (VP office itself carries no salary). Acts as President up to six months; then gets President's emoluments and does not act as RS Chairman.

3.4 Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

  • Lord Morley: PM is "primus inter pares" (first among equals) and "keystone of the Cabinet arch".
  • India adopted the British parliamentary executive; PM = Head of Government.
  • Art. 75: PM appointed by President – leader of majority party/coalition in Lok Sabha (or person most likely to secure majority). No fixed tenure; no direct election.
  • Functions: prepares list of ministers; three ranks – Cabinet Ministers, Ministers of State, Deputy Ministers; may designate a Deputy PM (discretion); President allocates portfolios on PM's advice; PM can reshuffle, bifurcate/trifurcate/amalgamate departments.
  • Pre-eminence: leader of LS majority; selects/dismisses ministers; allocates business; Chairman of Cabinet; chairs Inter-State Council, Nuclear Command Authority etc.; coordinates policy; resignation/death of PM ends the Council of Ministers (a minister's resignation only creates a vacancy); sole channel of communication between President and ministers; chief spokesperson in foreign affairs.

Prime Minister's Office (PMO)

  • Secretarial assistance and advice to PM; extra-constitutional body with status of a department of GoI.
  • Came into existence in 1947; called Prime Minister's Secretariat (PMS) till 1977. Headed politically by PM, administratively by the Principal Secretary.
  • Functions: links PM with ministries and States; assists PM as chairman of NITI Aayog and National Development Council; public relations; Rules-of-Business references; relations with President, Governors, foreign representatives; PM's "think-tank"; subjects not allotted to any ministry. Not concerned with Cabinet work – that is the Cabinet Secretariat.

3.4.2 Central Council of Ministers

  • Art. 74: Council of Ministers with PM at head to aid and advise the President, who acts on its advice – there must always be a Council.
  • Cabinet Ministers make policy; Ministers of State and Deputy Ministers have administrative roles.
  • Art. 75: other ministers appointed by President on PM's advice; hold office during President's pleasure (in practice PM's choice).
  • Collective responsibility to the Lok Sabha (speak in one voice) + individual responsibility.
  • Shadow Cabinet – English practice: opposition team examines issues portfolio-wise. Kitchen Cabinet – informal inner circle ("cabinet within a cabinet"), real centre of power, keeps secrecy.

3.4.3 Union Cabinet

  • Cabinet = Cabinet-rank ministers; inner body/nucleus of the Council; extra-constitutional (book); highest decision-making body.
  • Functions: major legislative, financial, foreign policy; higher appointments; recommends ordinances; appoints enquiry commissions, resolves inter-departmental disputes; recommends emergencies, dissolution of LS, prorogation.
  • Cabinet Secretary: top civil servant; chairman of the Senior Selection Board (selects Joint Secretaries); presides over the annual conference of Chief Secretaries; chief adviser to PM on administration; link between PMO and agencies, and between civil service and political system.

3.5 Executive of the State

  • Arts. 153–167, Part VI – State Executive = Governor + Council of Ministers headed by CM.
  • Art. 153: a Governor for each State; 1956 amendment (extra: 7th Amendment) allows one person as Governor of two or more States.
  • Art. 155: appointed by President. Qualifications: citizen, 35 years, no office of profit, not an MP/MLA.
  • Art. 156: term 5 years, but holds office during President's pleasure; can be removed or transferred any time; salary fixed by Parliament.
  • Governor = constitutional and executive head; real power with CM-led Council, collectively responsible to Legislative Assembly.
  • Activity: Lieutenant Governor – administrator of certain UTs.

Introduction

  • Judiciary = one of three organs (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary); interprets law, protector of the Constitution; in a federal polity like India also the protector of the federation (settles Centre–State conflicts).
  • Quote – Harilal J. Kania, first Chief Justice of India: the Supreme Court "has goodwill and sympathy for all, but is allied to none".

4.1 Evolution – Judicial system in Ancient India

  • No separation of judiciary from executive. Vedic period: head of family (Kulapa/Kulapati) free from royal interference; tribal bodies Grama, Gopa, Vishaya, Jana, Gana autonomous. King = highest judicial authority ("fountain of justice" – book MCQ answer: Monarch).
  • Disputes mostly settled locally by caste/local bodies; treason in king's court; no regular jurisprudence. Accused proved innocence by evidence or ordeals.
  • Punishments harsh: whipping, amputation, impalement, banishment, confiscation, beheading, trampling by elephants; blood money (material compensation).
  • Smritis – Manu Smriti (core), Narada Smriti, Yajnavalkya Smriti – upheld varna–jati order; no "equality before law"; Brahmins exempted from torture/capital punishment; changing caste occupation (Varna Sankara) treated as crime; women treated as inferior; Sudras and Panchamas denied fair trial.
  • Arthasastra: disproportionate punishments; fines and confiscation a major source of royal income.
  • Trade disputes settled by guilds (Srenis). Mahasabhas of Pallava–Pandya–Chola empires insulated Brahmins; Mahasabha's variyams (Dharma variyam, Nyaya variyam) settled issues. Local bodies Ur, Urar, Nadu, Nattar, Nagarathar had own judicial arrangements.
  • Buddhist kingdoms (Asoka) disregarded smritis, more equal treatment; Asoka removed cruel punishments. Kovalan's execution in Silappadikaram shows defects of judicial procedure. "No rule of law but rule of the powerful."

4.2 Judicial system in Medieval India

  • Muslim rulers applied Islamic law where Muslims were involved; non-intervention in non-Muslims' socio-religious affairs; clear distinction between civil and criminal; extreme punishment for blasphemy.
  • Sultan = supreme judicial authority (book MCQ), in three capacities: Diwan-e-Qaza (arbitrator), Diwan-e-Mazalim (head of bureaucracy), Diwan-e-Riyasat (commander-in-chief). Courts at capital, provinces, districts, parganas, villages.
  • Six courts at the capital: King's Court; Diwan-al-Mazalim; Diwan-e-Risalat; Sadr-e-Jahan's Court; Chief Justice's Court; Diwan-e-Siyasat.
  • King's Court: presided by Sultan, original + appellate, highest court of appeal; Sultan assisted by Muftis. Diwan-al-Mazalim = highest appeal in criminal; Diwan-e-Risalat = highest appeal in civil matters.
  • In Sultan's absence courts presided by Qazi-ul-Quzat (highest judicial officer); later Sadr-e-Jahan became de facto head of judiciary; his court and the Chief Justice's court amalgamated by Alauddin Khilji.
  • Officers of Chief Justice's court: Mufti, Pandit, Mohtasib (prosecutions), Dadbak (administrative officer). Diwan-e-Siyasat = cases of high treason.

4.4 Supreme Court of India

  • Three-tier judiciary: Supreme Court → High Courts → District and Sessions Courts. India has a single integrated (unitary) judiciary, unlike the dual system of the USA. Ambedkar: "The Indian Federation, though a dual polity, has no dual judiciary."
  • Sources of law: (1) Constitution – fountain source; (2) statutes of Union/State/UT legislatures; (3) subordinate legislation – rules, regulations, bye-laws.
  • Original jurisdiction: disputes between GoI and States, or between States; FR cases (original + appellate). Writs: habeas corpus, mandamus, prohibition, certiorari, quo warranto.
  • Appellate: highest court of appeal (civil and criminal) against HC judgments.
  • Advisory – Art. 143: President may seek advice; not binding.
  • Guardian of Constitution; final interpreter; can declare laws/executive actions null and void.
  • Organisation: Part V, Chapter IV; Arts. 124–147. Originally CJI + 7 judges (8 total); raised to 30 (by 2008, per book) and 34 including CJI by 2019.
  • Collegium: CJI consults four senior-most SC judges (consensus). Judges appointed by President; retire at 65; resign by letter to President; removed by Parliament (impeachment).
  • Removal: order of President after an address by each House supported by majority of total membership and two-thirds of members present and voting, in the same session.
  • Qualifications: citizen; HC judge for 5 years, or HC advocate for 10 years, or a distinguished jurist in the President's opinion. Appointments generally by seniority.
  • SC began functioning 28 Jan 1950 in the Chamber of Princes (Parliament House); moved to present building in 1958. First CJI H.J. Kania; first judges with him: Saiyid Fazl Ali, M. Patanjali Sastri, Mehr Chand Mahajan, Bijan Kumar Mukherjea, S.R. Das.
  • 2007: K.G. Balakrishnan – first CJI from a Scheduled Caste.
  • Debate box ("Justice delayed is justice denied"): National Judicial Data Grid – 2.91 crore cases pending in district/subordinate courts, 21.90 lakh over 10 years; UP tops (8 lakh+ decade-old), then Bihar, Maharashtra; Sikkim (2) and Andaman & Nicobar (0) lowest.

High Courts

  • Head of State judiciary; four HCs serve more than one State (book); among UTs only Delhi has its own HC (book).
  • HC Chief Justice appointed by President in consultation with CJI and the Governor; for other judges, the HC Chief Justice is also consulted.
  • HC judges retire at 62; removed like SC judges. Qualification: citizen + 10 years judicial office or HC advocate (extra: 10 years).
  • Original and appellate jurisdiction; superintendence over all courts in its area; SC has no administrative control over HCs. Judges transferred by President after consulting CJI.
  • Art. 226 – HCs issue writs (and other directions/orders) – book MCQ answer.

4.5 Judicial Review, PIL and Judicial Activism

  • Judicial review (SC and HCs): constitutionality of laws and legality of executive action; extended to constitutional amendments via the basic structure doctrine (e.g. secularism, democracy, federalism cannot be destroyed).
  • PIL: any citizen may file – SC under Art. 32, HC under Art. 226, magistrate under Sec. 133 CrPC. Categories: bonded labour, neglected children, non-payment of minimum wages, jail petitions, police refusing to register cases / dowry, bride burning, rape etc., atrocities on SC/ST, environmental pollution. Not adversarial; filed against State/Central govt, municipal authority – not against a private party.
  • PIL cases: Common Cause Society v. Union of India (2005) – plea for a road safety act; Sangammal Pandey v. State of UP – Lucknow bench stayed construction near Kanshiram memorial; PUDR v. Union of India – PIL allowed by "public-spirited citizens" for the disadvantaged; Parmanand Katara v. Union of India – every doctor must give immediate medical aid to injured without waiting for procedural formalities.
  • Judicial activism: term coined by Arthur Schlesinger Jr. in 1947 (article "The Supreme Court: 1947"); Black's Law Dictionary – judges depart from precedent for progressive social policies. Indian judiciary more activist than US; Art. 21 most invoked.
  • A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras – SC rejected the "fair, reasonable and just procedure" argument; later Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India read substantive due process into Art. 21 (due process avoided by framers was brought in by judicial activism). Bhagwan Dass v. State of Delhi – death sentence upheld for honour killing.

4.6 Constitutional Law, Administrative Law, Indian Penal Code

  • Constitutional law = supreme law; defines roles/powers of legislature, executive, judiciary, citizens' rights, Centre–State relations; includes FRs, FDs, DPSP.
  • Rule of Law (English concept, brought by colonial regime): all equal before law; nobody above law; same law for all.
  • Box: In Australia the highest court is the High Court of Australia; State courts are called Supreme Courts.
  • Administrative law = branch of public law; relationship of individuals and government; controls administrative/quasi-judicial authorities. Reasons for growth: welfare state, inadequacy of legislature, judicial delay (→ administrative tribunals), not codified so flexible. Subordinate to constitutional law.
  • Indian Penal Code: criminal code; drafted 1860 on recommendation of the first Law Commission (1834) (picture: Thomas Babington Macaulay); came into force 1862; armed forces governed by separate laws. (extra) Replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 from 1 July 2024.
  • Lok Adalat ("people's court"): statutory status by the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987; disputes settled by mutual agreement – alternative dispute resolution.

Glossary (Unit 4)

  • Trial by jury – jury decides facts (vs bench trial). Judicial restraint – judges limit their own power. Original jurisdiction – hear a case first. Appellate – review lower court decisions. Advisory – advice of SC on law. Public law – relations between individuals and government. PIL – litigation for public interest, not necessarily by the aggrieved party.

5.1 Meaning of federalism

  • Federalism = constitutionally guaranteed distribution of powers between a national government and regional governments; Constitution is the supreme authority (Constitution = "parent", Centre and States = "siblings" in the book's figure). Democratic federalism ensures "Unity in diversity".
  • Evolution: USA = first federal State in the modern world; then Australia, Canada; trilingual Switzerland; the European Union = voluntary federal formation.
  • Rise in India: Regulating Act 1773 (Madras, Calcutta, Bombay under the Company); GoI Act 1919 – partial autonomy (Dyarchy) in provinces; GoI Act 1935 – provincial autonomy + dyarchy proposed at the Centre; Nehru Committee Report 1928 favoured federal structure (book MCQ: federalism first demanded by the Nehru Report). Seventh Schedule holds the three lists.

Federal features of the Indian Constitution

  • Written Constitution (book: 28 State governments + 1 national government).
  • Supremacy of the Constitution.
  • Distribution of powers – three lists (USA has a single mode of distribution).
  • Bicameralism – Rajya Sabha = guardian of States' rights.
  • Rigidity – Art. 368, Part XX special amendment procedure.
  • Supreme Court = umpire of federal system; exclusive original jurisdiction in Centre–State / inter-State disputes (e.g. TN vs Union).

Unitary / non-federal features

  • Single Constitution (US States have their own constitutions); single citizenship (USA: dual).
  • Flexibility – some provisions amended by simple majority.
  • No right to existence for States – Arts. 3 and 4: Parliament can create/abolish/rename States by an ordinary law (simple majority), bill on President's recommendation. Activity: Sikkim was once an "Associate State".
  • Union of States vs Territory of India: Union of States = 28 States + Centre; Territory of India = 28 States + 8 UTs + acquired territories (e.g. Pondicherry, Daman & Diu before becoming UTs).
  • Anti-federal Rajya Sabha: seats by population, not equality (UP 31, Nagaland 1; US Senate – 2 per State for all 50); TN has 18; Art. 249 lets RS move a State subject to Parliament in national interest.
  • Imbalanced distribution – lucrative revenue with Union; Union List longest; Union prevails in Concurrent List; residuary powers with Union (USA: with States).
  • Emergency powers – Arts. 352–360, Part XVIII: Art. 352 national, Art. 356 State (constitutional breakdown), Art. 360 financial.
  • Integrated judiciary; single Election Commission for Parliament and State legislatures (Chief Electoral Officer in each State works under ECI). State Election Commission (not part of federal set-up) conducts Panchayat and urban local body polls under the 73rd and 74th Amendments.
  • Unified auditing – CAG, Art. 148.

5.2 Centre–State relations

Three distributions: legislative, executive, financial.

5.2.1 Legislative relations

  • Territorial: Union laws cover all India and have extra-territorial reach (Indian citizens/property abroad); State laws only within the State.
  • Subject distribution (book figures): List I Union – 100 subjects (defence, foreign affairs, banking, currency); List II State – 59 items (public order, police, public health, local government, agriculture, forests, fisheries); List III Concurrent – 52 items (criminal law and procedure, civil procedure, marriage, education). Clash → Parliament's law prevails. Residuary → Union. Scheme follows GoI Act 1935. (extra: originally 97/66/47 entries; most guides now quote 100/61/52 – if options show 61 for State List, that is the usual answer.)
  • Exceptions (Parliament legislates on State List): (a) National Emergency; (b) agreement of two or more States requesting a common law; (c) to implement an international agreement; (d) during Art. 356 President's rule in a State.

5.2.2 Executive relations

  • Executive power co-terminous with legislative power; Concurrent List executive power ordinarily with States, but Union can issue directions to States (normal times and emergencies).
  • Mutual delegation: Union ↔ State with consent of the other; Union can also entrust functions to a State without its consent but with Parliament's consent.

5.3 Co-operative federalism

  • Granville Austin called Indian federalism "Co-operative Federalism". USA = "indestructible union of indestructible States"; India = quasi-federal – Union can pull down a State (emergency powers).
  • Inter-State Council – Art. 263 (set up by President): (a) inquire and advise on inter-State disputes; (b) investigate subjects common to Union and States; (c) recommend better coordination. Earlier subject councils: Central Council of Health, Transport Development Council, Central Council of Local Self-Government.
  • Holistic Inter-State Council set up in the early 1990s on Sarkaria Commission recommendation (extra: 1990). Chairman = PM; members: CMs of all States and UTs with legislatures, six Union Cabinet ministers, administrators of UTs without legislatures, Governors of States under President's rule. Standing Committee: Union Home Minister + 5 Cabinet ministers + 9 CMs.
  • Statutory bodies:
    • Zonal Councils – States Reorganisation Act 1956; Nehru: "develop the habit of co-operative working"; 5 originally (Northern, Southern, Eastern, Western, Central) + North Eastern (1971). Chairman = Union Home Minister (common for all); members – CM + 2 ministers of each State, UT administrators. Discuss economic/social planning, border disputes, inter-State transport.
    • River Boards Act 1956 – River Boards advise on regulation of inter-State rivers/valleys.
    • Inter-State Water Disputes Act 1956 (under Art. 262) – ad hoc tribunals if negotiations fail.
  • NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India – book: "Commission") replaced the 65-year-old Planning Commission in January 2015; PM = ex-officio chairman; Governing Council – CMs of all States, CMs of Delhi and Puducherry, Lt Governor of Andaman & Nicobar; aim: "foster cooperative federalism…"; "strong States make a strong nation".

5.4 Inter-State river water disputes

  • Major disputes: Cauvery (TN, Karnataka, Kerala, Puducherry); Vamsadhara (Andhra Pradesh, Odisha); Sutlej (Punjab, Haryana); Mahadayi (Goa, Maharashtra, Karnataka).
  • Art. 262: Parliament may by law provide adjudication of disputes over inter-State rivers and may exclude the Supreme Court and other courts.
  • Inter-State River Water Disputes Act 1956: negotiation first; else Union sets up an ad hoc tribunal; head nominated by the Chief Justice of India from sitting/retired SC or HC judges (earlier one-member, later more members). Award published in the Official Gazette → final and binding; no court has jurisdiction; no tribunal for disputes already under arbitration under the River Boards Act 1956. "Dual strategy" – negotiation, then tribunal.

Cauvery dispute (activity box)

5.5 Issues and demands in Indian federalism

  • Governor – seen as Centre's agent; Rajamannar Committee highly critical. Dravidian parties since C.N. Annadurai ("Arignar Anna – champion of State rights") demand genuine State autonomy; demand consultation with State on Governor's appointment; objections to partisan Governors, role in hung assemblies/splits.
  • Education – originally State List; moved to Concurrent List by the 42nd Amendment (1976); TN parties demand its return to the State List.
  • Reservation of State bills for President: for money bill President assents or withholds; other bills – may also direct reconsideration, but even if re-passed President need not assent. Criticised as politically motivated.
  • Misuse of Art. 356 (Part XVIII) – used more than 100 times; misuse reduced after S.R. Bommai v. Union of India.
  • All India Services – Art. 312: recruited by Union, posted to States; States post/transfer, only Centre can dismiss; Rajamannar suggested complete revamp.

S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994) – case study

  • S.R. Bommai (1924–2007): 11th CM of Karnataka; HRD Minister in United Front Govt 1996–98 (Deve Gowda, Gujral). His government was dismissed (book: 1994; extra – dismissal was in 1989, judgment 1994).
  • Judgment: Art. 356 is exceptional, used rarely; valid grounds – hung assembly, State disregarding constitutional directions, State aiding internal subversion; invalid – mere law and order problem, ruling party losing Parliamentary elections; Governor's report must be placed before Parliament; Assembly cannot be dissolved before both Houses approve the proclamation; courts can review and restore the dismissed government (judicial review over Art. 356).
  • Ambedkar hoped Art. 356 would remain a "dead letter".
  • Dicey: "A federal state is nothing but a political contrivance intended to reconcile national unity with maintenance of states' rights."

Unit 6 · Administrative Machinery in India (PDF p. 115–140)

6.1 Framework of Indian administration

  • Hierarchy chart: Government of India → State Governments → Divisions → Districts (Zilla Parishad) → Blocks (Tehsils) → Villages (Gram Panchayat); urban side: Municipal Corporations (Mahanagar Palika), Municipalities (Nagar Palika), Wards.
  • Present position: centralised administrative system; State personnel serve "twin masters" (State and Union executive). Liberalisation = reducing govt's regulatory control over private sector, with a trend to more centralisation in policy. This shift = New Public Management (NPM).
  • New tasks: disinvestment, de-subsidisation, liberalising/centralising tax system; corporatising mining, ports, petroleum, airways; revising labour laws; following World Bank, WTO, IMF guidelines.
  • Regulators: IRDA (insurance), TRAI (telecom), CCI (competition), PFRDA (pension), SEBI (securities); tax reforms – VAT regime, low uniform rates → precursor of GST.

6.2 Ministry, Department, Boards and Commissions

  • Central Secretariat = all ministries and departments; main policy-making institution.
  • Three-tier ministry: (1) political head – Cabinet minister (helped by MoS, Deputy Minister; an MoS may hold independent charge); (2) secretariat under a Secretary (career civil servant) with Joint/Deputy/Under Secretaries; (3) executive organisation under a head of department (Director, Director-General, Commissioner, Inspector-General, Chief Controller).
  • Units: Department (Secretary) → Wing (Additional/Joint Secretary) → Division (Deputy Secretary) → Branch (Under Secretary) → Section (Section Officer); section = lowest unit.
  • GoI (Allocation of) Rules of Business, 1961 classify ministries/departments. Art. 77: President makes rules for convenient transaction of Central Government business and allocation among ministries.
  • Central Secretariat functions: assist minister in policy and parliamentary work; frame legislation and rules; sectoral planning; budgeting and expenditure control; supervise execution; build personnel competence; coordinate and keep contact with States. It is policy-making, not executing.
  • Chart: School education is a Concurrent List subject – national: MHRD, CBSE, ICSE, NCERT; State: Directorates (School, Elementary, Matriculation, Govt Examinations, Non-formal & Adult Education), Teachers' Recruitment Board, SCERT, Samagra Shiksha, TN Textbook and Educational Services Corporation, Public Libraries; district: CEO, DEO, DIET, DPO; block: BEO, BRC, BIET; cluster: CRC; school: HM.

Cabinet Secretariat

  • Works directly under the PM; administrative head = Cabinet Secretary, also ex-officio Chairman of the Civil Services Board. Business under Allocation of Business Rules 1961: secretarial help to Cabinet and Cabinet Committees; Rules of Business; inter-ministerial coordination through Committees of Secretaries; crisis management.
  • Three wings: Civil (main – aid to Union Cabinet), Military (Defence Committee, Military Affairs Committee), Intelligence (Joint Intelligence Committee). Other organisations: RAW, DG Security, SPG, Joint Intelligence Group, DG Public Grievances (1988), National Authority – Chemical Weapons Convention.
  • Support to Cabinet committees: convenes meetings on PM's orders, agenda, papers, records of discussion (circulated after PM's approval), monitors implementation; custodian of Cabinet papers.
  • Cabinet Secretary: office created 1950; first – N.R. Pillai; senior-most civil servant.
  • Apex bodies listed: President, VP, Cabinet Secretariat, ECI, UPSC, NHRC, CAG, Indian Audit & Accounts Dept, NITI Aayog, NCW, NCST, 15th Finance Commission, NCM, IRDA, Office of Principal Scientific Adviser.
  • PMO: PM's immediate staff; called PM's Secretariat until 1977, renamed during Morarji Desai ministry; headed by Principal Secretary; has anti-corruption unit and public grievances wing.
  • Office of Principal Scientific Adviser: set up November 1999; serves PM-STIAC (Science, Technology and Innovation Advisory Council); placed under Cabinet Secretariat in August 2018.

6.3 Personnel administration

  • Aims: effective use of human resources, good working relations, maximum development, meeting social/legal responsibilities; curb favouritism and nepotism.
  • Functions: manpower planning, recruitment, training, promotion, salary structuring, employee welfare.
  • Civil service advantages (independent, permanent, impartial): credible recruitment guards against abuse; continuity, expertise, institutional memory; long-term view vs politicians' short-term gain; uniformity and unifying force; evolves an ethical basis.

6.3.2 All India Services, Central Services, State Services

  • All India Services – common to Centre and States (unique feature); recruited on all-India basis, uniform pay. Central Services – Union subjects (defence, income tax, customs, posts & telegraphs, railways). State Services – own.
  • All India Services Act, 1951 – Centre makes rules for recruitment and conditions. Only three AIS: IAS, IPS, Indian Forest Service. Members appointed by the President (book MCQ).
  • Recruitment modes: (i) direct – Civil Services Exam (IAS, IPS) and Indian Forest Service Exam, both by UPSC; (ii) promotion/selection – State Civil/Police/Forest Service officers; non-State Civil Service officers selected to IAS.
  • IAS: descendant of the Indian Civil Service (ICS); ultimate control with Union, divided into State cadres; salary and pension paid by States, but discipline/penalties by Centre (on UPSC advice). Deputation to Centre for tenures of 3/4/5 years = tenure system; generalist administrators.
  • IPS: pre-independence origin; mostly works in States; pay/status below IAS; 5-month foundation course, then Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel National Police Academy, Hyderabad; after training and a UPSC exam → Assistant Superintendent of Police; managed by Ministry of Home Affairs.
  • Indian Forest Service: only AIS created after independence – operational by Act of Parliament in 1963 (extra: constituted 1966); separate UPSC exam; specialised, not generalist; 3-month foundation course then 2-year training at Dehradun; managed by Department of Personnel and Administrative Reforms.
  • Indian Foreign Service (not an AIS): Central Civil Service – Class I, created after independence; exclusive Central control; top ranks of the Civil Services Exam; managed by Ministry of External Affairs; foreign allowance based on local cost of living, extra expenditure abroad, representational expenditure; 3-year training.

Civil Services Examination – pattern (book)

  • Preliminary: two objective papers of 200 marks each, 2 hours; CSAT qualifying at 33%; Hindi and English.
  • Main: qualifying – Paper A Indian language (Eighth Schedule) 300, Paper B English 300; merit – Essay 250, GS I–IV 250 each (GS-I heritage/history/geography/society; GS-II governance, Constitution, polity, social justice, IR; GS-III technology, economy, biodiversity, environment, security, disaster management; GS-IV ethics, integrity, aptitude), Optional 2 × 250 → 1750; Personality Test 275; Grand total 2025.

6.3.4 State Public Service Commission (e.g. TNPSC)

  • Same Arts. 315–323, Part XIV. Chairman and members appointed by the Governor; strength left to Governor; only rule – half the members must have held office for at least 10 years under GoI/State govt; Governor fixes service conditions.
  • Term: 6 years or age 62, whichever earlier (UPSC: 65); resign to the Governor. Governor may appoint an acting chairman (vacancy or absence).
  • Removal only by the President (not Governor): insolvency; paid employment outside duties; infirmity of mind/body; or misbehaviour – after Supreme Court enquiry whose advice is binding; Governor may suspend during enquiry. Misbehaviour = interest in a govt contract or sharing its profits (other than as a shareholder of an incorporated company).
  • Independence: security of tenure; conditions not varied to disadvantage; expenses charged on Consolidated Fund of the State (not voted); after term – chairman may become UPSC chairman/member or chairman of another SPSC; member may become UPSC chairman/member or chairman of that/another SPSC; no other govt job; no second term in same office.
  • Functions: exams for State services; consulted on methods of recruitment, principles of appointment/promotion/transfer, suitability of candidates, disciplinary matters (censure, withholding increments/promotion, recovery of loss, reduction in rank, compulsory retirement, removal, dismissal), legal expenses, injury pensions.
  • SC: consultation is directory, not mandatory – no court remedy if govt fails to consult. State legislature may extend jurisdiction (local bodies, corporations). Annual report to Governor, laid before legislature with memorandum on non-acceptance of advice.
  • Not consulted: reservations for backward classes; claims of SC/ST in appointments. Governor's regulations excluding matters must be laid before legislature for 14 days.
  • Role: "watchdog of merit system"; only a central recruiting agency (Department of Personnel / General Administration Department = central personnel agency); advice not binding. State Vigilance Commission (1964) also consulted in discipline – SPSC has the edge as a constitutional body. Consulted (with HC) on rules for judicial service other than district judges.
  • Book MCQ: Joint PSC for two or more States – created by Parliament after resolutions of the State legislatures concerned.

6.3.5 Staff Selection Commission (SSC)

  • Recruits for Group "B" posts (and Group "C" non-technical) in Central ministries and attached/subordinate offices; exams at different centres, posting near home State where possible.
  • Open exams for: LDCs, Grade C and D Stenographers, Assistants, Inspectors of Central Excise, Sub-Inspectors in CBI and Central Police Organisations, Divisional Accountants/Auditors, Junior Engineers (CPWD), Statistical Investigators, Tax Assistants, Section Officers (incl. Audit). Departmental promotion exams: LDC → UDC, Steno D → C.
  • (extra) Set up in 1975 as the Subordinate Services Commission, renamed SSC in 1977.

6.4 Election Commission

  • Permanent, independent constitutional body; Art. 324 – superintendence, direction and control of elections to Parliament, State legislatures, President and Vice-President. All-India body common to Centre and States.
  • Not concerned with panchayat and municipal polls – State Election Commission.

6.5 Comptroller and Auditor General (CAG)

  • Art. 148; head of the Indian Audit and Accounts Department; "guardian of the public purse"; controls finances at Centre and State levels.
  • Ambedkar: CAG is "the most important officer under the Constitution". Bulwarks of democracy: Supreme Court, Election Commission, UPSC, CAG.

6.6 Financial administration

  • Objectives: manage public finances; implement projects; public goods and social services; growth, employment, price stability; capital formation; productive use of funds; smooth parliamentary process; equity.
  • Principles: primacy of public interest; political direction and control; correspondence; unity of organisation; stability and balance; simplicity and flexibility; conduct, discipline, regularity; public trust and accountability.
  • Four phases of financial administrative history: I 1765–1858 creation of structure; II 1860–1919 systems and procedures; III 1919–1947 democratisation and decentralisation; IV 1950– development orientation.
  • Emerging trends: control of fiscal deficit; cut non-development spending; zero-base perspective; de-emphasised public sector; non-bureaucratic delivery; decentralised responsibility; deregulation and liberalisation.
  • Budget cycle: preparation and submission → approval → execution → audit.
  • Preparation starts with a Finance Ministry circular in September/October (~6 months before presentation); "the spender prepares the estimates". Proposals show: accounts classification, budget estimates (current year), revised estimates, actuals (previous year), proposed estimates (next year).
  • Financial year: first modern budget 1860 – year 1 May–30 April; from 1866 → 1 April–31 March (as in England).
  • Book MCQ: advance grant pending the budget = Vote on Account; bills presented with the budget = Finance Bill and Appropriation Bill; share of States in taxes decided by Finance Commission; Contingency Fund spending recouped through supplementary/additional/excess grants; PSC first set up in 1926.

6.6.2 Tax structure

  • Direct taxes (paid directly to govt): income tax, wealth tax, corporate tax, capital gains tax, securities transaction tax. Indirect (on manufacture/sale of goods and services): customs, excise, sales tax, service tax, entertainment tax, stamp duty, luxury tax, GST.
  • GST: CGST + SGST on intra-State supply; IGST on inter-State supply. Subsumed – Central: excise, additional excise, countervailing duty, additional customs duty, surcharges/cesses, service tax; State: VAT/sales tax, purchase tax, entertainment tax, luxury tax, lottery tax, State cesses.
  • Book slabs: 5% – skimmed milk powder, fish, fertilisers, tea; 12% – butter, cheese, pickles, fruit juices, tooth powder, medicine; 18% – ice cream, sauces, mineral water, suitcases, wrist watches; 28% – pan masala, washing machine, vacuum cleaner, automobiles, motorcycles. Outside GST (book): cooking gas, petrol, diesel, aviation fuel, natural gas, liquor.
  • (extra) GST came into force 1 July 2017 (101st Amendment, 2016); from 22 Sept 2025 slabs were rationalised mainly to 5% and 18% with a 40% rate for sin/luxury goods – the book's 12%/28% examples are now outdated.

Glossary (Unit 6)

  • Civil service – non-legislative, non-judicial, non-military public service, usually by competitive exam. Decentralisation – transfer of powers/resources from central to local governments. E-democracy – ICT to enhance democratic processes. E-governance – ICT in govt–citizen–business interaction and internal operations. Good governance (World Bank) – efficiency, accountability, transparency, legal framework. Integrity – honesty, antithesis of corruption. Transparency – public access to timely information. Welfare State – govt responsible for citizens' minimum living standard (free education, medical care, insurance, pensions).

7.1 Integration of Princely States

  • Princely (native) state = political unit ruled by a monarchic lineage or a subsidiary ally of a stronger power. In western/central India they arose with the Rajputs (migrated from Central Asia c. 200 CE; "Rajput" = sons of kings). Non-Rajput states: Nawabs, Nizams, native dynasties – Mysore, Travancore, Pudukkottai. "Princely" retained by the British to show subordination to the Crown.
  • Earlier integration attempts: Magadhan kings Bimbisara, Ajatasatru; Mauryas (Chandragupta, Ashoka); Samudragupta; disunity later helped Arab/Persian invasions and the Mughal empire.
  • 565 princely states in pre-independent India.
  • Gun salute system – showed a state's affiliation/prestige with the British: salute states ~117–120; 21-gun = highest (book MCQ: indicates the state actively collaborates with the East India Company); minimum 9-gun.
    • 21-gun: Scindia of Gwalior, Gaekwar of Baroda, Maharaja of Jammu & Kashmir, Maharaja of Mysore, Nizam of Hyderabad and Berar.
    • 9-gun: Nawab of Sachin, Maharaja of Patna, Maharana of Wadhwan, Nawab of Loharu.
  • Non-salute states: not acknowledged, lower prestige, or obsolete states whose rulers kept entitlements/pensions.
  • Clement Attlee (British PM), House of Commons, 15 March 1946: India "a congeries of races, religions and languages… difficulties can only be overcome by Indians"; minorities should "live free from fear".
  • Negotiations to merge began April 1947; problems – communal riots, partition, refugees. Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (Deputy PM and Home Minister) with his secretary V.P. Menon (ICS officer who had served the last three Viceroys) integrated the states; rulers promised their personal assets would not be taken. Holdouts: Junagadh, Kashmir, Hyderabad (and initially Jodhpur).

7.2 Linguistic reorganisation of States

  • Pre-independence: languages mobilised the freedom struggle; Annie Besant's Home Rule Movement had more southern participants. Congress began linguistic-province planning in 1917; Andhra Provincial Congress Committee (1917) grouped Telugu districts of Madras Presidency; redistribution process from 1927.
  • Odisha – struggle from 1895 to separate from Bihar → first linguistic province, 1936. Tilak, Annie Besant, Gandhi supported linguistic states.
  • Dhar Commission (Linguistic Provinces Commission, 1948, set up by Constituent Assembly, Justice S.K. Dhar) – did not favour linguistic reorganisation (unity, administration).
  • JVP Committee (1948) – Jawaharlal Nehru, Vallabhbhai Patel, Pattabhi Sitaramayya (Congress President) – initially opposed, later allowed linguistic States under pressure.
  • First linguistic State – Andhra: Potti Sriramulu died on the 58th day of his fast (19.10.1952 – 15.12.1952); violence followed. (extra) Andhra State formed 1 Oct 1953; book: Nehru declared Andhra Pradesh by merging Telugu areas of Hyderabad in 1956.
  • States Reorganisation Commission (Fazl Ali Commission): Chairman Saiyid Fazl Ali, members H.N. Kunzru and K.M. Panikkar; in place 22 Dec 1953 (book); report Sept 1955. Four criteria: (1) linguistic and cultural homogeneity (but "one language one State" rejected – e.g. Hindi across many States); (2) financial, economic, administrative considerations; (3) preservation of unity and security of the nation; (4) planning and welfare of people. Recommended 16 States and 3 UTs.

7.3 India after reorganisation

  • ~600 administrative units (princely states) under colonial rule; current units formed 1947–50; some kept British-era borders (Mysore, Hyderabad, Bhopal). Undercurrent of new-State demands: access to resources and identity.
  • Nehru and Patel feared further division after partition – wanted a pluralistic, unified State.
  • Challenges: balancing developed and underdeveloped States; smaller States fear larger ones; language-only criterion privileges some languages – e.g. Bodos (Assam), Coorgis (Karnataka). Book: 12 languages recognised in 1950, later 22 (extra: the Eighth Schedule originally listed 14 languages; now 22).
  • Border disputes: Karnataka–Maharashtra, Maharashtra–Gujarat, Haryana–Punjab, Odisha–West Bengal, Andhra–Madras. Dominant language imposed on minorities; "elite" and "mass" cultures.
  • Regional parties rose from regional identity movements – more challenge for the Centre.
  • Formation of new States (Art. 3 procedure as in book): bill referred by President to the State legislature for its views within a set period → President recommends bill in Parliament → passed → President's assent → new State.

7.4 Social, economic and political challenges of nation building

  • State = territory + people + government + sovereignty (may lack nationality feeling). Nation – from Latin nasci (to be born), nationem (breed/race); stresses consciousness of unity; a nation need not be a State. Nationality = sentiment of unity (race, language, geography, religion, political aspirations, history).
  • Ernest Renan: "a nation is a soul, a spiritual principle" – common legacy of remembrances (past) + actual consent to live together (present).
  • Box: Nehru's letter to Chief Ministers (1953) after the Everest ascent – condemned petty quarrel over whether Tenzing or Hillary reached first and Tenzing's nationality; great achievements are combined endeavours.
  • Know-how/economy: Nehru chose a planned, mixed economy; Five-Year Plans – irrigation, basic industries, electrification; malaria eliminated, food self-sufficiency. Late 1960s–early 70s recession; wars with China and Pakistan; bank nationalisation (Indira Gandhi); electronics revolution (Rajiv Gandhi); 1990s – LPG (Liberalisation, Privatisation, Globalisation).
  • Agriculture: declining since mid-1990s; farmer suicides (cotton farmers of Vidarbha, Maharashtra); sector holds 60%+ people but grew only 1.65% (1996/97–2004/05) – risk of a second agrarian crisis (first in early 1960s); US$15 billion loan waiver in 2008/09 budget; 80% marginal farmers lack formal loans.
  • Industry: Industrial Disputes Act protects <10% of workforce (90%+ unorganised); capital-intensive industry; land acquisition displaces tribals – should need local consent and compensation. Mandal Commission – reservation for OBCs in Union education and jobs.
  • Political: inter-State inequalities grew after 1991; regional parties demand autonomy, bigger role for Inter-State Council, more revenue sharing.
  • Economic: Deaton and Drèze – people below $1/day fell from 36% to 26% (1993/94–1999/2000); India ~270 million absolutely poor vs China ~110 million.
  • Social: IMR fell 30% in 1980s, only 12.5% in 1990s; India 80/1000 vs Bangladesh 91 (1990); 1999 India 71 vs Bangladesh 61.
  • Communalism ("narratives of hatred"); caste and gender discrimination – honour killings, violence against women, 33% women's reservation (then) not realised. Key need: inclusive growth.

7.5 Formation of Tamil Nadu State

  • Madras Presidency created 1802 by Lord Wellesley – included present TN, Andhra Pradesh, parts of Odisha, Kerala, Karnataka and Lakshadweep (map on PDF p. 157). After independence → Madras State; separate Tamil State on 1 Nov 1956; renamed Tamil Nadu later.
  • Linguistic nationalism: Congress gave Hindi national-language status → Tamil sub-nationalism (1910s); non-Brahmin movement – T. Madhavan (= Dr T.M. Nair, Taravath Madhavan Nair), Tyagaraja (= Sir P. Theagaraya Chetty) and others formed the South Indian Liberal Federation → "Justice Party"; won 1920 Madras elections; formed four of five ministries 1920–37; lost to Congress in 1937. Then Periyar E.V. Ramasamy and the Self-Respect Movement → Tamil nationalism consciousness.
  • Dravida Nadu idea: 1937 Congress won; Rajaji CM – abolished untouchability, made Hindi compulsory in schools → anti-Hindi agitation; Maraimalai Adigal, Thiru.Vi.Ka held meetings; Periyar's slogan "Tamil Nadu Tamilarukke"; Dravida Nadu Conference 1939.
  • 1944 Salem Conference: Periyar reorganised the Justice Party into Dravidar Kazhagam – aim: sovereign federal Dravidian Republic of four linguistic units; no support outside Tamil areas.
  • Dakshina Pradesh (Rajaji's counter-idea): merger of Tamil Nadu and Kerala (plus South); spread through C. Subramaniam, Bhaktavatsalam; opposed by N. Sreekantan (RSP), Achutha Menon (Cochin Communist Party), A.K. Gopalan (Travancore Communists), Periyar (editorial in Viduthalai, telegram campaign), Sardar Sarana Gowda (Karnataka); raised again at Amritsar Congress 1956; K. Kamaraj (CM) opposed it.
  • After independence two policy directions: official language policy and reorganisation of States.
  • SRC report 1955 recommended Madras, Mysore, Kerala States. Transfers to Tamil Nadu from Travancore: taluks Agasteeswaram, Thovalai, Kalkulam, Vilavancode (Kanyakumari) and Shenkottai; Devikulam and Peermedu (Idukki), though Tamil-majority, retained in Travancore-Cochin for geographical reasons.
  • Renaming struggle: Ma.Po. Sivagnanam – all-party conference 27 Jan 1956, general hartal 20 Feb 1956; Sankaralinganar fasted 27 July – 13 Oct 1956 (died). Congress lost popularity → 1967 DMK came to power and renamed Madras State "Tamil Nadu" (extra: effective 14 Jan 1969).
  • PDF p. 162 = Tamil Nadu political map (district headquarters) – no text.
  • Multiculturalism: Gandhi and Nehru opposed religious/cultural nationalism and the two-nation theory; critics: inadequate powers to States, Hindi replacing English erodes regional identity – Nehru's assurance: English continues as long as non-Hindi people desire.

8.2 Planning Commission of India

  • Set up 15 March 1950 by a resolution of GoI (extra-constitutional, advisory), chairman Jawaharlal Nehru – for "most effective and balanced utilisation of the country's resources". Implementation by Centre and States.
  • Functions: assess material, capital and human resources; prepare plans; set priorities and stages; identify hindrances; machinery for implementation; periodic appraisal; advise governments; fix growth rate and sector targets.
  • Composition: PM + 4 full-time members + part-time Cabinet-rank members. After Administrative Reforms Commission: PM (Chairman, presides), Deputy Chairman (de facto executive head, drafts plan for Cabinet), secretary, 4 full-time members, ministers as part-time; Finance Minister and Planning Minister ex-officio; member secretary (senior IAS). No State representatives – a central body. Worked on collective responsibility; organs – General, Subject, Administrative Divisions (+ Evaluation). Last Commission had 8 full-time members.
  • National Planning Council – constituted 1965 (book: during the Fourth Plan) – experts in science, engineering, economics.
  • National Development Council (NDC): PM (chairman) + all CMs + Union ministers + UT administrators + Planning Commission members; extra-constitutional, non-statutory, advisory to Planning Commission; first meeting 8–9 Nov 1952; 57th meeting 27 Dec 2012 (extra: set up 6 Aug 1952). Functions: guidelines for the National Plan, consider the Plan, review its working, mobilise resources, balanced development of all regions.
  • Poverty: growth ≠ poverty reduction; from the Fourth Plan, focus on "Garibi Hatao" (early 1970s); employment emphasised in all plans.
  • Art. 38(2): State shall minimise inequalities in income, status, facilities, opportunities among individuals and groups. "Socialistic pattern of society".
  • Box – Manual scavenging: SC held State and contractors constitutionally bound to ensure safety of persons doing hazardous jobs.

Rural–urban divide, democratic socialism, mixed economy

  • Nehru = architect of planning; inspired by the Soviet model and liberal capitalism → democratic socialism; mixed economy (private enterprise + public sector).
  • Industrial Policy Resolution 1956 (adopted April 1956) – basis of Five-Year Plans; three categories of industries (State-only; State-owned with private participation; private sector, regulated for social interest).

NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)

  • Replaced Planning Commission by Union Cabinet resolution of 1 January 2015 (PM Narendra Modi); aims at decentralised, bottom-up planning and cooperative federalism; government's think tank.
  • Structure: Chairman – PM; Vice-Chairman appointed by PM; 5 full-time and 2 part-time members (part-time from universities/research bodies); up to 4 Union ministers ex-officio; CEO appointed by PM; Governing Council – all CMs and Lt Governors of UTs; Regional Councils for specific regional issues (time-bound); special invitees – experts.

8.3 Land reforms in India

  • Colonial Permanent Settlement and land regulations left landlord–tenant friction. Peasant revolts on the eve of independence – Telangana, Travancore, Tanjore.
  • Bhoodan movement – Vinoba Bhave (voluntary gift of surplus land); Sarvodaya – cooperatives for poor farmers. In TN: Jagannathan and Krishnammal popularised Bhoodan/Sarvodaya. Land ceiling laws in 1950s–60s.
  • Four categories of reforms: (1) tenancy reform (registration, terms, transfer of ownership); (2) abolition of intermediaries (Zamindars) – most States before 1958; (3) ceilings on holdings and redistribution; (4) consolidation of holdings. Main impetus of the first three: poverty reduction.
  • Abolition of intermediaries: Zamindars, Jagirdars; easier in temporarily settled areas (UP, MP) with records.
  • Land ceiling: varied by State; uniform limits (book) – Assam 50 acres, J&K 22¾ acres, West Bengal 25 acres (Manipur also uniform); became a political issue, poorly implemented.
  • Tanjore Pannaiyal Protection Act 1952 (Pannaiyaal Paathukaappu Sattam) – against feudal serfdom in undivided Tanjore; later extended to whole State. TN Land Ceiling Act 1961 – surplus land to landless.
  • Tenant regulation: regulation of rents, security of tenure, ownership to tenants (Planning Commission guidelines).
  • Co-operative farming: by late 1960s 7,294 societies, 1.88 lakh members, 3.93 lakh ha; many defunct or formed to evade reforms.
  • Co-operative Act 1904 (book: "Indian Union Co-operative Act") → Madras State Co-operative Act; three forms – co-operative banks, land development banks, co-operative societies; TN has 10,000+ co-op societies (also weaving, silk, pottery).
  • Land Bill box: LARR Act 2013 (Right to Fair Compensation and Transparency in Land Acquisition, Rehabilitation and Resettlement); 2015 Amendment Bill – five special categories (defence, rural infrastructure, affordable housing, industrial corridors, infrastructure incl. PPP) exempted from consent of 80% owners (private) / 70% (PPP), from Social Impact Assessment and the bar on irrigated multi-cropped land; "private company" → "private entity".
  • Tamil Nadu's development experience (TN Human Development Report 2017, State Planning Commission): TN with Kerala tops South Asia in health and education; credited to social mobilisation against caste hierarchies (Kalaiyarasan 2014); TN poverty was above national average till the 1980s, fell fast from early 1990s; Drèze and Sen (2013) credit social investments like the free Mid-day Meal Scheme; welfare spending did not hurt growth.

8.4 Green Revolution

  • Agriculture ~12–15% of GDP (book); largest employer. Early plans prioritised industry; population grew ~2.5% a year; shortfalls by the 1960s (primitive technology, monsoon dependence, droughts, famine).
  • New Agriculture Policy (1960s) on Ford Foundation report "India's Food Crisis and Steps to Meet It": irrigation, fertilisers, high-yielding/hybrid seeds, pesticides = Green Revolution. Planned by Dr M.S. Swaminathan – "Father of India's Green Revolution" (also coined "Evergreen Revolution"; extra: Norman Borlaug = father of the global Green Revolution). Period 1967–78 → 50% rise in output.
  • IADP (Intensive Area Development Programme): pilot in 1960 in 7 districts of 7 States; then one district per State; 144 districts in 1965. Area: 2 million ha initially → 70 million ha (40% of cropped area). Single → multiple and mixed cropping.
  • Wheat success in Punjab, Haryana, UP; less success with rice in Karnataka, TN.
  • Impacts: output up 2–3 times vs 1950s; ended humiliation of PL-480 grain imports from the USA; more farm employment; link with industry (tractors, equipment) and markets; inter-State grain trade (to dry areas like Vidarbha, Telangana); disparity between small and big farmers; farmers' mass participation.
  • Shortfalls: regional/crop variations; rich–poor farmer gap; small farmers lacked finance; harmful fertilisers/pesticides; poor preparedness; population growth.
  • Achievements: ended grain scarcity; HYV experimentation; spurred changes in fishing, poultry and dairy – book names them Blue, Yellow and White revolutions (extra: standard usage – Blue = fish, Yellow = oilseeds, Silver = eggs/poultry, White = milk); cash/spice crop exports; sugarcane, oilseeds, cereals neglected till 1970s–80s.
  • Book MCQ: Green Revolution HYV seeds mainly for wheat; refers to High Yield Variety Programme.

White Revolution (Operation Flood)

  • 1950s: India imported butter, milk powder, infant food; dairy was secondary, primitive, low-yield native breeds, poor transport/preservation, few vets, high cattle mortality.
  • NDDB rural programme (national milk grid) – 1970 (book) (extra: NDDB set up 1965; Operation Flood launched 1970).
  • Model: AMUL (Anand Milk Union Limited), Anand, Gujarat – founded under Verghese Kurien; began in 1950s with <200 farms supplying Bombay. Book: Lal Bahadur Shastri inaugurated Operation Flood with NDDB under Kurien (book says 1966); Kurien = architect, Father of White Revolution.
  • Kurien quote: butter imports 500 t (1955) → co-ops produce 12,000 t; baby food imports 3,000 t → 38,000 t produced; all milk imports stopped by 1975.
  • Objectives: "flood of milk"; rural incomes; fair consumer prices; cut imports; National Milk Grid; nutrition.
  • Phase I (1970–79): 18 milk sheds linked to Mumbai, Delhi, Calcutta, Madras; cost ₹116 crore. Phase II (1981–85): 18 → 136 milk sheds, 290 urban markets; 43,000 village co-ops, 42.5 lakh producers; milk powder 22,000 t → 140,000 t (by 1989). Phase III (1985–96): vet care, nutrition, artificial insemination; 30,000 new co-ops added to 42,000; milk sheds peaked at 173 (1988–89); more women's co-ops.
  • Achievements: milk 20 → 100 million MT in 40 years – India largest milk producer; 500 million cattle (largest); co-ops in 125,000+ villages, 180 districts, 22 States.
  • Tamil Nadu: TN Dairy Development Corporation – 1972; TCMPF (Aavin) – 1981 on Kurien (Anand) pattern; per capita milk 169 g/day (1993–94) → 268 g/day (2017–18).
  • National Milk Day – 26 November (Kurien's birth anniversary); World Milk Day – 1 June.

8.5 Industrialisation

  • Under IPR 1956 and the plans India became (book) the sixth most industrialised country; engineering industry was key; basic and capital goods share of productive capital 50% (1959) → 79% (1990–91); growth in iron and steel, fertilisers, chemicals, cement, petrochemicals, power, railways, highways, telecom.
  • After reforms: faster growth of capital and consumer goods, slower basic industries; growth of banking, insurance, ports, aviation.
