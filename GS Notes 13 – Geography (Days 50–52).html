Unit 1 · India – Location, Relief and Drainage

Introduction – size and frontiers

  • Rank by area: India = 7th largest country; ≈ 2.4 % of world area; area 32,87,263 sq.km. Separated from rest of Asia by the Himalayas.
  • Land frontier: 15,200 km – Pakistan & Afghanistan (NW); China, Nepal, Bhutan (N); Bangladesh & Myanmar (E).
  • Longest border: Bangladesh (4,097 km); shortest: Afghanistan (106 km).
  • Coastline: mainland ≈ 6,100 km; including islands 7,516.6 km. Washed by Indian Ocean + its two arms – Arabian Sea (W), Bay of Bengal (E).
  • Palk Strait – narrow, shallow sea separating India and Sri Lanka (Palk Strait + Gulf of Mannar separate India from Sri Lanka).
  • India and the World: central location between East and West Asia; Trans-Indian Ocean routes (Europe ↔ East Asia) give strategic central location → west coast trades with West Asia, Africa, Europe; east coast with SE & East Asia.
  • Subcontinent: India + Myanmar, Bangladesh, Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka – distinct continental characteristics (physiography, climate, vegetation, minerals, human resources).
  • Peninsula = landmass bounded by sea on three sides (exercise).

1.2 Major Physiographic Divisions (5)

1. Northern Mountains 2. Northern (Great) Plains 3. Peninsular Plateau 4. Coastal Plains 5. Islands.

A. The Northern Mountains

  • Youngest and loftiest mountain chains in the world; formed a few million years ago by folding of earth's crust (tectonic activity).
  • Length 2,500 km – Indus Gorge (W) to Brahmaputra Gorge (E); width 500 km in Kashmir to 200 km in Arunachal Pradesh.
  • Pamir Knot = "Roof of the World" – links Himalayas with high ranges of Central Asia; Himalayas extend eastward from Pamir in an arc.
  • "Himalaya" – Sanskrit, "Abode of Snow".
  • Three divisions: (1) Trans-Himalayas (2) Himalayas (3) Eastern Himalayas / Purvanchal hills.

Unit 2 · Climate and Natural Vegetation of India

  • Weather = state of atmosphere of a place at a given time. Climate = accumulation of daily & seasonal weather over 30–35 years.
  • Equable climate = "British climate" – neither too hot nor too cold.
  • India's climate = "Tropical Monsoon type". Isohyets = lines joining places of equal rainfall (exercise).

2.1 Factors affecting climate (6)

FactorFacts

Jet streamsFast winds in narrow zone of upper atmosphere. Jet stream theory: SW monsoon onset driven by shift of sub-tropical westerly jet from Indian plains to Tibetan plateau; easterly jets cause tropical depressions in SW & retreating monsoon.

2.2 Monsoon

  • From Arabic "Mausim" = season; used by Arab navigators for seasonal wind reversal over Arabian Sea. Blows SW→NE in summer, NE→SW in winter.
  • Dynamic concept: seasonal migration of planetary winds & pressure belts with the sun. Summer solstice – sun vertical over Tropic of Cancer → belts & ITCZ shift north → SE trade winds cross equator, deflect → SW monsoon. Winter – belts shift south → NE monsoon (trade winds).
Season (IMD)MonthsKey facts
WinterJan–FebSun vertical over Tropic of Capricorn → slanting rays, low temp; clear skies, light northerly winds, low humidity, large daily range; high pressure over N India, NW winds down Indus–Ganga valleys; in S India wind E→W. Rain over W. Himalayas, T.N., Kerala. Western disturbances (brought by jet stream) → rain in Punjab, Haryana, H.P., snow in J&K hills – good for winter wheat.

Mango showers – quick ripening of mangoes, coast of Kerala & Karnataka. Norwesters / Kalbaisakhi – thunderstorms in Bihar, West Bengal, Assam (Apr–May).

Bay of Bengal branch – trapped by Garo, Khasi, Jaintia hills → heaviest rain at Mawsynram (Meghalaya); rain decreases E→W. ≈ 75 % of India's rain from SW monsoon.

2.3 Distribution of Rainfall

  • Average annual rainfall of India = 118 cm; highly uneven.
  • > 200 cm: west coast, Assam, S. Meghalaya, Tripura, Nagaland, Arunachal.
  • < 100 cm: all Rajasthan, Punjab, Haryana, W & SW U.P., W. M.P., Deccan plateau east of W. Ghats (except narrow T.N. coast strip). Rest 100–200 cm.
  • Mawsynram (Meghalaya) – highest rainfall in the world, 1,141 cm.

Unit 3 · India – Agriculture

3.1 Soils

3.2 Irrigation

  • Irrigation = watering crops by artificial means. Area under irrigation: Tube wells 46 %, Canals 24 %, Other wells 16 %, Other sources 11 %, Tanks 3 %.
  • Canal irrigation – 2nd most important; suits low relief, deep fertile soil, perennial water. Inundation canals – taken directly from rivers without barrage, work in rainy season (flood diversion); perennial canals – from perennial rivers via barrage. ≈ 60 % of canal-irrigated area is in the northern plains.
  • Well irrigation – most important; cheap, dependable, popular. Open wells – Ganga plains, deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna, Cauvery, parts of Narmada & Tapti valleys. Tube wells – low water table, power, soft strata: Gujarat, Maharashtra, Punjab, M.P., Tamil Nadu.
  • Tank irrigation – very old; popular in peninsula because: undulating relief & hard rocks; natural depressions; no perennial rivers; impermeable rocks; scattered population and fields.
  • Modern methods: Drip – drops through nozzles, saves up to 70 % water; Sprinkler – simplest, pipes with holes, works on uneven land; Rain gun – crops up to 4 feet (sugarcane, maize); Central-pivot = water wheel / circle irrigation.
  • PMKSY (Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana) – more production using water-saving technologies.
  • Multipurpose river valley projects – dams for irrigation, hydro-power, drinking/industrial water, flood control, fisheries, navigation (mostly irrigation + power).

5.6 Trade

  • Barter – goods for goods. Balance of trade = exports − imports; favourable if exports > imports.
  • Internal (domestic/local) trade – land transport, local currency. International (foreign/external) – exports & imports, foreign currency, waterways & airways. Bilateral (2 countries) vs multilateral (> 2).

6.12 Natural disasters in Tamil Nadu

  • Disaster = sudden natural catastrophe causing great loss of life/property. Disaster Risk Reduction (UNDRR) = systematically analysing and reducing causal factors – less exposure, less vulnerability, land/environment management, preparedness, early warning.
  • Landslide – collapse of earth/rock; water = commonest trigger; The Nilgiris – one of India's most vulnerable districts; also Coimbatore, Palani hills (Kodaikanal).
  • Flood – coastal districts in NE monsoon; recent major flood 2015.
  • Cyclone – Bay of Bengal cyclones in NE monsoon; T.N. divided into 5 cyclone-prone zones (very high → very low).
  • Drought – T.N. is water-deficit; depends on NE monsoon; remedies: rain-water harvesting, conservation (pollution control, renovating traditional sources, modern irrigation, forest cover, crop pattern change…).
  • Forest fire – summer, deciduous & thorn forests; keep 30 feet defensible space.
  • Earthquake – high-risk zones in north & central India; T.N. in moderately low risk zone. During: take cover under strong table.
  • Tsunami – 26 Dec 2004; move to high ground / upper storey; never go to shore to watch.
  • Organisations: State Disaster Management Authority (Chairman – Chief Minister), Relief/DM Dept, police, forest, fire & civil defence, health, transport, PWD, veterinary, food & civil supplies. District: District Disaster Management Authority (Chairman – District Collector), revenue, local police, civil defence, fire & emergency services, home guards, NGOs. Website: tnsdma.tn.gov.in.

Unit 1 · Lithosphere I – Endogenetic Processes

  • Four spheres: lithosphere (solid outer part), atmosphere (thin layer of gases), hydrosphere (watery part – oceans, rivers, lakes, water vapour), biosphere (layer where life exists). Abiotic = litho, atmo, hydro; biotic = biosphere.
  • Earth's surface area = 510 million sq.km.
  • Quote on the spheres ("ball of rock … envelope of air … biosphere") – Arthur Holmes.

1.2 Structure of the Earth (crust, mantle, core)

LayerKey facts
CrustOuter "skin", solid & rigid, 5–30 km. Continental crust thicker than oceanic. Continental crust = SIAL (Silica + Aluminium); oceanic crust = SIMA (Silica + Magnesium). SIAL floats over SIMA.
MantleAbout 2,900 km thick. Upper part solid, lower part molten. Molten rock inside Earth = magma.

1.4 Geomorphic processes

  • Endogenetic (internal) processes: forces acting from interior towards surface, e.g. volcanic eruption; they build landscape, create topographic relief.
  • Exogenetic (external) processes: natural agents on surface (running water, glacier, wind, waves); they tear landscape down into low plains.
  • Internal processes: generate heat and eject materials; internal radioactivity is the principal source of power.

Plate tectonics

Unit 2 · Lithosphere II – Exogenetic Processes

  • External processes result from solar energy and gravitational forces; internal processes from Earth's internal heat.
  • Exogenetic process = Weathering & mass movement (physical, chemical, biological) + Gradation (degradation, aggradation).

2.1 Weathering

  • Weathering: breaking, disintegration and decomposition of crustal materials by exposure to atmosphere. Types: physical, chemical, biological.
  • Physical weathering: breakdown without change in chemical composition (freezing–thawing, expansion–contraction).
    • Exfoliation – peeling of rounded rock like onion layers (alternate heating/cooling); sheeting & shattering are other forms.
    • Granular disintegration – crystalline rocks; grains loosen & fall (temperature).
    • Block disintegration – stress on joints from day–night expansion/contraction.
  • Soil = disintegrated rock + decayed organic matter (humus).
  • Chemical weathering: by chemical reactions; predominant in hot humid (equatorial, tropical, subtropical) zones. Agents: oxygen, CO₂, hydrogen, water.
    • Oxidation – oxygen + iron in rocks → iron oxide (like rusting).
    • Carbonation – water + CO₂ → carbonic acid; dissolves carbonate rocks; forms caves in limestone.
    • Solution – dissolution of rock substances in water.
    • Hydration – absorption of water into minerals; minerals swell → cracks.
  • Biological weathering: plant roots, earthworms, burrowing animals (rabbits, rats), human activities.

2.2 Gradation

  • Gradation = levelling of land by natural agents (rivers, groundwater, wind, glaciers, sea waves). Degradation = eroding land; Aggradation = building up landforms.

I. Rivers (running water – most extensive agent)

  • Origin = catchment area; meets sea = mouth; whole path = course.
  • Tributary – small stream joining main river, e.g. Bhavani. Distributary – channel separating from main river, e.g. Kollidam.
CourseDominant workLandforms
UpperErosion (steep slope, high velocity)I-shaped & V-shaped valleys, gorges, canyons, rapids, pot holes, spurs, waterfalls

  • Gorge – valley with almost vertical sides in hard rock; India: upper reaches of Brahmaputra, Indus, Cauvery. Canyon – deep gorge running hundreds of km, e.g. Grand Canyon, river Colorado, USA.
  • Waterfall – hard rock over soft rock horizontally; plunge pool = depression at base. Highest: Angel Falls (979 m), Venezuela.
  • Rapid (river jumps) – shallow fast-flowing water. V-shaped valley – vertical erosion. Pot holes – cylindrical holes drilled in river bed.
  • Meander – sweeping loops of slow, debris-laden river. Ox-bow lake – abandoned meander. World's largest: Lake Chicot, Arkansas, USA; Asia's largest freshwater ox-bow lake: Kanwar Lake, Bihar.
  • Alluvial fan – fan-shaped deposit at foothills (book text calls it "alluvial plain").
  • Flood plain – fine fertile sediments on banks; continued deposition raises banks → levees.
  • Delta – triangular low-lying deposit at mouth; e.g. Cauvery Delta (TN). Named after Greek letter Δ resembling Nile delta. Sunderban (Ganga–Brahmaputra) = largest delta in world.
  • Estuary – where river meets sea; waves remove silt so no deposition; e.g. Narmada, Tapti.
  • Fact: best-known geyser – Old Faithful, Yellowstone National Park, Wyoming, USA.

World's largest karst: Nullarbor, Great Australian coast. Also S France, Spain, Mexico, Jamaica, W Cuba, Central New Guinea, Sri Lanka, Myanmar.

III. Glaciers

  • Glacier – large mass of ice moving slowly; "river of ice". Accumulation place = snowfield; snowline = height above which permanent snow. Higher latitude → lower snowline (Alps 2,700 m; Greenland 600 m).
  • Firn / névé – gradual transformation of snow into granular ice.
  • Erosional (Switzerland, Norway): cirque (bowl-shaped armchair depression); arête (knife-like ridge where two cirques meet back to back); pyramidal peak/horn (3+ cirques, e.g. Matterhorn); U-shaped valley; hanging valley (tributary glacier valley above main valley); fjord (glacial valley partly submerged in sea).
  • Depositional: moraines (ground, terminal, lateral); drumlins (inverted teaspoons / half-cut eggs – "basket of egg topography"); eskers (long narrow ridges of boulders, gravel, sand by meltwater streams); kames; outwash plain (sand, gravel, silt at glacier terminus).

IV. Wind (Aeolian process – arid regions)

  • Wind = air blowing horizontally at/near surface.
  • Erosional: mushroom (pedestal) rock – soft bottom worn away; near Jodhpur, Rajasthan. Inselberg (German "island mountain") – isolated resistant residual hill, e.g. Uluru / Ayers Rock, Australia. Yardang – vertical hard/soft layers, irregular crests.
  • Depositional: sand dunes; barchans (isolated crescent dunes; gentle windward, steep leeward slope); transverse dunes (asymmetrical; alternate slow/fast winds from same direction); longitudinal (seif) dunes (long ridges parallel to winds; "seifs" in Sahara); loess (fine silt & porous sand – N & W China, Pampas of Argentina, Ukraine, Mississippi Valley). Thickest loess: 335 m, Loess Plateau, China.

V. Waves (sea)

  • Wave = up (crest) and down (trough) movement of surface water; most powerful agent, confined to narrow coastal belt.
  • Erosional: sea cliff (steep vertical rock face); sea cave; sea arch (two caves unite, e.g. Neil Island, Andaman & Nicobar); sea stack (pillar after arch collapses, e.g. Old Man of Hoy, Scotland); wave-cut platform (flat surface at foot of cliffs; wave-cut bench/terrace).
  • Depositional: beach (most constructive work; Juhu – Mumbai, Puri – Odisha, Marina – Chennai); bar (elongated deposit parallel to shore); spit (ridge attached to land at one end; e.g. Kakinada spit).
  • Exercise pairings: distributaries – lower course; mushroom rock – aeolian; eskers – glacial; stalactites – karst; cliff – sea waves.

3.7 Humidity

  • Humidity = water vapour in atmosphere. Absolute humidity = grams of water vapour per cubic metre of air. Relative humidity – in %; at 100% air is saturated.
  • Dew point – temperature at which air becomes saturated. Measured by wet and dry bulb thermometer (hygrometer).
  • Recap: atmosphere heated through conduction; Sun is chief energy source. Exercise facts: dew as ice crystals = frost; condensation = gas → liquid; leeward side – rain shadow; willy-willy – Australia.

Unit 4 · Hydrosphere

4.1 Hydrosphere

  • Blue planet – Earth, as it holds water in abundance. Hydrosphere = water in all forms on Earth.
  • >97% of Earth's surface water in oceans; <3% on land (glaciers, ice caps, groundwater, rivers, lakes, water vapour). 71% of Earth covered by water.
  • Five major oceans: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic.

4.2 Hydrological cycle

  • Hydrological (water) cycle = continuous movement of water on, above and below the surface. Three major processes: evaporation, condensation, precipitation. Water resources: fresh water and salt water.

4.3 Fresh water

  • Rain water – purest form (very little salt) = fresh water. Major part of fresh water is frozen (ice caps, glaciers); ~1% liquid (rivers, streams, lakes, ponds).
  • Groundwater – surface water penetrating porous rocks. Water table – level below ground where water collects. Aquifers – porous rock strata filled with water.
  • Finland – "Land of Thousand Lakes" – 1,87,888 lakes.

4.4 Oceans

  • Northern Hemisphere = Land Hemisphere; Southern Hemisphere = Water Hemisphere (81% water). Book says NH "holds 61% of land"; actually NH is about 61% water / 39% land, but holds most of the world's land (extra).
  • Oceans = "resource bowl of the earth".
  • Sylvia Earle – American oceanographer; first "Hero for the Planet" (Time magazine) for marine-life protection.
  • Jacques-Yves Cousteau (1910–1997) – French ocean explorer; French Navy information service; missions to Shanghai & Japan (1935–38), USSR (1939). Honours: Cross of War 1939–45 (1945), US Presidential Medal of Freedom (1985).

4.4.2 Movement of ocean water

  • Driven by temperature, salinity, density, sun, moon, winds. Waves and currents – horizontal; tides – vertical.
  • Waves – strongest movement; ripples caused by wind; height depends on wind speed, duration, direction. Tremors on ocean floor → tsunami. Wave energy plants: Vizhinjam (Kerala) and Andaman & Nicobar.
  • Tides – periodic rise and fall due to gravitational pull of sun and moon.
    • Spring tide – Sun, Moon, Earth in a line; high tide; on full moon and new moon days.
    • Neap tide – Sun and Moon at right angles; low tide; twice a month at first and last quarter moon.
  • Tidal energy zones in India: Gulf of Khambhat, Gulf of Kutch, Sundarbans.
  • NIO (National Institute of Oceanography) – est. 1 January 1966; HQ Dona Paula, Goa.

  • Ocean currents – movement of ocean water in a definite direction; clockwise in NH, anticlockwise in SH. Causes: Earth's rotation, prevailing winds, differences in temperature and salinity.

4.5 Marine resources & conservation

  • Marine resources – biotic and abiotic resources in ocean water and bottom. Biological: fish, plankton, sea grass, coral. Mineral: petroleum, natural gas, metallic minerals, sand, gravel. Energy: fuels, tidal, wave energy.
  • 4.5.1 Conservation: oceans are "life blood" of planet; oil, fishing, non-conventional energy, ports & harbours, coastal tourism.
  • Gangetic Dolphin – declared National Aquatic Animal in 2010; endangered.
  • Great Barrier Reef – world's largest coral reef system; 2,900 reefs, 900 islands, ~2,000 km long, ~3,50,000 sq.km; Coral Sea, off Queensland, Australia; visible from space; built by coral polyps; CNN: one of 7 natural wonders.
  • Exercise: heavy rains – decrease salinity; ocean temperature decreases with depth.

Unit 5 · Biosphere

  • Biosphere – fourth sphere; life-supporting layer encompassing litho-, hydro- and atmosphere. Vertical range ~20 km (ocean floor to troposphere); most life within ~1 km above and below MSL.
  • Species – grouping of living things; habitat – area where an organism lives; biodiversity – wide variety of plants and animals in a habitat.

5.1 Ecosystem

  • Ecosystem – community of organisms interacting with each other and with non-living environment. Size: smallest unit (e.g. bark of a tree) to global ecosystem (ecosphere). Examples: cropland, pond, forest, desert. (Exercise: ecosystem = smallest unit of biosphere.)
  • Ecology – science of ecosystems; ecologist – one who studies it.

5.1.2 Functions of ecosystem

  • Trophic levels – hierarchical levels of energy flow. Food chain – transfer of energy through trophic levels. Food web – interlocking, interdependent food chains. (Arctic tundra food web: lichen, grasses, wildflower → lemming, arctic hare, caribou, musk ox → wolf, polar bear, hawk.)

5.2 Biodiversity

  • Biodiversity – variety of living organisms in a habitat; influenced by topography, climate, human activities; maintains ecological balance; benefits tourism, education, research.
  • 5.2.1 Loss of biodiversity – extinction of species by human and natural influences.
  • Hotspot – region that has lost >70% of original habitat; 34 hotspots in world; India: Himalayas, Western Ghats, Indo-Burma, Sundaland.

5.3 Biomes

  • Biome – geographically extensive ecosystem; defined by relief, climate, soils, vegetation. Two categories: terrestrial (mainly temperature & rainfall: forest, grassland, desert) and aquatic (freshwater, marine).

5.3.2 Aquatic biomes

  • Freshwater – lakes, ponds, rivers, streams, wetlands; water lily, lotus, duckweed; trout, salmon, turtles, crocodiles.

Unit 6 · Man and Environment

  • Environment – from French environ = encircled/surrounded; set of relationships between man and nature; biotic + abiotic.
  • Stockholm Conference, 1972 – declared man both creator and moulder of his environment. Earth Summit = UNCED, Rio de Janeiro, June 1992 – members to reduce CO₂, methane and other greenhouse gas emissions.
  • Classification: natural (land, water, air, creatures); human (individual, family, community, religion, education, economic, political); man-made (buildings, roads, bridges, parks, industries, monuments).

6.1 Man and environment

  • Man's dominance: erect posture, hands, intelligence; from Palaeolithic to Neolithic invented wheel, fire, tools, agriculture, housing → modified environment, exploited resources.

6.2 Population

  • Latin populus = people. Population – total people in a place at a given time. Demography – statistical study of population (Greek demos = people, graphis = measurement).
  • 6.2.1 Growth: rapid rise around the Industrial Revolution. Natural increase = birth rate − death rate. Population change – by births, deaths and migration (increase: births, immigration; decrease: deaths, emigration).
  • World population: 500 million (1650) → 1000 million (1850); projected ~8 billion (2025), ~9 billion (2050).
  • Census – official periodic enumeration (age, sex, literacy, occupation); UN recommends every 5–10 years. First known: Babylonians, 3800 BCE. First modern: Denmark. India: first 1872; regularly every 10 years since 1881.
  • Black Death – plague; killed 30–60% of Europe's population in the 14th century (England affected).
  • 6.2.2 Distribution factors: physical (temperature, rainfall, soil, relief, water, vegetation, minerals, energy); historical (river valley civilisations, wars, invasions); economic (education, jobs, industry, trade, amenities).
  • World Population Day – 11 July; started by UNDP in 1989.
  • 6.2.3 Density = total population ÷ total land area (persons per sq.km). High (>50): East Asia, South Asia, NW Europe, Eastern N America. Moderate (10–50): Angola, Congo, Nigeria, Zambia. Low (<10): Central Africa, Western Australia, Northern Russia, Canada.
  • TN districts (2011 census, book activity table – area, population): Chennai 178.2 sq.km, 46,46,732; Thiruvallur 3,424 sq.km, 37,28,104; Salem 5,205 sq.km, 34,82,056; Vellore 6,077 sq.km, 39,36,331; Kanchipuram 7,857 sq.km, 39,98,252. (For density use the Class 10 figure: Chennai 26,553 per sq.km – densest district.)
  • Overpopulation – more people than resources; underpopulation – too few people to develop potential.
  • India's population policy – 1952; India was the first country to announce one (aim: slow growth via birth control).

6.5 Environmental issues

  • Issues: deforestation, pollution (air, water, noise), urbanisation, fracking, waste disposal. (Exercise: loudspeaker – noise pollution; ozone in stratosphere – protective shield against UV.)
  • Deforestation – permanent cutting of trees for other land uses. Effects: floods, droughts, loss of soil fertility, air pollution, extinction, global warming, desertification, water depletion, melting ice, sea-level rise, ozone depletion.
  • Conservation of forests: regulate tree cutting; control forest fires (monitoring, controlling movement of people); proper use of forest products.

6.6 Sustainable development

  • Brundtland Commission (1987): "development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs".
  • Three core elements: economic growth, social aspects, environmental protection.
  • Social sustainability – social system functioning at a defined level of well-being; symptoms of failure: war, endemic poverty, injustice, low education.
  • Economic sustainability – growth in balance with ecosystem; implemented through a strong Public Distribution System.
  • Environmental sustainability – environment supporting a defined quality and resource extraction forever.
  • Conservation and awareness bring sustainability. Student tips: lifestyle (cloth bag), fixing, recycle, needs vs wants – "act locally, think globally".

Unit 8 · Disaster Management: Responding to Disasters

  • Case study – Tsunami (26 Dec 2004): Moken tribe (Andaman) knew of Laboon – "wave that eats people"; elders: "If the water recedes after an earthquake, run to high ground". Earthquake magnitude 9.1 off Sumatra; Andaman & Nicobar deaths 1,879, 5,600 missing; islanders who knew the stories survived; most victims (southern Nicobar) were outsiders.
  • Disaster – "a catastrophe that causes great damage or loss of life and property".

8.1 Disaster response

  • Disaster response – restoring facilities, rehabilitation, restoring livelihoods, reconstruction. Response phase – emergency relief: saving lives, first aid, restoring communications/transport, food, water, shelter, mental & spiritual support.
  • First responders: police officers, firefighters, emergency medical technicians (also mental health professionals, hospitals) – not insurance agents.
  • Disaster management: Prevention, Mitigation, Preparedness, Response, Recovery; all levels of government + NGOs + community organisations; includes pre-disaster planning. Crisis management is only a part.
  • Disaster management cycle: Pre-disaster risk reduction phase (mitigation/prevention & DRR, preparedness) → disaster → post-disaster recovery phase (response, recovery).

8.1.1 Earthquake

  • Sudden vibration from plate movement along plate boundaries; focus, epicentre (most damage); measured by seismograph, recorded in Richter scale.
  • Japan – densest seismic network; Indonesia – most earthquakes (larger area); most per unit area – probably Tonga, Fiji or Indonesia (subduction zones).
  • Some quakes are foreshocks; minimise movement, stay indoors till shaking stops.
  • Indoors: DROP, COVER, HOLD ON under sturdy table; else cover head, crouch in inside corner; under lintel of inner door/bed; away from glass, outside doors/walls, falling objects; stay inside till shaking stops.
  • Outdoors: away from buildings, trees, streetlights, wires; stay in open. Greatest danger directly outside buildings at exits/exterior walls; most casualties from collapsing walls, flying glass, falling objects.
  • In vehicle: stop safely away from buildings, trees, overpasses, wires; then avoid damaged roads, bridges, ramps.
  • Mock drill: "earthquake position – drop, cover, and hold on".

8.1.2 Tsunami

  • Series of enormous waves from earthquakes, underwater landslides, volcanic eruptions or asteroids; speed 700–800 km/h, waves 10–30 m (note: Unit 1 gives >500 km/h, >15 m). Floods; disrupts transport, power, communication, water.
  • Respond: know if home/school is in hazard area; plan evacuation routes; weather radio/local radio/TV; discuss with family; review flood safety.
  • After: keep listening (Coast Guard emergency frequency); first aid to self first; call professionals for rescue; help infants, elderly, disabled; stay out of buildings with water around (foundations undermined); check gas leaks – open window, get out.

8.1.3 Riot

  • Causes: racial, religious, economic, political, social. Pew Research Centre (198 countries, 11 April 2015): Syria tops, then Nigeria, Iraq, India.
  • At travel destination: follow news; don't be inquisitive at demonstrations – take another route; avoid police/security action areas.
  • If caught: keep to edge of crowd, break away to building/doorway/alley; walk, don't run; don't resist arrest, contact law advisor; avoid glass shop fronts, move with the flow; if shooting – drop to ground, cover head & neck, lie flat.

8.1.4 Fire

  • Wildfires – in hot, dry periods; forests, grasslands, bush, deserts; spread by wind. Causes: lightning, sparks, volcanic eruption, arson/accidents. Smoke – respiratory hazard ("smoke kills more than fire").
  • India: ~25,000 deaths per year from fire; females ~66%; ~42 females and 21 males die daily.
  • Do's & Don'ts: know evacuation plan; evacuate on fire/CO alarm; feel door with back of hand – if hot, don't open; stay low in smoke; know outside rally point; know extinguishers, alarm pull stations, exits.
  • During fire: stay calm; pull alarm or call 112; give name & location, don't hang up till dispatcher says; leave immediately, inform others; walk, don't run; never use elevators.
  • Mock drill: Stop, Drop and Roll (clothes on fire). HOTS: cut tree branches below 3 m near house.
  • Helplines (book): 011-23438252, 011-23438253, 011-1070; website ndmindia.nic.in; references: TNSCERT Disaster Management Module, NDMA.
